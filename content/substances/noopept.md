## Summary

Noopept (omberacetam; GVS-111) is N-phenylacetyl-L-prolylglycine ethyl ester, a Russian nootropic drug with limited regional human evidence and a much larger preclinical literature. Human efficacy, target affinity, pharmacokinetics, interactions, and long-term safety remain substantially less characterized than promotional summaries imply.

Deep Research synthesis: Noopept — returned Deep Research evidence record.

## Description

PubChem identifies Noopept as CID 180496 (C17H22N2O4; 318.4 g/mol); the retained SMILES records connectivity, while the named drug contains the L-proline stereocenter. It was selected from N-acylprolyl dipeptides in 1996; synthesis proceeds conceptually through N-phenylacetyl-L-proline followed by peptide coupling to glycine ethyl ester. Rat studies detect cyclo-prolylglycine (PubChem CID 126154), but human metabolite evidence is weak. Bioanalysis has used HPLC, GC/GC-MS, and, in market surveillance, chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry; modern solid-state characterization includes NMR, DSC/TGA, PXRD/SCXRD and SEM. The current Russian label supplies 10-mg oral tablets and names omberacetam as the active ingredient.

Deep Research synthesis: The full returned Deep Research report is preserved in the pending acquisition folder for noopept.

## Evidence note

Human evidence is concentrated in small Russian studies, notably a 53-patient active-comparator trial and an open post-stroke study; placebo-controlled replication and robust healthy-volunteer cognition data were not found. A current ClinicalTrials.gov search found no Noopept/omberacetam/GVS-111 record. Human numeric PK is sparse, and receptor-affinity evidence is mostly preclinical. Manufacturer sponsorship of the key human publications could not be established from inspected declarations, so sponsorship remains not-assessed rather than presumed independent. Preclinical metabolite, neurotrophin, HIF-1 and receptor findings must not be treated as demonstrated human mechanisms.

### Evidence summary

Identity correction: the marketed Russian active ingredient should not be described as a racemate. Noopept/omberacetam/GVS-111 is conventionally identified as N-phenylacetyl-L-prolylglycine ethyl ester; the L stereochemistry is part of the drug identity. The current Russian product is omberacetam 10 mg oral tablets, registration ЛП-N(004920)-(РГ-RU) dated March 20, 2024. The current holder information lists omberacetam 10 mg/tablet and indications for adult cognitive/emotional-lability symptoms associated with traumatic brain injury/post-concussion states, cerebrovascular insufficiency/encephalopathies, asthenic disorders, and related reductions in intellectual productivity. [clinicaltrials-search-2026]

The human efficacy record is small and methodologically weak by modern standards. Short-term cognitive or symptom improvement has been reported in vascular/post-stroke cognitive impairment and in vascular/post-traumatic organic brain disorders, but much of the literature is Russian, small, open-label or active-comparator rather than blinded placebo-controlled, and incompletely reports allocation concealment, masking, attrition, prespecified outcomes, funding, and conflicts. The 2008 Russian and 2009 English Neznamov–Teleshova publications represent the same or substantially overlapping study rather than independent replication. [clinicaltrials-search-2026][clinicaltrials-search-2026]

A more recent 2022 randomized three-active-arm study in 150 patients with non-demented vascular cognitive impairment used omberacetam 10 mg twice daily for 45 days. MoCA increased during treatment, but at 4.5 months after the course the reported change from baseline was only +0.8 points and not statistically significant, p=0.061. The study was explicitly sponsored by OTISIFARM, the Noopept marketing authorization holder, and its disclosure states that the conflict of interest did not affect the results rather than declaring an absence of conflict. [dr-noopept-s4]

Evidence for healthy-person cognitive enhancement/biohacking is insufficient. The directly relevant PubMed-indexed healthy-volunteer study exposed young adults to cold, heat, and hypobaric hypoxia and assessed “meteoadaptation”/functional state; it was not a contemporary placebo-controlled trial of memory, attention, executive function, or academic/work performance under ordinary conditions. A study of older teachers was confounded by concomitant head-and-neck self-massage. [dr-noopept-s5][molekul-link-175]

A PubMed search restricted to systematic reviews/meta-analyses of Noopept/omberacetam/GVS-111 returned zero compound-specific records. ClinicalTrials.gov searches on October 10, 2026 likewise returned zero records under “Noopept,” “omberacetam,” and a query covering “GVS-111” or the full chemical name. This establishes absence from ClinicalTrials.gov under those searched aliases, not that no study has ever been registered in any national or historical registry.

BHWiki evidence judgment: short-term symptomatic/cognitive benefit in selected vascular/post-traumatic populations is low-certainty; durable benefit is very-low-certainty/uncertain; ordinary healthy-person cognitive enhancement is unsupported by adequate clinical trials. [clinicaltrials-search-2026][dr-noopept-s7][molekul-link-174]

[dr-noopept-s9] |

| Intranasal sprays, research powders, custom capsules, bulk material | Not interchangeable evidence-wise with the registered 10 mg tablet. No included human efficacy or human PK study validated these formulations. Market-surveillance evidence documents Noopept in unauthorized/bulk-market channels. [dr-noopept-s10] |

The direct human PK evidence is unusually thin. Boĭko and colleagues reported slower elimination in humans than in rats/rabbits, substantial human interindividual variability, and no detectable drug metabolites in human blood plasma, which they attributed to the low clinical dose and low concentrations. The PubMed abstract does not provide a sufficient modern concentration-time dataset to establish full human Cmax, AUC, metabolic fractions, renal clearance, food effect, or dose proportionality. [dr-noopept-s9]

Current product information describes rapid absorption and gives values including approximately 15-minute time to peak concentration, half-life around 0.38 h, high relative bioavailability, and conversion to phenylacetic acid, phenylacetylproline, and cycloprolylglycine. Those label statements should not automatically be converted into fully validated human PK parameters, because the publicly retrieved label does not transparently expose the underlying species/study data for every parameter and the published human PK paper did not detect metabolites in plasma. [dr-noopept-s11][dr-noopept-s9]

Mechanistic claims require a strict evidence boundary. NGF/BDNF induction, HIF-1 activation, cholinergic/glutamatergic receptor effects, antioxidant pathways, and CPG-mediated receptor hypotheses are preclinical/mechanistic propositions, not demonstrated mechanisms of clinical benefit in humans. The human efficacy trials retrieved did not measure NGF or BDNF induction, HIF-1 target engagement, receptor occupancy, or a CPG exposure-response relationship. Current product information itself mixes clinical-use language with mechanisms derived substantially from experimental models, so these mechanisms should be stored in a separate BHWiki preclinical field rather than used to grade human efficacy. [dr-noopept-s11]

### Safety, dependence, interactions, and special populations

The current Russian product information lists allergic reactions and possible increases in blood pressure in patients with hypertension, particularly severe hypertension. The comparative vascular/post-traumatic development program also reported sleep disturbance, irritability, and increased blood pressure during treatment. These trials are far too small to estimate uncommon or delayed adverse events reliably. [dr-noopept-s11][clinicaltrials-search-2026]

For psychiatric and neurologic safety, the direct human record is inadequate to quantify risks of mania/hypomania, psychosis, suicidality, seizure provocation, movement disorders, or persistent sleep disturbance. Irritability and sleep disturbance provide a limited human signal, but neither small positive trials nor absence of published case series can establish the absence of rarer hazards. [clinicaltrials-search-2026]

For dependence and withdrawal, current product information states that a withdrawal syndrome is not observed after stopping treatment. That is a regulatory/product-label statement, not the equivalent of a dedicated human abuse-liability, tolerance, physical-dependence, or controlled withdrawal study; none was identified in the allowed evidence set. The presence of Noopept in illicit “smart-drug” commerce and in bulk-material interceptions establishes a nonmedical-use market, not pharmacologic dependence. [dr-noopept-s11][dr-noopept-s10]

For renal and hepatic impairment, current Russian instructions contraindicate use in severe liver and/or kidney dysfunction. No dedicated human renal-impairment or hepatic-impairment PK study, validated dose-adjustment algorithm, dialysis study, or quantitative organ-impairment exposure analysis was identified. [dr-noopept-s11]

For pregnancy and lactation, pregnancy and breastfeeding are contraindications in the current Russian instruction. No prospective human pregnancy cohort, controlled reproductive study, placental-transfer study, breast-milk PK study, or infant-exposure dataset was identified in the permitted evidence base. [dr-noopept-s11]

For drug interactions, Russian product information has stated that interactions were not established with ethanol, hypnotics, antihypertensive agents, or psychostimulants. “Not established” must not be rendered as “proven safe to combine”: no modern controlled human drug-drug-interaction program was identified. Likewise, absence of a characterized overdose syndrome does not establish a toxic threshold or a wide overdose safety margin. [dr-noopept-s11]

The labeled adult regimen begins at 20 mg/day, given as 10 mg in the morning and 10 mg during the day; with inadequate efficacy and good tolerability it may be increased on medical advice to 30 mg/day in three 10 mg doses. Current instructions advise against administration after 18:00. These doses apply to the registered 10 mg oral tablet, not automatically to bulk powder, nasal products, salts, or online capsules of uncertain composition. [dr-noopept-s11]

### Regulation, registries, and product quality

Russia. The current GRLS/PharmPortal record identifies the marketed medicine as Noopept/omberacetam, registration ЛП-N(004920)-(РГ-RU), with current product information specifying a 10 mg tablet; OTISIFARM is the marketing authorization holder. [clinicaltrials-search-2026]

United States. In a U.S. analytical study of cognitive-enhancement supplements, Cohen and colleagues treated omberacetam as an unapproved drug and found it in all ten purchased products selected for analysis. Recommended servings delivered measured omberacetam quantities as high as about 40.6 mg; 75% of quantified declared drug amounts across the products were inaccurate, and some products contained undeclared pharmaceuticals or omitted declared ones. Thus, “Noopept” on an online supplement label does not establish identity, dose accuracy, single-ingredient composition, or equivalence to the Russian pharmaceutical product. [dr-noopept-s16][dr-noopept-s17]

Europe/Australia surveillance. A 2025 coordinated study involving official medicines-control laboratories documented 159 samples and 166 molecular identifications involving 34 compounds collected from January 2020 through September 2024. Most samples were presented as dietary supplements or medicines; 69% came from the illegal market, and Noopept, phenylpiracetam, and phenibut were among substances intercepted as large quantities of raw material. The authors caution that surveillance probably captured only a fraction of the market. [russia-label-2024]

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor-dependent interneuron activation
  description: In rat hippocampal slices, 5 µM Noopept increased firing of stratum-radiatum GABAergic interneurons and increased spontaneous inhibitory postsynaptic currents in CA1 pyramidal cells; α7-selective antagonists nearly abolished these effects. This supports α7-receptor involvement, not direct agonist binding or a human mechanism.
  sourceId: kondratenko-2022-alpha7
  conceptId: nicotinic-receptor
- title: Low-potency competition at AMPA-receptor ligand sites
  description: In rat-brain radioligand assays, Noopept competed at AMPA-receptor binding sites with an IC50 of 80 ± 5.6 µM. IC50 in this assay is not a Ki or Kd and does not establish clinically relevant receptor occupancy.
  sourceId: firstova-2011-ampa
  conceptId: ampa-receptor
- title: HIF-1 reporter activation and proposed PHD2 interaction
  description: In HEK293 reporter assays, Noopept increased HIF-1-dependent signal while eight other tested transcription-factor reporters were not significantly changed. Docking proposed PHD2 binding, but direct enzyme affinity or inhibition was not measured.
  sourceId: molekul-link-168
- title: Neurotrophin-expression changes in rat hippocampus
  description: Acute and 28-day rat studies reported increased hippocampal NGF and BDNF mRNA after Noopept exposure, with no tolerance to the reported neurotrophic signal. These are molecular endpoints in rats, not evidence of human cognitive benefit.
  sourceId: molekul-link-178
- title: Formation of cyclo-prolylglycine in rats
  description: After 5 mg/kg intraperitoneal GVS-111 in rats, cyclo-prolylglycine increased in brain while parent compound was below HPLC detection at one hour; plasma and brain enzymes formed the cyclic dipeptide in vitro. Human conversion to this metabolite has not been robustly demonstrated.
  sourceId: gudasheva-1997-metabolite
```
