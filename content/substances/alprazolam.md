## Summary

Alprazolam is the parent compound represented by PubChem CID 2118 and is a triazolobenzodiazepine marketed in the United States as immediate-release tablets and as extended-release XANAX XR. Current U.S. immediate-release labeling covers acute treatment of generalized anxiety disorder in adults and treatment of panic disorder, with or without agoraphobia, in adults; XANAX XR is labeled for panic disorder. Alprazolam is a Schedule IV controlled substance under U.S. federal law. Sources: pubchem; alprazolam-label; xanax-xr-label; dea-schedules-2026.

The evidence base is indication- and formulation-specific. Older randomized panic-disorder trials found alprazolam superior to placebo across most measured clinical outcomes, while later reanalysis of the FDA trial record for extended-release alprazolam found that only one of five phase 2/3 efficacy trials was clearly positive and estimated a smaller effect when unpublished trials were included. In generalized anxiety disorder, a four-week randomized trial found a statistically significant placebo-adjusted reduction in total Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale score with alprazolam 1.5 mg/day, but its somatic-anxiety factor did not significantly differ from placebo. These findings should not be collapsed into a single formulation-independent or indication-independent efficacy estimate. Sources: cross-national-panic-1992; rickels-gad-2005; ahn-horst-turner-2024.

Acute healthy-volunteer studies consistently separate desired or subjective effects from objective performance. Oral alprazolam can increase sedation and impair driving, memory, information processing, and psychomotor performance, with effects depending strongly on dose and assessment time. One dose-ranging study found statistically significant performance impairment chiefly at 1 mg and no drug-related change in visual-analog ratings of drug effect; a separate 1 mg on-road study found substantial driving and laboratory impairment. A two-week 0.5 mg nightly study found selected visual-memory impairments but no detected psychomotor slowing on its choice-reaction-time measure. These healthy-volunteer results are not treatment-effect estimates for people with anxiety or panic disorder. Sources: kelly-1997; verster-2002; chowdhury-2016; smith-kroboth-1984.

Dependence, withdrawal, misuse, and interactions are central to alprazolam's risk profile. Current U.S. labeling warns that physical dependence and clinically important withdrawal can occur with therapeutic use, that abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction can precipitate potentially life-threatening reactions including seizures, and that concomitant opioids can cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. A panic-disorder discontinuation report documented both rebound panic and a distinct transient withdrawal syndrome, while an outpatient choice experiment found alprazolam functioned as a reinforcer in most of 14 anxious patients yet did not show accompanying signs of abuse or addiction in most participants. Sources: alprazolam-label; pecknold-1993; roache-1997.

## Description

Alprazolam has molecular formula C17H13ClN4, molecular weight 308.8 g/mol, and the preserved BHWiki connectivity SMILES `CC1=NN=C2N1C3=C(C=C(C=C3)Cl)C(=NC2)C4=CC=CC=C4`. This record describes parent alprazolam and does not merge evidence from other benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants into alprazolam treatment evidence. Other substances appear only when a trial used them as comparators or when they define a documented interaction context. Sources: pubchem.

Immediate-release alprazolam has dose-proportional plasma concentrations over the 0.5–3 mg range described in the current label, reaches maximum plasma concentration in about 1–2 hours after oral administration, and has a labeled healthy-adult mean elimination half-life of approximately 11.2 hours with substantial interindividual variation. In six fasting healthy men given 1 mg both orally and intravenously, mean oral half-life was 11.8 hours and estimated mean absorbed fraction was 0.92. The objective psychomotor impairment measured in that experiment had returned to baseline at the five-hour assessment despite a much longer plasma elimination half-life, illustrating why half-life should not be reported as subjective or functional duration. Sources: alprazolam-label; smith-kroboth-1984.

Alprazolam is metabolized principally by CYP3A4. The current label identifies 4-hydroxyalprazolam and α-hydroxyalprazolam as major active metabolites but reports plasma concentrations of each below 4% of parent in the studies underlying the immediate-release label. In a healthy-male sustained-release study spanning single oral doses of 2–10 mg, concentrations of both hydroxylated metabolites rose with dose and their combined plasma concentrations remained below 15% of unchanged alprazolam. The metabolites therefore demonstrate measurable systemic exposure but should not be assumed to dominate clinical effects. Sources: alprazolam-label; wright-1997.

Extended-release alprazolam has a deliberately slower absorption profile. The XANAX XR label reports a morning-dose Tmax of about 10 hours, approximately 90% absolute bioavailability, and about 100% relative bioavailability compared with immediate-release XANAX; parent alprazolam and its two major active metabolites show linear pharmacokinetics through 10 mg once daily. Those XR values should not be substituted for immediate-release peak timing. Sources: xanax-xr-label.

## Evidence note

Patient-treatment evidence, healthy-volunteer pharmacology, misuse research, and regulatory-trial evidence answer different questions. The large Cross-National Collaborative Panic Study supports short-term panic-disorder efficacy of immediate-release alprazolam, but its publication-era reporting does not provide the disclosure detail, modern estimand conventions, or full numerical outcome reporting expected from newer trials. The FDA-record analysis of alprazolam XR is particularly important as negative evidence: five phase 2/3 efficacy trials were identified, only one was clearly positive, and inclusion of unpublished trials reduced the estimated standardized effect to Hedges g 0.33 (95% CI 0.07–0.60). That analysis concerns the XR regulatory evidence base and should not be treated as a re-estimate of every older immediate-release study. Sources: cross-national-panic-1992; ahn-horst-turner-2024.

For generalized anxiety disorder, the four-week fixed-dose active-comparator trial provides a directly quantified alprazolam-placebo result: the least-squares mean total HAM-A change was −10.91 with alprazolam 1.5 mg/day and −8.35 with placebo, a difference of −2.55 points (95% CI −4.72 to −0.39; P=.02). The same study preserves an important clinical null: alprazolam did not significantly improve the HAM-A somatic factor versus placebo at week 1 (P=.21) or the LOCF endpoint (P=.15). The trial was funded by Pfizer, included several Pfizer-affiliated authors, and disclosed extensive industry relationships among several investigators. Sources: rickels-gad-2005.

Healthy-volunteer performance evidence is dose- and task-specific. In the Kelly study, 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg were tested during three hours after dosing; dose-related changes occurred on most performance tasks, but statistically significant impairment was reported principally at 1 mg, and visual-analog drug-effect ratings showed no drug-related change. In the Verster crossover, 1 mg produced marked on-road driving, memory, and psychomotor impairment. A separate two-week 0.5 mg nightly trial reported impairment on selected visual-memory measures but no significant choice-reaction-time impairment. These are not mutually exclusive findings: dose, repeated exposure, timing, population, and test sensitivity differed materially. Sources: kelly-1997; verster-2002; chowdhury-2016.

Tolerance is similarly domain-specific. In an elderly multiple-dose study, sedation and measured impairment were not greater on day 4 even though alprazolam concentrations were two to three times those on day 1, consistent with partial tolerance; performance nevertheless had not returned to baseline. Current labeling also cautions that tolerance to therapeutic effects can occur while tolerance to amnestic and other cognitive impairment may be incomplete. Sources: kroboth-1990; alprazolam-label.

ClinicalTrials.gov was searched as a registry source rather than treated as efficacy evidence. As of this review, NCT06757140 is a recruiting alprazolam–opioid interaction study with planned subjective, respiratory, and pharmacokinetic outcomes and no posted efficacy or safety results from which a conclusion can yet be drawn. Historical registry records also document alprazolam reinforcement research, but registry presence by itself does not establish effect magnitude, safety, or clinical benefit. Sources: nct06757140.

No universal subjective-effect duration is assigned in this record. Plasma Tmax, plasma elimination half-life, onset of improvement in a multiday patient trial, observer-rated sedation, self-rated drug effect, and objective driving or psychomotor impairment are deliberately kept as separate measurements rather than converted into an unsupported onset-duration timeline. Sources: alprazolam-label; xanax-xr-label; smith-kroboth-1984; kelly-1997; verster-2002.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Positive allosteric modulation at the GABAA benzodiazepine site
  description: Alprazolam binds to the benzodiazepine site associated with GABAA receptors and enhances GABA-mediated synaptic inhibition. The resulting CNS-depressant pharmacology provides a mechanistic basis for anxiolytic, sedative, amnestic, anticonvulsant, and psychomotor effects but does not by itself establish a clinical effect size for any indication.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: CYP3A-dependent oxidative metabolism
  description: CYP3A4 is the principal pathway identified in current U.S. labeling for formation of 4-hydroxyalprazolam and α-hydroxyalprazolam. This pathway explains the large exposure changes produced by potent CYP3A inhibitors and the increased clearance observed with enzyme induction.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Low circulating exposure to active hydroxylated metabolites
  description: The current immediate-release label reports plasma concentrations of 4-hydroxyalprazolam and α-hydroxyalprazolam below 4% of parent concentrations. A sustained-release healthy-volunteer study likewise found that the combined concentrations of these metabolites remained below 15% of unchanged alprazolam over 2–10 mg single doses, arguing against assuming that circulating metabolites dominate the observed acute effects.
  sourceId: wright-1997
- title: Exposure–performance relationship
  description: In sustained-release healthy-volunteer research, parent-drug AUC and Cmax rose proportionally over 2–10 mg while maximum sedation increased through 8 mg and psychomotor performance worsened as dose increased. Concentration-effect hysteresis showed that plasma concentration and effect-site response are not perfectly synchronous.
  sourceId: wright-1997
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Opioid boxed warning
  description: Current U.S. labeling warns that concomitant benzodiazepine and opioid use can cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Concomitant prescribing is to be reserved for patients for whom alternative treatment options are inadequate, with the lowest effective doses and durations and monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Abuse, misuse, and addiction
  description: The boxed warning states that alprazolam exposes users to risks of abuse, misuse, and addiction, including overdose or death, and advises assessment of risk before prescribing and monitoring during treatment. The label notes that serious outcomes are more frequent when misuse involves other medications, alcohol, or illicit substances.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Physical dependence and acute withdrawal
  description: Continued alprazolam use can produce physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dosage reduction can precipitate acute withdrawal reactions that may be life-threatening, including seizures; risk rises with longer treatment and higher doses, but dependence and withdrawal are not limited to supratherapeutic use.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Rebound and withdrawal documented in panic-disorder discontinuation studies
  description: In one short-term discontinuation dataset summarized by Pecknold, 109 patients treated for eight weeks were tapered for four weeks and followed for two weeks; rebound panic attacks were reported in 27% and a distinct transient withdrawal syndrome in 35%. A separate longer-term naturalistic cohort found that many patients had difficulty remaining off treatment after tapering. These older studies used taper schedules that do not define an optimal modern taper for an individual patient.
  sourceId: pecknold-1993
- title: Protracted withdrawal
  description: Current labeling states that protracted withdrawal symptoms can persist beyond the initial withdrawal period and, in some cases, for weeks to more than 12 months. The label does not provide a reliable patient-level probability or duration prediction.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Tolerance does not remove cognitive risk
  description: Tolerance can develop to some alprazolam effects during continued use. In a four-day elderly study, sedation and performance impairment showed evidence of tolerance despite rising plasma concentrations, but performance remained below baseline; current labeling separately cautions that little tolerance may develop to some amnestic and cognitive impairments.
  sourceId: kroboth-1990
- title: Driving and hazardous activities
  description: Alprazolam can impair alertness, coordination, memory, and psychomotor performance. Patients are cautioned by the label about driving and hazardous machinery, and a placebo-controlled on-road study found substantial driving impairment after a single 1 mg dose in healthy volunteers.
  sourceId: verster-2002
- title: Strong CYP3A inhibitors
  description: Strong CYP3A inhibitors can markedly raise alprazolam exposure. The immediate-release label contraindicates use with strong CYP3A inhibitors such as ketoconazole and itraconazole, with a specific labeled exception and dose-management scheme for ritonavir because its net effect changes with duration of exposure.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Pregnancy and neonatal effects
  description: Current labeling warns that late-pregnancy benzodiazepine exposure may cause neonatal sedation and withdrawal. Available observational data summarized in the label have not established a clear alprazolam-associated increase in major birth defects, but this does not establish absence of fetal or neonatal risk and the label does not provide pregnancy-specific maternal pharmacokinetic estimates.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Lactation
  description: Alprazolam is present in human milk. The label reports concern for sedation, poor feeding, and poor weight gain based on benzodiazepine exposure and recommends against breastfeeding during alprazolam treatment.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Respiratory impairment and additive CNS depression
  description: The label advises caution in patients with impaired respiratory function and warns that alcohol and other CNS depressants can add to alprazolam's CNS-depressant effects. Controlled interaction studies should not be interpreted as proving safety of combinations outside their specific monitored doses and populations.
  sourceId: alprazolam-label
- title: Reinforcement is not synonymous with diagnosed addiction
  description: In a 14-patient outpatient choice study, 11 participants strongly preferred alprazolam over placebo, demonstrating reinforcement under the experimental procedure, but the investigators reported that most participants did not show accompanying signs of abuse or addiction. This small selected sample cannot estimate population-level misuse risk.
  sourceId: roache-1997
```
