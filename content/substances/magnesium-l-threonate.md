## Summary

Magnesium L-threonate (MgT) is a salt with a relatively low elemental-magnesium fraction. Human trials show selected cognitive or sleep-related signals, but null endpoints, small samples, multi-ingredient products, and commercial sponsorship limit the strength of the evidence. [efsa-2024] [lopresti-smith-2026] [liu-2016] [hausenblas-2024]

## Description

The record separates exact salt exposure from elemental magnesium, peripheral magnesium measurements from brain or cerebrospinal-fluid claims, subjective effects from measured outcomes, and preclinical mechanisms from human findings. It includes the EFSA novel-food assessment, NIH magnesium safety guidance, published trials, and current registry records. [efsa-2024] [nih-magnesium-2026] [nct07640685]

## Evidence note

Short-term cognitive or subjective sleep signals remain uncertain; broad enhancement and superior human brain delivery are unestablished. Positive trials include commercial sponsorship or product involvement. Renal impairment, pregnancy or lactation, interactions, and long-term use remain evidence gaps. [lopresti-smith-2026] [hausenblas-2024] [efsa-2024] [nih-magnesium-2026]

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Peripheral magnesium handling
  description: Magnesium L-threonate supplies magnesium as a salt, and oral magnesium exposure can alter peripheral magnesium handling. The human measurements reviewed here do not establish greater brain or cerebrospinal-fluid delivery.
  sourceId: efsa-2024
  conceptId: magnesium-homeostasis
- title: Preclinical CSF-magnesium signal
  description: EFSA summarizes rat studies in which magnesium L-threonate produced higher relative absorption than selected comparator salts and increased cerebrospinal-fluid magnesium. These are animal findings and do not establish a human blood–brain-barrier advantage.
  sourceId: efsa-2024
  conceptId: magnesium-homeostasis
- title: Synaptic and NMDA-related hypothesis
  description: Synaptic-density, NMDA-receptor, neuronal-energy, GABA, and melatonin explanations are mechanistic or preclinical hypotheses. The 2016 human paper described its synaptic-density rationale as arising from preclinical work; these mechanisms are not clinical outcome measurements.
  sourceId: liu-2016
  conceptId: nmda-receptor
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Renal impairment
  description: NIH states that impaired kidney function raises magnesium toxicity risk because renal clearance is reduced. Magnesium L-threonate trials commonly excluded renal impairment, so safety in chronic kidney disease is not established.
  sourceId: nih-magnesium-2026
- title: Supplemental magnesium upper limits are elemental
  description: NIH lists a U.S. adult upper limit of 350 mg/day elemental magnesium from supplements and medications, while EFSA's assessment uses 250 mg/day supplemental magnesium under its proposed conditions. These thresholds are regulatory or safety contexts, not efficacy doses.
  sourceId: nih-magnesium-2026
- title: Short-term tolerability has limited scope
  description: EFSA described 47 adverse events in 28 of 51 safety participants in the controlled 2016 study as mild; gastrointestinal events occurred in 5 of 25 active participants and 4 of 26 placebo participants. The study was small and excluded important higher-risk groups.
  sourceId: efsa-2024
- title: Pregnancy and lactation evidence gap
  description: The EFSA proposed conditions excluded pregnant and lactating women. This article does not infer safety for those populations from adult trials.
  sourceId: efsa-2024
- title: Commercial sponsorship affects certainty
  description: Positive cognitive and sleep studies include commercial funding, product supply, sponsor design involvement, patent ownership, consultant relationships, or industry-employed authors. The disclosure status is shown per reference rather than treated as an efficacy grade.
  sourceId: lopresti-smith-2026
```
