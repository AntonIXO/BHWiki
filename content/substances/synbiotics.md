## Summary

Synbiotics combine live microorganisms with substrates used by microorganisms. Human effects belong to the studied strains, substrate and product, rather than to a universal synbiotic class effect.

## Description

Complementary and synergistic formulations are distinct. Trials address gastrointestinal, infection or metabolic outcomes in different populations and with different controls. Microbiome or laboratory changes must be separated from patient-important clinical endpoints.

## Evidence note

Product identity, comparator, trial quality and commercial involvement constrain interpretation. Results cannot be transferred automatically between strains or preparations. Mixtures have no single molecular identity or elimination half-life; missing safety or disclosure evidence remains unassessed.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Complementary synbiotic action
  description: In a complementary synbiotic, the live microorganism qualifies as a probiotic and the substrate qualifies as a prebiotic at their respective conditions of use; they may contribute separate benefits without evidence that the substrate preferentially supports the administered strain. The prebiotic is selectively utilized by host microorganisms, principally resident microbiota.
  sourceId: isapp-2020
  conceptId: selective-microbial-substrate-utilization
- title: Synergistic synbiotic action
  description: A synergistic synbiotic is intentionally designed so that the substrate is selectively utilized by the co-administered microorganism or microorganisms. Demonstrating growth or substrate utilization in vitro is insufficient by itself; selective utilization and a host health benefit must be demonstrated in the relevant target context.
  sourceId: isapp-2020
  conceptId: selective-microbial-substrate-utilization
- title: Administered-strain establishment is not guaranteed by adding a putative prebiotic
  description: In adults with obesity, 5 g/day GOS produced a bifidogenic response in resident microbiota but did not significantly raise strain-specific fecal numbers of B. adolescentis IVS-1 or B. animalis subsp. lactis BB-12 when added to those organisms. The investigators found no functional synergism and proposed competition from resident microbiota as one explanation.
  sourceId: krumbeck-2018
  conceptId: selective-microbial-substrate-utilization
- title: Neonatal strain persistence is setting-dependent
  description: A small Indian neonatal study reported months-long persistence after L. plantarum ATCC 202195 + FOS, whereas the larger 2025 Bangladesh phase-II study found progressive decline after dosing and no persistence beyond approximately two months. In Bangladesh, FOS did not materially increase abundance or persistence relative to the organism-only regimen, showing that colonization cannot be assumed from formulation alone.
  sourceId: pell-2025
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Effects are strain-, substrate-, dose-, formulation-, and population-specific
  description: The synbiotic label does not make products interchangeable. Clinical findings from one strain/substrate pairing should not be extrapolated to another, and a health benefit established for a component alone does not prove that a new combination is synergistic.
  sourceId: isapp-2020
- title: Older “synbiotic” trials may not satisfy the current scientific definition
  description: Many historical studies called any probiotic-plus-prebiotic combination a synbiotic without demonstrating the selective-utilization requirements now used for synergistic synbiotics. Such trials can still provide evidence about the tested formulation, but their terminology should not be treated as proof of mechanism.
  sourceId: isapp-2020
- title: Safety of a novel pairing cannot be inferred completely from its individual components
  description: ISAPP notes that enhancing growth or physiological activity of a co-administered live microorganism with a targeted substrate can change the effective in-vivo exposure, so a synergistic pairing needs safety assessment as a combination. Published probiotic/prebiotic/synbiotic trials have also historically reported adverse events inconsistently.
  sourceId: isapp-2020
- title: Evidence in severely ill or immunocompromised people is limited
  description: Several human synbiotic trials used restrictive eligibility criteria excluding severe illness, immunosuppression, active cancer, or important systemic disease. Absence of a safety signal in those selected cohorts therefore does not establish safety in substantially immunocompromised or critically ill populations.
  sourceId: krumbeck-2018
- title: Neonatal ATCC 202195 efficacy does not establish that FOS was the active or necessary component
  description: The large Indian efficacy trial tested ATCC 202195 and FOS only as a combination. The later Bangladesh factorial-style comparison found no material advantage of FOS for strain abundance or persistence. Whether FOS contributes to the reported Indian clinical protection remains unresolved.
  sourceId: pell-2025
- title: B420 body-composition conclusions depend on analysis population
  description: The six-month B420/polydextrose trial was negative for the primary total-body-fat comparison in the intention-to-treat population. Favorable findings came from the smaller per-protocol population and a post-hoc factorial analysis, which are more vulnerable to attrition and analytic-selection bias.
  sourceId: stenman-2016
- title: Commercial sponsorship and investigator conflicts require transparent interpretation
  description: The B420 trial was commercially funded and involved sponsor employees in the research program. The Krumbeck synbiotic study disclosed industry research funding to investigators and co-ownership of a company developing products involving IVS-1. These disclosures do not invalidate results, but they are relevant to evidence appraisal.
  sourceId: stenman-2016
```
