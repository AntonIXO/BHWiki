## Summary

- claim: "Magnesium malate is the 1:1 magnesium salt of malic acid, formula C4H4MgO5, molecular weight 156.38 g/mol; the anhydrous formula contains approximately 15.5% elemental magnesium by mass, a calculation rather than a universal product assay."
  sourceId: MM-01
- claim: "The principal human fibromyalgia trial commonly described as a magnesium-malate study used Super Malic, a combination of malic acid and magnesium hydroxide, not isolated magnesium malate; its blinded phase found no clear treatment effect."
  sourceId: [MM-05, MM-06]
- claim: "One human study used timed-release di-magnesium malate, but the product also supplied vitamins C, B6, folate, and B12; reported fatigue and symptom changes therefore cannot be attributed to di-magnesium malate alone."
  sourceId: MM-08
- assessment: "No isolated human trial establishes magnesium malate efficacy for fibromyalgia, chronic pain, fatigue, energy, or exercise performance. Short-term safety and absorption evidence is product-specific and limited."
  sourceId: [MM-04, MM-05, MM-06, MM-07, MM-08]

## Description

- identity: "Magnesium malate is also called magnesium 2-hydroxybutanedioate; the FDA substance record lists it as a 1:1 magnesium salt with CAS 869-06-7."
  sourceId: [MM-01, MM-02]
- elemental_math: "For anhydrous C4H4MgO5, 24.305 g magnesium divided by 156.38 g/mol gives approximately 15.5% elemental magnesium; 1,000 mg of that idealized salt would therefore contain approximately 155 mg elemental magnesium."
  sourceId: MM-01
- naming_caveat: "Retail terminology can include mono-magnesium malate, hydrated magnesium malate, and di-magnesium malate or magnesium-malate complexes; these are not interchangeable by compound mass. EFSA found the proposed di-magnesium-malate material insufficiently characterized and could not establish that it differed from already authorized magnesium malate."
  sourceId: MM-07
- trial_identity: "Super Malic was described as malic acid plus magnesium hydroxide. It should not be treated as a trial of the isolated 1:1 magnesium-malate salt."
  sourceId: MM-05
- label_rule: "U.S. Supplement Facts panels report elemental magnesium, not the mass of the complete magnesium-containing compound."
  sourceId: MM-03

## Evidence note

- scope: "This article does not transfer pain, fatigue, or efficacy findings from citrate, glycinate, oxide, chloride, sulfate, or other magnesium salts to magnesium malate."
  sourceId: [MM-06, MM-07, MM-09]
- attribution: "A magnesium-plus-malic-acid product cannot determine whether any observed effect came from magnesium, malic acid, their combination, placebo response, or other study factors."
  sourceId: [MM-05, MM-06, MM-08]
- evidence hierarchy: "The strongest directly relevant human evidence is a small, manufacturer-funded blinded crossover trial of a magnesium/malic-acid combination; later improvement occurred only in an uncontrolled dose-escalation extension."
  sourceId: MM-05
- inference boundary: "Malate participation in the Krebs or tricarboxylic-acid cycle is a mechanistic rationale, not evidence that oral magnesium malate increases energy or reduces pain in humans."
  sourceId: [MM-04, MM-05, MM-06]

**Doses:**

- exact_salt_dose: "No validated elemental-magnesium dose has been established for isolated magnesium malate for pain, fibromyalgia, fatigue, or energy."

  sourceId: MM-05

**Pharmacokinetics:**

- human_acute: "In the MagSRT study, 2 tablets supplying 250 mg magnesium as timed-release di-magnesium malate increased serum magnesium by 22% at 4 hours; the increase was 17% above baseline at 8 hours."
  sourceId: MM-08
- human_cellular: "In the same product study, red-blood-cell magnesium increased 6% after 30 days and 30% after 90 days in the 24-person extension subgroup."
  sourceId: MM-08
- human_comparison: "In an unpublished 14-person crossover submitted to EFSA, reported serum-magnesium AUC values were 0.196 for di-magnesium malate, 0.225 for buffered bisglycinate, 0.164 for bisglycinate, and 0.109 for magnesium oxide; the difference was not a peer-reviewed independent comparison of commercial mono-magnesium malate."
  sourceId: MM-07
- animal_comparison: "In rats given a single dose equivalent to 400 mg magnesium per 70 kg, magnesium malate had the highest AUC among the tested compounds and remained elevated in serum longer; this does not establish human pharmacokinetics or clinical benefit."
  sourceId: MM-09
- evidence_gap: "No independent human study has established an absorption fraction, half-life, tissue distribution, or superiority of ordinary mono-magnesium malate over other forms; timed-release di-magnesium malate data cannot be generalized to every product labeled magnesium malate."
  sourceId: [MM-07, MM-08, MM-09]

**Cautions:**

- gastrointestinal: "Supplemental magnesium can cause diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramping; magnesium-malate-specific studies also reported loose stools, diarrhea, abdominal symptoms, dizziness, and postural hypotension."

  sourceId: MM-03
- upper_limit_context: "The adult 350 mg/day supplemental-magnesium upper limit was exceeded by the 500 mg/day MagSRT intervention and by the highest reported Super Malic regimens; those study doses cannot be treated as generally safe doses."

  sourceId: [MM-01, MM-07, MM-08]

**Interactions:**
