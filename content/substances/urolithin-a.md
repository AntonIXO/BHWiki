Urolithin A has been studied in controlled human trials of muscle performance and mitochondrial biomarkers. Secondary or surrogate improvements do not by themselves establish a broad anti-aging benefit.

### Urolithin A

BHWiki slug: urolithin-a

Included evidence: primary human trials, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, ClinicalTrials.gov records, current FDA regulatory material, and the primary human renal-calculus case series. Animal/cell studies, narrative reviews, marketing pages, and mechanistic extrapolation from animals were excluded from efficacy conclusions.

### Evidence verdict

Urolithin A (UA) should be split into two materially different exposure categories. Direct oral UA—typically purified/manufactured UA and frequently the proprietary ingredient Mitopure—delivers UA without requiring microbial conversion. Pomegranate, walnuts, berries, ellagitannins, and ellagic acid are precursor interventions whose conversion to UA varies markedly by microbiome; their clinical effects cannot be treated as evidence for a known dose of direct UA. In a 100-person human bioavailability study, only about 12% had detectable UA at baseline and roughly 40% became substantial UA producers after pomegranate juice, whereas direct 500 mg UA produced more than sixfold greater integrated systemic UA/conjugate exposure. [dr-urolithin-a-s2]

The strongest human efficacy signal is for selected measures of skeletal-muscle fatigue resistance and strength, not global physical function or longevity. In older adults, 1,000 mg/day for four months improved hand and leg fatigue-resistance versus placebo at two months, but the treatment difference was no longer significant at four months; the co-primary 6-minute walk and maximal ATP-production outcomes were not significantly improved. [dr-urolithin-a-s3] In ATLAS, 500 and 1,000 mg/day improved hamstring torque, but the prespecified peak-power endpoint was null, while favorable peak-VO₂ and 6-minute-walk changes at 1,000 mg were primarily within-group/borderline rather than conventionally significant against placebo. [dr-urolithin-a-s4]

The most current systematic synthesis is correspondingly cautious. A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis included five randomized trials and 236 participants; only two studies could be pooled for the 6-minute walk test, yielding +17.03 m (95% CI −5.33 to 39.40; p=0.135; I²=0%), with low GRADE certainty. Strength, endurance, aerobic, biochemical, and mitochondrial outcomes were too heterogeneous for quantitative pooling and were characterized as exploratory rather than reproducibly efficacious. [dr-urolithin-a-s11]

There is no demonstrated human anti-aging or longevity efficacy in the sense of longer lifespan, delayed disability, reduced dementia, or validated healthspan extension. The direct-UA studies are weeks to four months long and mostly assess muscle, circulating biomarkers, muscle molecular signatures, or immune-cell phenotypes. A 2024 systematic review likewise found molecular/mitochondrial and selected muscle signals but no consistent improvement in physical function, anthropometrics, or cardiovascular outcomes. [dr-urolithin-a-s12] No study in the direct-UA human corpus reported here used lifespan, all-cause mortality, dementia incidence, disability-free survival, or another validated human longevity endpoint. Claims based on animal mitophagy/lifespan experiments therefore remain mechanistic hypotheses, not demonstrated human anti-aging efficacy. [dr-urolithin-a-s11][dr-urolithin-a-s12]

### Identity, microbiome conversion, and pharmacokinetics

| Exposure | Human intervention and PK | Interpretation |

Circulating forms included parent UA, UA-glucuronide, and UA-sulfate. Direct UA produced >6-fold greater integrated systemic exposure than pomegranate juice. [dr-urolithin-a-s2] | Bypasses the microbiome-conversion bottleneck. |
| Pomegranate precursor | The comparison used about 8 fl oz/240 mL pomegranate juice, containing ellagitannin/ellagic-acid precursors rather than UA itself. Only a subset of participants generated substantial UA. [dr-urolithin-a-s2] | A pomegranate trial is not a direct-UA dose trial. |
| Walnuts/berries/other ellagitannin foods | These provide precursors that require microbial metabolism. Human UA production differs substantially among individuals. [dr-urolithin-a-s2] | Clinical effects of the whole food cannot automatically be assigned to UA. |
| Mitopure | ClinicalTrials.gov explicitly identifies Mitopure as urolithin A in the ENERGIZE and ATLAS programs; registered doses include 500 and 1,000 mg/day. [dr-urolithin-a-s16][dr-urolithin-a-s17] | Mitopure is a branded ingredient/formulation of UA, not a separate metabolite. Evidence from it should not automatically establish equivalence for every marketed UA formulation. |

After 500 mg direct UA, mean UA-glucuronide concentrations in the comparative bioavailability trial were approximately 481 ng/mL at six hours and 256 ng/mL at 24 hours, versus about 13 ng/mL and 110 ng/mL, respectively, after pomegranate juice. Earlier direct-UA work was reported as consistent with an approximately 24-hour elimination half-life and disappearance over several days. [dr-urolithin-a-s2]

Conjugation is quantitatively important. In ENERGIZE after chronic 1,000 mg/day dosing, plasma parent UA was approximately 1,038 pg/mL (1.038 ng/mL) while UA-glucuronide was approximately 1,014 ng/mL at four months—illustrating that measurements of free parent UA alone substantially understate systemic metabolite exposure. [dr-urolithin-a-s3] The 2026 renal-calculus report likewise notes that glucuronidated and sulfated species dominate biological exposure and are more soluble than unconjugated UA. [dr-urolithin-a-s13]

This distinction also affects “bioavailability” claims. Direct UA provides a comparatively standardized exposure, while precursor foods produce delayed and metabotype-dependent exposure. Thus, a result from a direct 1,000 mg/day UA trial should not be translated into “eat pomegranate/walnuts for the same dose,” nor should an ellagitannin-food trial be counted as a direct-UA efficacy replication. [dr-urolithin-a-s2]

The sports evidence is therefore mixed rather than uniformly ergogenic: a small soccer pilot was positive, resistance-trained athletes improved selected fatigue/isometric tests rather than 1RM, and highly trained runners had improved recovery-related measures without demonstrable 3,000-m performance enhancement. For general physical function, the null primary findings matter. ENERGIZE did not beat placebo on 6-minute walk or maximal ATP production; ATLAS did not meet its peak-power primary endpoint; and the 2026 meta-analysis found the pooled 6-minute-walk estimate statistically inconclusive and low-certainty. [dr-urolithin-a-s3][dr-urolithin-a-s4][dr-urolithin-a-s11]

### Biomarkers, metabolic effects, cognition, and longevity

The first-in-human study found modulation of circulating acylcarnitines and skeletal-muscle mitochondrial gene expression after four weeks, demonstrating biological activity, not clinical rejuvenation. [dr-urolithin-a-s1] ENERGIZE subsequently found reductions in selected acylcarnitines, ceramides, and CRP while simultaneously finding no improvement in directly measured maximal ATP-production capacity. [dr-urolithin-a-s3]

ATLAS similarly produced a complicated dose-dependent molecular picture rather than one uniform mitochondrial response. The 500-mg arm showed mitochondrial transcriptomic and acylcarnitine signals; the 1,000-mg arm did not reproduce every one of those changes, while proteomics and targeted assays suggested effects on oxidative-phosphorylation proteins and Parkin-associated mitophagy markers. These muscle molecular findings are supportive mechanistic biomarkers and should remain separate from the trial's null peak-power primary endpoint. [dr-urolithin-a-s4]

In the runner trial, skeletal-muscle proteomics suggested upregulation of mitochondrial pathways and downregulation of inflammatory pathways, but the authors reported no change in measured mitochondrial function and no significant improvement in race-performance outcome. [dr-urolithin-a-s7] This is a particularly clear example of why a “mitochondrial signature” is not interchangeable with a clinical performance benefit.

The immune-aging trial likewise measured biology rather than aging outcomes. Four weeks of 1,000 mg/day increased naive-like/less-terminally-exhausted CD8 cells by a treatment difference of about 0.50 percentage points and increased CD8 fatty-acid-oxidation capacity by about 14.72 percentage points, with additional mitochondrial and immune-function changes. It did not show reduced infections, morbidity, disability, mortality, or slower clinical aging. [dr-urolithin-a-s8] The study received Mitopure and research support from Amazentis and included company-linked authors. [dr-urolithin-a-s8]

Inflammatory findings are not perfectly consistent. CRP declined in ENERGIZE and ATLAS. [dr-urolithin-a-s3][dr-urolithin-a-s4] In the small resistance-trained trial, however, CRP increased from baseline within the UA arm, even though the between-group comparison favored UA; IL-6 was not significantly altered and SOD moved in an unexpected direction relative to placebo. [dr-urolithin-a-s5] The evidence therefore supports possible immunometabolic modulation more comfortably than a blanket claim that UA is a proven anti-inflammatory treatment.

Human metabolic efficacy remains unestablished. ATLAS found no meaningful lean- or fat-mass change despite molecular metabolic effects. [dr-urolithin-a-s4] The 10-person HFrEF trial found unchanged glucose and most lipid measures, with only HDL-C increasing. [dr-urolithin-a-s6] Acylcarnitine changes are appropriately recorded as metabolic/mitochondrial biomarkers, not evidence that UA prevents diabetes or causes weight loss.

For cognition, the October 2026 obesity RCT is the most directly relevant published study located. Four weeks of 1,000 mg/day changed task-evoked cerebrovascular hemodynamics but did not improve task performance; its primary peripheral endothelial outcome was also null. [dr-urolithin-a-s10] Consequently, a cerebral blood-flow biomarker should not be rewritten as evidence that UA “improves cognition.”

For longevity, the human evidence remains insufficient. The 2024 systematic review covered doses of roughly 10–1,000 mg/day over 28 days to four months and found molecular and selected muscle effects but no consistent physical-function, anthropometric, or cardiovascular improvement. [dr-urolithin-a-s12] No study in the direct-UA human corpus reported here used lifespan, all-cause mortality, dementia incidence, disability-free survival, or another validated human longevity endpoint. Claims based on animal mitophagy/lifespan experiments therefore remain mechanistic hypotheses, not demonstrated human anti-aging efficacy. [dr-urolithin-a-s11][dr-urolithin-a-s12]

### Safety, renal/hepatic evidence, pregnancy, and interactions

Short-term tolerability in selected adults is reasonably reassuring but should not be described as established long-term safety. In ENERGIZE, 33 total adverse events occurred—18 in the UA group and 15 with placebo—with no serious adverse events and one withdrawal per group. Routine vitals, chemistry, hematology, and urinalysis showed no significant between-group safety difference. Two liver-enzyme elevations and one alkaline-phosphatase elevation occurred in the UA arm, without establishment of a causal hepatotoxicity signal. [dr-urolithin-a-s3]

ATLAS likewise reported 102 treatment-emergent events across the three arms without a significant routine laboratory, hematology, vital-sign, or urinalysis safety signal during 120 days. [dr-urolithin-a-s4] The key limitation is duration: the principal published direct-UA efficacy studies extend only to roughly four months, not years. [dr-urolithin-a-s12]

A new renal signal deserves explicit inclusion. The 2026 primary case series identified urolithin A in urinary stones from eight patients aged 59–87, accounting for 5–80% of stone composition; all stones also included calcium oxalate and/or calcium phosphate. Exposure histories were available in six patients, all of whom reported walnuts daily or nearly daily, often in smoothies; none reported taking direct UA supplements. [dr-urolithin-a-s13] Thus, UA can occur as a component of human urinary calculi, but this case series does not establish that Mitopure or another direct-UA supplement caused those stones. [dr-urolithin-a-s13]

The renal report hypothesizes that a high-UA-producing microbiome and unusually high urinary unconjugated/free UA—possibly related to less efficient conjugation—could facilitate crystallization, but the mechanism and incidence are unknown. [dr-urolithin-a-s13] This signal is especially relevant because controlled UA trials generally enrolled selected participants rather than patients with substantial kidney disease.

Renal/hepatic disease evidence is correspondingly weak. ENERGIZE excluded dialysis-requiring severe CKD and clinically important uncontrolled renal/hepatic disorders. [dr-urolithin-a-s16] ATLAS excluded renal or hepatic impairment/disease. [dr-urolithin-a-s17] Normal short-term laboratory results in these selected cohorts therefore cannot establish safety in CKD, cirrhosis, or other clinically important organ impairment.

Pregnancy and lactation data are inadequate. ATLAS excluded pregnancy/breastfeeding and required contraception for participants of childbearing potential; the 2026 obesity study also excluded pregnant/breastfeeding participants. [dr-urolithin-a-s17][dr-urolithin-a-s19] A registered lactation study involved pomegranate juice and measurement of gut-derived metabolites in breast milk, not direct-UA supplementation and not a definitive maternal/infant UA safety trial. [dr-urolithin-a-s21] No adequate direct-UA pregnancy/lactation safety evidence was identified.

Medication-interaction evidence is also insufficient. No dedicated human pharmacokinetic drug–drug interaction trial was identified in the allowed-source corpus. ATLAS specifically excluded or restricted important medication classes, including anticoagulant/antiplatelet agents, statins, thyroid medications, and steroid contexts, which further limits inference about concomitant therapy. [dr-urolithin-a-s17] “No interaction observed” should therefore not be used as a general medication-safety claim.

No regulator pharmacovigilance analysis establishing an incidence rate or specific postmarketing UA safety signal was verified in this source-limited review. The renal-calculus series is best treated as a primary human safety signal, not as a quantified pharmacovigilance rate. [dr-urolithin-a-s13]

### Regulation, product identity, sponsorship, and evidence quality

In the United States, FDA's current GRAS framework addresses whether an intended food use is generally recognized as safe and therefore exempt from the ordinary food-additive premarket-approval requirement; it is not an approval of efficacy, disease treatment, cognition enhancement, or anti-aging effects. [dr-urolithin-a-s14] Accordingly, food-ingredient or dietary-supplement regulatory status must not be presented as FDA validation of clinical efficacy.

ClinicalTrials.gov identifies Mitopure as urolithin A in multiple Amazentis-sponsored studies, including first-in-human, ENERGIZE, ATLAS, and the immune-health program. [dr-urolithin-a-s15][dr-urolithin-a-s16][dr-urolithin-a-s17][dr-urolithin-a-s18] A current UA-specific FDA GRAS notice entry could not be directly retrieved from the official inventory during this research pass, so this report intentionally does not rely on an unverified GRAS-notice number. The current FDA GRAS framework itself was directly verified. [dr-urolithin-a-s14]

Sponsor concentration is a material evidence-quality issue. ENERGIZE was funded by Amazentis SA, and JAMA's disclosure states that the funder had a role in study design/conduct, data collection and management, analysis/interpretation, manuscript preparation/review, and the publication decision; D'Amico and Singh reported Amazentis employment, and Singh reported pending patents. [dr-urolithin-a-s3]

The direct-UA/pomegranate bioavailability work was likewise Amazentis-funded, with multiple employee, board, and advisory relationships. [dr-urolithin-a-s2] The runner trial was Amazentis-funded and included sponsor employees, while the immune trial received product/study funding from Amazentis and also included company employees/executive relationships. [dr-urolithin-a-s7][dr-urolithin-a-s8] This concentration does not make findings invalid, but it makes independent replication, prespecified primary outcomes, multiplicity control, and between-arm comparisons especially important.

The 2026 meta-analysis provides a useful counterweight: it was supported by Korean public/government research programs, declared no commercial/financial conflict, and concluded that pooled 6MWT evidence was low-certainty while non-6MWT findings remained exploratory. [dr-urolithin-a-s11]

For BHWiki grading, the evidence supports low-to-moderate confidence in a signal for selected muscle fatigue-resistance/strength outcomes, low confidence for whole-body physical function or athletic performance, repeated but surrogate mitochondrial/immunometabolic biomarker activity, and insufficient evidence for cognition, metabolic-disease treatment, or human longevity/anti-aging efficacy. Short-term safety in selected adults is fairly reassuring through approximately four months, while long-term safety, direct-supplement kidney-stone risk, significant renal/hepatic impairment, pregnancy/lactation, and drug interactions remain inadequately characterized. [dr-urolithin-a-s11][dr-urolithin-a-s13]

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Mitophagy induction in preclinical models
  description: UA induced mitophagy in C. elegans and mammalian experimental systems and was associated with improved muscle function in rodent models. This establishes a strong preclinical mitochondrial-quality-control mechanism but does not by itself establish human clinical efficacy.
  sourceId: ryu-2016-mitophagy
  conceptId: mitophagy
- title: Human skeletal-muscle mitochondrial and Parkin-associated pathway engagement
  description: ATLAS skeletal-muscle transcriptomic, proteomic and targeted protein analyses showed UA-associated enrichment of mitochondrial pathways and Parkin-mediated quality-control markers, including increased phospho-Parkin in a small biopsy subset. These are supportive pathway-engagement markers; direct whole-body mitophagy flux was not measured.
  sourceId: singh-2022-atlas
  conceptId: mitophagy
- title: Phase-II conjugation after absorption
  description: Human bioavailability studies detect parent UA together with UA-glucuronide and UA-sulfate in plasma, demonstrating extensive conjugative metabolism after direct oral exposure. Circulating conjugates should not be treated interchangeably with unconjugated parent UA in pharmacokinetic interpretation.
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Clinical efficacy remains uncertain
  description: A 2026 systematic review found five small heterogeneous randomized muscle-related trials and low-certainty, statistically inconclusive pooled evidence for the 6-minute-walk test. Strength, endurance and biomarker findings were considered exploratory rather than reproducible proof of efficacy.
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta
- title: Several important primary or performance endpoints were null
  description: ENERGIZE did not establish significant UA superiority for its 6-minute-walk or maximal-ATP-production primary endpoints; ATLAS did not significantly improve its prespecified peak-power endpoint; and the trained-runner trial did not significantly improve 3,000 m performance.
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta
- title: Published long-term safety evidence is limited
  description: The core randomized direct-UA studies evaluated exposures lasting weeks to approximately four months. They were generally well tolerated, but this evidence does not establish safety of indefinite use or safety across unstudied populations.
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta
- title: Pregnancy, lactation and pediatric evidence is insufficient
  description: The principal direct-UA efficacy trials examined adults and frequently excluded pregnancy; the available trial set does not establish safety or benefit in pregnancy, lactation, infants or children.
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability
- title: Direct UA is not equivalent to ellagitannin-rich food or extract
  description: Gut microbial conversion of ellagitannin and ellagic-acid precursors varies substantially among individuals. Direct 500 mg UA produced far more consistent exposure than pomegranate juice in NOURISH, so precursor-food studies should not be assigned the pharmacokinetics of direct UA.
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability
- title: Industry sponsorship and inventor interests are common in the foundational evidence
  description: ATLAS was funded by Amazentis SA and disclosed multiple authors as company employees plus board or scientific-advisory relationships. Other pivotal trials have similar commercial involvement. These disclosures warrant attention to independent replication but do not by themselves invalidate the findings.
  sourceId: singh-2022-atlas
- title: Biomarkers are not disease-treatment evidence
  description: Changes in acylcarnitines, mitochondrial proteins, inflammatory markers or immune-cell states provide biological evidence but do not demonstrate that UA treats sarcopenia, infection, cancer, neurodegeneration or other diseases.
```
