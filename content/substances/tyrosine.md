## Summary

Tyrosine is recorded here as C9H11NO3, PubChem CID 6057. It is indexed under Amino acid derivative. Effects, doses, pharmacokinetics, interactions, and legal status have not been assessed for this draft.

Deep Research synthesis: L-tyrosine is the free amino-acid form of tyrosine and a precursor for catecholamines, thyroid hormones, melanin, and proteins.[dr-tyrosine-s14] In randomized human studies, acute free L-tyrosine most consistently preserved selected working-memory, vigilance, and information-processing tasks during cold exposure or sleep loss; it is not a reliably effective general cognitive enhancer in rested conditions.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s6] A later randomized trial found worse cognitive flexibility after tyrosine under high cognitive load.[dr-tyrosine-s9]

N-acetyl-L-tyrosine (NALT) is an acetylated derivative, not the same compound as free L-tyrosine. The available human NALT study measured intravenous precursor handling rather than cognition and found substantial unchanged urinary excretion, so superior oral delivery or cognitive equivalence is not established.[dr-tyrosine-s13]

## Description

Tyrosine is an identity record for PubChem CID 6057 (C9H11NO3). The structure and molecular weight come from that compound record. Salts, stereoisomers, preparations, and commercial products can differ. It is indexed under Amino acid derivative. Effects, doses, pharmacokinetics, interactions, and legal status have not been assessed for this draft.

Deep Research synthesis: Free L-tyrosine is incorporated into proteins and can be produced from L-phenylalanine.[dr-tyrosine-s14] Tyrosine hydroxylase converts it to L-DOPA; downstream enzymes produce dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine.[dr-tyrosine-s14] Tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin participate in formation of thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), while tyrosinase-dependent pathways produce melanin.[dr-tyrosine-s14]

Most human cognition trials used free L-tyrosine, usually as a single oral load. The evidence below should not be transferred automatically to NALT.[dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s4][dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s6][dr-tyrosine-s8][dr-tyrosine-s9]

## Evidence note

A systematic review of 15 placebo-controlled studies in healthy adults found that positive cognitive findings were concentrated in demanding conditions such as cold, heat, multitasking, or sleep loss; most studies were acute, small, and heterogeneous.[dr-tyrosine-s2] Another review concluded that benefits are most plausible when catecholamine function is temporarily challenged or depleted, rather than when baseline neurotransmitter function is intact.[dr-tyrosine-s1]

The evidence is therefore strongest for task-specific, short-term preservation of performance under acute stress. It is weaker for rested-state enhancement, long-term supplementation, disease treatment, mood treatment, or athletic performance.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]

**Doses:**

The following are research exposures, not a general-use dosing protocol.

| Context | Human exposure | Main result |
|---|---:|---|
| Plasma pharmacokinetics | 100 or 150 mg/kg orally, fasting | Plasma tyrosine peaked at approximately 2 hours and remained elevated for 2–8 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s11] |
| Cold chamber | 150 mg/kg orally, 2 hours before testing | Reversed a cold-induced working-memory deficit; no effect occurred at 22°C.[dr-tyrosine-s3] |
| Cold-water immersion | 150 mg/kg before each of two immersions; total 300 mg/kg | Improved selected match-to-sample and reaction-time outcomes during approximately 10°C immersion.[dr-tyrosine-s4] |
| Extended wakefulness | 150 mg/kg split dose after approximately 6 hours of testing | Reduced deterioration in psychomotor performance and high-event-rate vigilance for about 3 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s5] |
| Sleep deprivation | 150 mg/kg after overnight sleep deprivation | Improved several, but not all, cognitive and motor measures; effects were less broad than with d-amphetamine.[dr-tyrosine-s6] |
| Heat study | Two 75-mg/kg or two 150-mg/kg pharmacokinetic doses; separate military heat crossover | Serum tyrosine rose substantially, but cognition and physical performance did not improve.[dr-tyrosine-s8] |
| Cognitive-load study | 2.0 g orally | Worsened cognitive flexibility after high cognitive load in a 70-person randomized trial.[dr-tyrosine-s9] |
| Short-term safety study | 1, 2, 3, or 4 g/day for 4 weeks | No serious adverse events or clinically meaningful dose-related laboratory abnormalities in healthy adult men under study conditions.[dr-tyrosine-s14] |
| NALT precursor study | 5 g N-acetyl-L-tyrosine by intravenous infusion over 4 hours | Only a modest plasma-tyrosine rise occurred; 56% of the infused NALT was excreted unchanged within 4 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s13] |

**Pharmacokinetics:**

In 12 fasting adults, 100 or 150 mg/kg of oral free L-tyrosine produced maximal plasma concentrations at approximately 2 hours; concentrations remained significantly elevated for up to 8 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s11] The plasma tyrosine ratio relative to competing neutral amino acids increased from approximately 0.10 at baseline to 0.28 after 100 mg/kg and 0.35 after 150 mg/kg.[dr-tyrosine-s11]

In older adults, 100- and 150-mg/kg doses peaked around 90 minutes, while 200 mg/kg peaked around 120 minutes; concentrations remained above baseline at 4 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s10] Older adults had a larger plasma response than younger adults given the same 150-mg/kg dose, and higher doses were associated with poorer load-dependent working-memory performance in that study.[dr-tyrosine-s10]

Tyrosine and other large neutral amino acids share transport processes relevant to brain entry. In a fed human study, oral tyrosine increased the plasma tyrosine ratio despite a diet containing 113 g of protein; the intervention did not materially change the concentrations of the other measured competing neutral amino acids.[dr-tyrosine-s12]

NALT pharmacokinetics are less established. During 5-g intravenous infusions in 11 healthy volunteers, plasma NALT rose rapidly, plasma free tyrosine increased by approximately 25%, and 56% of administered NALT appeared in urine unchanged within 4 hours; no net splanchnic production of tyrosine was detected.[dr-tyrosine-s13] Because this was intravenous rather than oral administration, it does not fully define oral NALT absorption, but it does not support a claim of clearly superior conversion to free tyrosine.[dr-tyrosine-s13]

**Modifiers:**

- **Stressor type:** Positive cognitive findings occurred in cold exposure and sleep-loss paradigms, whereas a military heat-exercise study found no cognitive or performance benefit despite marked serum tyrosine elevation.[dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s4][dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s6][dr-tyrosine-s8]
- **Baseline state:** The cold-chamber study found benefit at 4°C but no effect at 22°C, supporting a state-dependent rather than universal effect.[dr-tyrosine-s3]
- **Cognitive demand:** A 2-g dose worsened cognitive flexibility after a demanding memory task in a later randomized trial, showing that “stress” and cognitive load do not produce identical responses.[dr-tyrosine-s9]
- **Age and dose:** Older adults had greater plasma exposure and poorer working-memory performance at higher doses in one dose-ranging trial.[dr-tyrosine-s10]
- **Timing:** Cognitive studies commonly tested participants about 1–2 hours after ingestion, overlapping with the observed plasma peak.[dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s11]
- **Food and competing amino acids:** Protein context changes the relative availability of neutral amino acids relevant to brain transport, although the human data do not define a universal fed-versus-fasted rule.[dr-tyrosine-s11][dr-tyrosine-s12]
- **Chemical form:** The cognition literature is primarily free L-tyrosine; NALT has different pharmacokinetic evidence and should not be treated as an interchangeable evidence base.[dr-tyrosine-s13]

**Effects:**

| Domain | Human randomized findings |
|---|---|
| **Cold and working memory** | In eight men, 150 mg/kg restored performance at the longest delayed-matching interval impaired by 4°C exposure; the same dose did not improve performance at 22°C.[dr-tyrosine-s3] In 19 participants undergoing repeated cold-water immersion, two 150-mg/kg doses improved selected match-to-sample accuracy and processing-speed measures relative to cold placebo.[dr-tyrosine-s4] |
| **Sleep loss and vigilance** | During more than 24 hours of wakefulness, a 150-mg/kg split dose reduced deterioration in psychomotor performance and vigilance for roughly 3 hours.[dr-tyrosine-s5] In a larger 76-man study after sleep deprivation, tyrosine improved several measures of running memory, reasoning, mathematical processing, tracking, and vigilance, but not every task.[dr-tyrosine-s6] |
| **Sleep and recovery** | In a separate 76-man sleep-deprivation protocol, 150 mg/kg did not change sleep quantity, sleep quality, or recovery-sleep architecture, although it stimulated prolactin release.[dr-tyrosine-s7] |
| **Heat stress** | In a small military heat-exercise crossover, tyrosine did not improve cognition, physiology, or time-trial performance despite marked serum tyrosine increases.[dr-tyrosine-s8] |
| **Rested or thermoneutral cognition** | No benefit was found in the thermoneutral 22°C condition of the cold-chamber study.[dr-tyrosine-s3] The broader systematic-review literature does not support a consistent, generalized rested-state enhancement effect.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2] |
| **Cognitive flexibility** | In 70 healthy adults, 2 g of tyrosine worsened Wisconsin Card Sorting Test flexibility after high cognitive load rather than preventing impairment.[dr-tyrosine-s9] |

**Outcomes:**

- **Most defensible outcome:** acute preservation of selected cognitive performance during cold exposure or sustained wakefulness, especially working memory, vigilance, and information processing.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s4][dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s6]
- **Not established:** a broad rested-state “focus” effect, reliable mood enhancement, sleep replacement, or treatment of a psychiatric or neurological disorder.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s7]
- **Physical performance:** evidence is mostly null, including a heat-exercise study with substantial biochemical exposure but no performance benefit.[dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s8]
- **Duration:** the cognition evidence is predominantly single-session; the 4-week safety study does not establish long-term cognitive benefit or long-term safety.[dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s14]
- **Potentially adverse context:** higher exposure in older adults and a demanding cognitive-load paradigm produced poorer performance on selected tasks.[dr-tyrosine-s9][dr-tyrosine-s10]

**Mechanisms:**

The conservative mechanism is substrate availability, not direct dopamine-receptor stimulation.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]

1. Acute stress, cold, prolonged wakefulness, or heavy cognitive demand may increase catecholamine requirements and alter catecholamine synthesis or release.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]
2. Raising circulating free tyrosine can increase the tyrosine-to-competing-neutral-amino-acid ratio relevant to transport into the brain.[dr-tyrosine-s11][dr-tyrosine-s12]
3. Tyrosine hydroxylase can then use additional substrate to form L-DOPA, with downstream conversion to dopamine and norepinephrine.[dr-tyrosine-s14]
4. Feedback inhibition and multiple rate-limiting steps constrain the size and duration of any central catecholamine increase.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]
5. The resulting effect is more plausibly **prevention of a stress-related decrement** than enhancement above an already adequate baseline.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]

The human studies generally measured performance and peripheral biomarkers rather than central brain catecholamines, so the catecholamine-preservation model remains mechanistic inference rather than a directly demonstrated explanation for every result.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2]

**Cautions:**

- The most recent controlled safety study enrolled healthy adult men and lasted 4 weeks; it found no serious adverse events and no clinically meaningful dose-related laboratory abnormalities at 1–4 g/day, but it does not establish safety for longer exposure, pregnancy, lactation, children, or people with significant disease.[dr-tyrosine-s14]
- Higher doses are not automatically better: older adults had dose-related plasma increases accompanied by poorer working-memory performance in one trial.[dr-tyrosine-s10]
- The thyroid relationship is biochemical: tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin participate in T4 and T3 synthesis.[dr-tyrosine-s14] However, the American Thyroid Association guideline states that clinical or preclinical evidence for L-tyrosine having thyromimetic properties is absent.[dr-tyrosine-s17]
- NALT has less direct human evidence than free L-tyrosine, and the available human precursor study was intravenous rather than oral.[dr-tyrosine-s13]
- Supplement legal status and label accuracy do not establish efficacy or guarantee that a commercial product contains only the stated ingredient.[dr-tyrosine-s18]

**Claims:**

| Claim | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| “L-tyrosine raises dopamine.” | It supplies substrate for dopamine synthesis, but the size and functional importance of any brain increase vary with state, dose, transport competition, and feedback regulation.[dr-tyrosine-s1][dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s14] |
| “It improves focus for everyone.” | Not supported; rested/control conditions can be null, and one cognitive-load trial found worse flexibility.[dr-tyrosine-s3][dr-tyrosine-s9] |
| “It counteracts sleep deprivation.” | Partly supported for selected short-lived task outcomes, but it does not restore sleep or normalize every cognitive measure.[dr-tyrosine-s5][dr-tyrosine-s6][dr-tyrosine-s7] |
| “It improves exercise performance.” | Not consistently supported; heat-exercise performance was unchanged despite increased serum tyrosine.[dr-tyrosine-s2][dr-tyrosine-s8] |
| “NALT is more bioavailable than free L-tyrosine.” | Not established by the available human evidence; intravenous NALT showed substantial unchanged urinary excretion and only a modest free-tyrosine increase.[dr-tyrosine-s13] |
| “It treats hypothyroidism.” | Not established; the ATA guideline reports no evidence that L-tyrosine has thyromimetic properties.[dr-tyrosine-s17] |
| “It is proven safe long term.” | Not established; controlled safety data cited here extend to 4 weeks in healthy adult men.[dr-tyrosine-s14] |

**Interactions:**

| Substance or context | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| **Phenelzine and related nonselective MAO-inhibitor labeling** | The current phenelzine label explicitly lists L-tyrosine among related compounds that patients should not take because MAO inhibition can potentiate sympathomimetic effects and cause hypertensive crises; the label does not quantify a tyrosine-specific incidence.[dr-tyrosine-s15] |
| **Levodopa/carbidopa** | The carbidopa-levodopa label states that levodopa competes with certain amino acids for intestinal transport and that high-protein intake can impair absorption in some patients. The magnitude of any interaction from a discrete L-tyrosine supplement is not quantified in the cited label.[dr-tyrosine-s16] |
| **Thyroid hormone therapy or thyroid disease** | Tyrosine participates in thyroid-hormone biosynthesis, but clinical thyromimetic activity has not been demonstrated. Direct supplement–thyroid-drug interaction data are insufficient in the cited clinical literature.[dr-tyrosine-s14][dr-tyrosine-s17] |
| **Protein meals and other large neutral amino acids** | These compounds share transport competition relevant to plasma ratios and brain entry; this is a nutrient-transport modifier rather than a conventional drug interaction.[dr-tyrosine-s11][dr-tyrosine-s12] |
| **NALT** | NALT should not be assumed to have the same exposure or interaction profile as free L-tyrosine because human conversion data are sparse and primarily intravenous.[dr-tyrosine-s13] |

**Legal:**

In the United States, dietary supplements are not approved by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or labeling before marketing; manufacturers are responsible for product safety, truthful labeling, and compliance with applicable rules.[dr-tyrosine-s18] Disease-treatment claims can cause a product to be regulated as a drug rather than as an ordinary dietary supplement.[dr-tyrosine-s18] Legal status outside the United States, and the status of a specific NALT formulation, depends on jurisdiction, formulation, labeling, and intended use.
