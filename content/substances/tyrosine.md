## Summary

Acute free L-tyrosine has the clearest human signal when cold exposure, prolonged wakefulness, or another demand challenges catecholamine function. Rested-state findings are often null, and one high-load study found worse flexibility; a universal focus effect is unsupported. NALT is a separate form whose IV data do not establish oral equivalence. [tyrosine-r1] [tyrosine-r2] [tyrosine-r9] [tyrosine-r13]

## Description

Tyrosine is an amino-acid precursor used in protein synthesis and in pathways leading to L-DOPA, dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, thyroid hormones, and melanin. Most cognition trials used free L-tyrosine as an acute oral load; NALT evidence is pharmacokinetic and intravenous, so its findings should not be relabeled as free L-tyrosine efficacy. [tyrosine-r13] [tyrosine-r14] [tyrosine-r17]

## Evidence note

The page separates free L-tyrosine from NALT, plasma observations from cognitive outcomes, and stress preservation from broad enhancement. Evidence is mostly acute and heterogeneous; a four-week safety study does not establish a parent half-life or long-term benefit. Funding and conflict disclosures are retained per reference. [tyrosine-r1] [tyrosine-r2] [tyrosine-r14]

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Catecholamine precursor availability
  description: Free L-tyrosine supplies substrate for tyrosine hydroxylase and downstream L-DOPA, dopamine, and norepinephrine synthesis. This is a biochemical pathway, not proof of a uniform brain dopamine increase.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r14
  conceptId: dopamine
- title: Stress-related preservation model
  description: Cold exposure, prolonged wakefulness, and heavy cognitive demand may increase catecholamine requirements; raising circulating tyrosine may help preserve selected task performance when synthesis or release is challenged.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r1
  conceptId: stress-response
- title: Large-neutral-amino-acid transport
  description: Plasma tyrosine competes with other large neutral amino acids relevant to brain transport. Oral tyrosine can raise the tyrosine-to-competitor ratio, but the size of central exposure and functional effect varies with diet and state.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r12
  conceptId: amino-acid
- title: Mechanistic uncertainty and NALT distinction
  description: Human cognition trials measured performance and peripheral biomarkers rather than central catecholamines. NALT showed substantial unchanged urinary excretion during intravenous infusion, so it cannot be assumed to reproduce free L-tyrosine exposure or effects.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r13
  conceptId: amino-acid
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Four-week safety boundary
  description: In healthy adult men, 1–4 g/day for four weeks produced no serious adverse events or clinically meaningful dose-related laboratory abnormalities. This does not establish long-term safety or safety in pregnancy, lactation, children, or significant disease.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r14
- title: Higher exposure is not automatically better
  description: Older adults had dose-dependent plasma increases and poorer load-dependent working-memory performance at higher doses in one acute trial.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r10
- title: Thyroid claims are unestablished
  description: Tyrosine residues participate in thyroid-hormone biosynthesis, but the American Thyroid Association guideline reports no clinical or preclinical evidence that L-tyrosine has thyromimetic properties.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r17
- title: NALT evidence is limited
  description: The cited NALT human study was an intravenous precursor study with no cognition endpoint; oral NALT equivalence, superior delivery, and long-term safety remain unestablished.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r13
- title: Supplement quality and regulatory boundary
  description: U.S. dietary supplements are not approved by FDA for safety or effectiveness before marketing, and disease-treatment claims can change the regulatory category. Legal status and label accuracy depend on jurisdiction and product.
  sourceId: tyrosine-r18
```
