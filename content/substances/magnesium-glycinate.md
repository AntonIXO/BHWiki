## Summary

Magnesium glycinate and bisglycinate commonly describe the same chelate; the anhydrous compound is about 14.1% elemental magnesium. One short sleep RCT found a small self-reported benefit, but it co-delivered 1,523 mg/day glycine. Overall cognition was null, and deficiency evidence remains disease-specific and dose-ambiguous. [mgc-pubchem] [mgc-sleep] [mgc-cognition]

## Description

Pure bisglycinate and buffered glycinate are different product identities: buffered material can contain magnesium oxide. Elemental magnesium, not total chelate mass, is the relevant mineral dose. [mgc-tga] [mgc-ods]

## Evidence note

Direct human evidence is limited to short or disease-specific trials. No human study here isolates magnesium from glycine for sleep, establishes pure-versus-buffered superiority, or provides a validated glycinate-specific terminal half-life. [mgc-sleep] [mgc-brilli] [mgc-review]
