## Summary

Atomoxetine is an oral selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor indicated in the United States for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older. It is not a controlled substance under the U.S. label. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

In ADHD, randomized trials and meta-analyses show symptom improvement over placebo, with smaller average effects than amphetamine or methylphenidate in comparative analyses. Adult benefit may emerge during the first treatment week but commonly grows over several weeks and months. [S2–S5] ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6109107/?utm_source=openai))

Atomoxetine’s plasma peak and elimination half-life do not determine clinical onset: median peak concentration is about 1 hour in most CYP2D6 metabolizer types, while controlled adult trials found progressively larger symptom effects through 26 weeks. [S1, S4] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Healthy-volunteer studies show task-specific effects—not general cognitive enhancement. Acute dosing improved response inhibition in one experiment but produced null findings on probabilistic learning and other measures; neuroimaging effects were context-dependent. [S7, S8] ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1867315/?utm_source=openai))

The major label risks are pediatric suicidal ideation, rare severe liver injury, increased heart rate or blood pressure, cardiovascular contraindications, psychiatric activation, and important exposure increases with CYP2D6 poor metabolism or strong CYP2D6 inhibitors. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

## Description

Atomoxetine hydrochloride is supplied as capsules containing 10, 18, 25, 40, 60, 80, or 100 mg of atomoxetine. The label describes it as a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

The approved indication is ADHD as part of a broader treatment program that may include psychological, educational, and social measures. The U.S. label does not establish atomoxetine as a general-purpose memory, intelligence, productivity, or cognitive-enhancement drug. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

## Evidence note

| Population or question | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Pediatric ADHD | Four randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies in patients aged 6–18 years established efficacy for the label. [S1] | Evidence applies to diagnosed ADHD, not healthy cognition. |
| Adult ADHD | Two 10-week placebo-controlled studies enrolled 280 and 256 adults; both significantly improved CAARS ADHD symptom scores. [S1] | Demonstrates short-term symptom efficacy. |
| Adult pooled trials | Six short-term studies included 1,961 participants and three 6-month studies included 1,413; mean CAARS improvement favored atomoxetine over placebo. [S3] | Most data were from Eli Lilly-sponsored trials. |
| Comparative efficacy | At approximately 12 weeks, clinician-rated standardized mean difference was −0.56 for atomoxetine, versus −0.78 for methylphenidate and −1.02 for amphetamines; negative values favor medication. [S2] | Indirect network comparisons; not a universal ranking for individual patients. |
| Executive functions in ADHD | A 2024 meta-analysis of seven atomoxetine studies involving 829 participants found no significant working-memory effect, but benefits in other tested domains with Hedges’ *g* about 0.36–0.64. [S6] | Null working-memory findings matter; “improves cognition” is domain-specific. |
| Healthy volunteers | A 60-mg acute dose in 60 healthy men improved stop-signal response inhibition but did not improve probabilistic learning or broad alertness measures. [S7] | Laboratory effects do not establish clinical benefit without ADHD. |
| Healthy-volunteer neuroimaging | Atomoxetine altered working-memory network activity differently according to reward context; this was not evidence of uniform behavioral enhancement. [S8] | Neural modulation is not equivalent to improved real-world cognition. |

**Doses:**

The following are U.S. label doses for acute and extended treatment; they are label ranges, not individualized dosing instructions. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

| Group | Starting dose | Target dose | Maximum dose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pediatric patients <70 kg | 0.5 mg/kg/day | 1.2 mg/kg/day | 1.4 mg/kg/day or 100 mg/day, whichever is less |
| Pediatric patients ≥70 kg and adults | 40 mg/day | 80 mg/day | 100 mg/day |

Dosing may be once daily in the morning or divided evenly between morning and late afternoon/early evening. Capsules are taken whole, with or without food. The label reports no additional pediatric benefit above 1.2 mg/kg/day and no evidence supporting effectiveness above 100 mg/day in adults. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

With a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor or in a known CYP2D6 poor metabolizer, the nominal starting, target, and maximum doses remain the same in the label, but the titration interval is extended to 4 weeks when a dose increase is being considered. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

For hepatic impairment, the label specifies 50% of the normal initial and target dose in Child-Pugh class B and 25% in class C; class A uses the normal dose. No taper is required for discontinuation in the label. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Pharmacokinetics:**

| Parameter | Most CYP2D6 metabolizer types | CYP2D6 poor metabolizers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Absolute bioavailability | 63% | 94% |
| Median time to peak concentration | 1 hour | 2.5 hours |
| Protein binding | 98% | 98% |
| Atomoxetine half-life | 5.2 hours | 21.6 hours |
| Accumulation | 1.1-fold | 3.3-fold |

Food does not materially change total exposure, but the label reports a 37% decrease in peak concentration and a 3-hour delay in peak time under the studied conditions. More than 80% of the dose is excreted in urine as 4-hydroxyatomoxetine-O-glucuronide; less than 3% is excreted unchanged. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Atomoxetine is primarily converted by CYP2D6 to 4-hydroxyatomoxetine, an equipotent norepinephrine-transporter inhibitor that is subsequently glucuronidated. N-desmethylatomoxetine is formed largely through CYP2C19 and has approximately 20-fold less pharmacologic activity than atomoxetine. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Onset versus half-life:** a 5-hour half-life in most metabolizers does not imply a 5-hour therapeutic onset. In pooled adult trials, atomoxetine separated from placebo by week 1, had a larger effect by week 4, and showed further average improvement by week 26; response rates also continued to rise during longer treatment. [S4] ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26922462/?utm_source=openai))

**Modifiers:**

**CYP2D6 genotype and phenoconversion.** CYP2D6 poor metabolizers have approximately 10-fold higher atomoxetine AUC and 5-fold higher steady-state peak concentration than other metabolizer types. Their mean heart-rate and blood-pressure changes are also higher in label analyses. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Strong CYP2D6 inhibition can produce a similar exposure pattern. Paroxetine increased atomoxetine AUC about 6.5-fold, while fluoxetine increased it about 6–8-fold in subjects who were not genetically poor metabolizers. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Hepatic impairment.** Atomoxetine AUC increased about two-fold in moderate hepatic impairment and four-fold in severe hepatic impairment, supporting the label’s 50% and 25% dose reductions. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Renal impairment.** Severe renal impairment produced approximately 65% higher systemic exposure, but the label describes the difference as not clinically significant after correction for dose per kilogram. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s000lbl.pdf?utm_source=openai))

**Effects:**

### ADHD symptoms and executive functions

The adult meta-analysis of 13 placebo-controlled trials involving 1,824 participants found atomoxetine superior to placebo for overall ADHD symptoms, with standardized mean difference −0.45; effects were −0.42 for inattention and −0.36 for hyperactivity/impulsivity. [S5] ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27931908/?utm_source=openai))

The chronic executive-function meta-analysis found atomoxetine benefits for attention, inhibition, and reaction-time-related outcomes, but no significant pooled working-memory effect. [S6] ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38718988/))

### Common adverse effects

In pediatric placebo-controlled studies, common adverse reactions included headache 19% versus 15% with placebo, abdominal pain 18% versus 10%, decreased appetite 16% versus 4%, somnolence 11% versus 4%, vomiting 11% versus 6%, nausea 10% versus 5%, fatigue 8% versus 3%, irritability 6% versus 3%, and dizziness 5% versus 2%. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Adult labeling also identifies nausea, dry mouth, decreased appetite, insomnia, sexual dysfunction, urinary hesitancy or retention, and tachycardia. In adult CYP2D6 poor metabolizers, selected adverse effects were more frequent than in other metabolizer types, including dry mouth 35% versus 17%, decreased appetite 23% versus 15%, erectile dysfunction 21% versus 9%, and insomnia 19% versus 11%. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Short-term ADHD trials found potentially clinically important changes—heart rate increase ≥20 beats/minute, systolic blood-pressure increase ≥20 mm Hg, or diastolic increase ≥15 mm Hg—in approximately 5–10% of pediatric and adult participants across the clinical-trial program. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Outcomes:**

In an integrated adult analysis, mean CAARS change favored atomoxetine over placebo by −12.2 versus −8.1 in short-term studies and −13.2 versus −9.7 after six months. A response definition requiring at least 30% CAARS improvement plus CGI-ADHD severity ≤3 was met by 34.8% versus 22.3% short-term and 43.4% versus 28.0% at six months. [S3] ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25035246/))

A separate pooled analysis found effect sizes of approximately 0.28 at week 1, 0.45 at week 4, and 0.52 at week 26; the 80-mg group reached an estimated effect size of 0.82 at six months. These are group averages and do not imply that every responder follows the same trajectory. [S4] ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26922462/?utm_source=openai))

In pediatric relapse-prevention studies, patients who continued atomoxetine after an open-label response had significantly longer time to relapse than patients switched to placebo, both after the initial maintenance phase and after approximately eight months of continued treatment. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

Evidence is strongest for clinician- or patient-rated ADHD symptoms. It is weaker for durable academic, occupational, driving, or other long-term functional outcomes, and healthy-volunteer studies do not establish benefit in people without ADHD. [S2, S3, S7, S8] ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6109107/?utm_source=openai))

**Mechanisms:**

**Established pharmacology:** atomoxetine blocks the norepinephrine transporter and increases noradrenergic signaling. Its principal active metabolite, 4-hydroxyatomoxetine, also inhibits the transporter. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Conservative interpretation:** norepinephrine-transporter blockade can alter prefrontal catecholamine signaling, and the healthy-volunteer stop-signal study is consistent with a role for noradrenergic modulation in inhibitory control. However, clinical ADHD response is not reducible to one neurotransmitter, brain region, or “dopamine boost,” and healthy studies show that effects depend on task and context. [S7, S8] ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1867315/?utm_source=openai))

The mechanism also does not imply immediate therapeutic onset. Drug exposure changes rapidly, while symptom improvement reflects downstream clinical adaptation and is observed over a longer trajectory in ADHD trials. [S1, S4] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Cautions:**

- **Suicidal ideation in pediatric patients:** pooled short-term studies reported suicidal ideation in 5 of 1,357 atomoxetine-treated pediatric patients, or 0.4%, versus 0 of 851 placebo-treated patients. One case involved a suicide attempt; no suicides occurred. All reported ideation occurred in patients aged 6–12 years and during the first treatment month. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
- **Severe liver injury:** rare postmarketing cases include liver failure and one transplant. Most reported cases began within 120 days; some involved aminotransferases above 20 times the upper limit of normal and jaundice. The label specifies discontinuation and no rechallenge when jaundice or laboratory evidence of liver injury occurs. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
- **Cardiovascular disease:** the drug is contraindicated in severe cardiac or vascular disorders expected to deteriorate with clinically important increases in heart rate or blood pressure. The label describes cardiovascular history and examination before treatment, periodic heart-rate and blood-pressure measurement, and prompt cardiac evaluation for exertional chest pain or unexplained syncope. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
- **Psychiatric activation:** new psychotic or manic symptoms, mania activation, aggression, or hostility can occur; the label calls for screening for bipolar risk factors before initiation. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
- **Contraindications:** MAOI use or use within the preceding 14 days, narrow-angle glaucoma, pheochromocytoma or prior pheochromocytoma, severe cardiovascular disorders, and known hypersensitivity. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
- **Other recognized risks:** urinary retention or hesitancy, priapism, growth or weight effects in pediatric patients, orthostatic hypotension, syncope, and possible small concentration-related QTc changes. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))

**Claims:**

| Claim | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| “Atomoxetine is a stimulant.” | False or imprecise. It is labeled as a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor; it is not a controlled substance, and an abuse-potential study found no stimulant or euphoriant properties. [S1] |
| “A 1-hour plasma peak means it works in 1 hour.” | Misleading. Peak concentration and clinical onset are different; controlled adult data show symptom effects emerging over weeks and increasing over months. [S1, S4] |
| “Atomoxetine is a general cognitive enhancer.” | Not established. Healthy studies show selective laboratory effects, including null findings, and the FDA indication is ADHD rather than general cognitive enhancement. [S1, S7, S8] |
| “More dose always means more benefit.” | Not supported. The label reports no additional pediatric benefit above 1.2 mg/kg/day, and a 2026 dose-effect network meta-analysis found no dose-effect pattern for atomoxetine in fixed-dose studies. [S1, S9] |
| “CYP2D6 status does not matter.” | False. Poor metabolizers and strong CYP2D6 inhibition substantially increase atomoxetine exposure and can increase adverse effects. [S1] |
| “MAOIs can be combined with atomoxetine.” | False. Concurrent use and use within 14 days of stopping either drug are contraindicated by the U.S. label. [S1] |
| “No taper means no discontinuation effects are possible.” | Overstated. The label requires no taper and found no evidence of a withdrawal syndrome or symptom rebound in its discontinuation data; this does not prove that every individual experiences no change after stopping. [S1] |

**Interactions:**

| Interacting exposure | Label-described issue |
|---|---|
| MAOIs | Contraindicated; at least 14 days must separate stopping an MAOI from starting atomoxetine, and stopping atomoxetine from starting an MAOI. [S1] |
| Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors, including paroxetine, fluoxetine, and quinidine | Increase atomoxetine exposure; the label uses a four-week titration interval in this setting. [S1] |
| Antihypertensive drugs | May require more frequent blood-pressure monitoring and dosage adjustment because atomoxetine can raise heart rate or blood pressure. [S1] |
| Systemically administered albuterol or other beta2 agonists | Cardiovascular effects, particularly heart rate and blood pressure increases, may be potentiated. [S1] |
| Methylphenidate | In one pharmacodynamic study, combined use did not increase cardiovascular effects beyond methylphenidate alone; this was not a general combination-safety finding. [S1] |
| CYP enzymes | The label found no clinically important inhibition or induction of CYP1A2, CYP3A, CYP2D6, or CYP2C9 by atomoxetine. [S1] |

**Legal:**

In the United States, atomoxetine is prescription-only, FDA-labeled for ADHD in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older, and not a controlled substance. The current Strattera label was revised in June 2026. Legal classification and labeling outside the United States are not assessed here. [S1] ([accessdata.fda.gov](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/021411s053lbl.pdf))
