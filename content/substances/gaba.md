## Summary

Oral GABA is rapidly absorbed into plasma, but direct human evidence that supplemental GABA enters the brain is lacking. Small controlled trials report mixed, formulation-specific effects on stress, sleep, mood, and cognition, with several positive studies commercially funded.

## Description

Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA; 4-aminobutanoic acid) is an endogenous inhibitory neurotransmitter and a non-proteinogenic amino acid also consumed in foods and supplements. This page concerns oral GABA itself, not phenibut, gabapentin, pregabalin, or GABA-receptor drugs. Human pharmacokinetic studies confirm systemic absorption, usually peaking near 30 minutes, but do not establish an elimination half-life or brain exposure.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: GABA-A receptor signaling
  description: GABA is an endogenous agonist at GABA-A receptors, pentameric ligand-gated anion channels. This receptor pharmacology does not establish central receptor engagement after oral supplementation.
  sourceId: iuphar-gabaa
  conceptId: gaba-a-receptor
- title: GABA-B receptor signaling
  description: GABA is an agonist at the metabotropic GABA-B receptor. Receptor pharmacology is distinct from evidence that orally administered GABA reaches human brain tissue.
  sourceId: iuphar-gabab
  conceptId: gaba-b-receptor
- title: Endogenous synthesis from glutamate
  description: Glutamate decarboxylase uses pyridoxal phosphate to catalyze decarboxylation of L-glutamate to GABA and carbon dioxide.
  sourceId: uniprot-gad1
  conceptId: glutamate-decarboxylase
- title: GAT1-mediated transport
  description: SLC6A1 encodes the sodium- and chloride-dependent GABA transporter GAT1, a major mechanism for cellular GABA uptake and synaptic clearance.
  sourceId: uniprot-gat1
  conceptId: gaba-transporter-1
- title: GABA-shunt metabolism
  description: GABA is transaminated by GABA transaminase to succinate semialdehyde, which succinate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase oxidizes to succinate.
  sourceId: uniprot-aldh5a1
  conceptId: gaba-transaminase
- title: GABAergic modulation of action cascading
  description: In a double-blind randomized study, 800 mg synthetic GABA was reported to speed stop-change responses and shorten stop-signal reaction time without changing simple GO responses, consistent with a possible role of GABAergic signaling in action selection and inhibition.
  sourceId: pubmed-26227783
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: High-dose acute tolerability
  description: Historical human studies reported transient throat burning, breathlessness, lethargy, leg weakness and modest blood-pressure reductions at high gram doses; these data do not establish opioid-like respiratory depression from ordinary oral GABA.
  sourceId: usp-2021
- title: Controlled-release liver signal
  description: In a six-month type-1-diabetes trial, nausea, fatigue, dizziness and AST elevations occurred; two participants discontinued for transaminase abnormalities and one had an approximately seven-fold idiosyncratic AST/ALT elevation that resolved after withdrawal.
  sourceId: gaba-liver-2025
- title: Pregnancy and organ-impairment gaps
  description: Adequate pregnancy/lactation studies and dedicated renal/hepatic impairment PK or dose-adjustment trials were not identified. Normal short-trial laboratory results do not establish safety in organ disease.
  sourceId: usp-2021
- title: Mixture pharmacovigilance limitation
  description: Most adverse-event reports mentioning GABA involved multi-ingredient products, preventing clean attribution to GABA alone.
  sourceId: usp-2021
- title: Open-label endocrine PK/PD study
  description: The 2 g single/repeated-dose study was open-label, self-controlled and limited to 12 healthy volunteers; endocrine changes and lower glycated albumin do not establish diabetes treatment benefit.
  sourceId: pubmed-26617516
- title: Dose- and duration-related transient adverse effects
  description: Repeated 2 g three-times-daily dosing produced more minor events (including sore throat, throat burning, headache and dizziness) than single dosing; four men had transient ALT/AST elevations that normalized without intervention.
  sourceId: pubmed-26617516
- title: Retracted action-selection publication
  description: PubMed metadata labels PMID 26227783 as a Retracted Publication. Its reported 800 mg action-selection effects should be treated as unreliable until the retraction basis is resolved.
  sourceId: pubmed-26227783
```
