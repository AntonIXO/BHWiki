## Summary

ABT-089 is an experimental nicotinic acetylcholine receptor compound studied for attention and cognition.

## Description

The cited human studies tested oral ABT-089 at 40–80 mg/day in adults and 0.085–1.4 mg/kg/day in children for 6–8 weeks. Animal studies used acute or repeated dosing; those findings do not establish human smoking-cessation efficacy.

## Evidence note

Study findings and methods are linked to their original publications.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: α4β2 partial agonism
  description: ABT-089 is a selective α4β2 neuronal nicotinic receptor partial agonist; pediatric full text reports high α4β2 affinity and weaker activity at other subtypes.
  sourceId: pubmed-21156272
- title: β2-containing receptor regulation
  description: In nicotine-withdrawn mice, ABT-089 reduced anxiety without upregulating β2-containing nicotinic receptors, unlike nicotine.
  sourceId: pubmed-24627467
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: No ADHD efficacy in cited trials
  description: Adult pilot and two pediatric randomized trials found no significant primary ADHD symptom benefit versus placebo, despite tolerability.
  sourceId: pubmed-22795204
- title: Animal relapse model
  description: Nicotine reinstatement and withdrawal-anxiety results come from rodents and cannot establish human cessation benefit.
  sourceId: pubmed-25128791
- title: State-dependent anxiety
  description: ABT-089 reduced withdrawal anxiety but increased anxiety-like behavior in nicotine-naive mice.
  sourceId: pubmed-24627467
```
