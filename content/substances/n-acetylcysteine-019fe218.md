## Summary

NAC supplies a cysteine precursor for glutathione synthesis and is used medically as a mucolytic and acetaminophen antidote. Oral trials show condition-specific benefits and important null findings; routine supplementation for healthy aging or cognitive enhancement remains unproven ([13](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ImmuneFunction-HealthProfessional/); [23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39349461/); [65](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42853821/)).

## Description

This page focuses on **oral NAC in adults**. COPD trials show heterogeneous effects on exacerbations, with positive PANTHEON findings alongside negative BRONCUS and 2024 coprimary outcomes. Small trials support symptom improvement in hair-pulling and skin-picking disorders, while OCD, addiction, mood, and metabolic studies give mixed or negative results. Biomarker changes do not establish disease prevention, and GlyNAC is a separate combination intervention ([22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24621680/); [20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15866309/); [23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39349461/); [25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19581567/); [27](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27007062/); [28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35304155/); [31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28623823/); [48](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41431629/); [60](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35821844/)).

## Evidence note

The supplied review (literature cutoff 13 November 2026) was checked against publication metadata, abstracts, selected full texts, and official sources. Reviews overlap and are not independent replications. Long-term healthy-adult outcomes, multi-year high-dose safety, and formulation superiority remain unresolved; inaccessible declarations stay unknown. No single subjective onset or duration is established. A small 2026 OCD pilot contains an internally inconsistent response statistic, which is omitted here ([65](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42853821/); [30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42260157/); [19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41138059/)).

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Cysteine supply for glutathione synthesis
  description: NAC supplies cysteine equivalents for glutathione synthesis. A rise in glutathione depends on baseline status and biological context; it is not proof of longevity or disease prevention.
  sourceId: nih-ods-nac
  conceptId: glutathione-precursor
- title: Mucus disulfide chemistry
  description: The thiol group can reduce disulfide bonds in mucus proteins. Direct mucolytic chemistry and the magnitude of chronic oral COPD outcomes are separate questions.
  sourceId: publication-link-a3cf12d82beb
  conceptId: mucolytic-disulfide-reduction
- title: Glutamate modulation remains a mechanistic hypothesis
  description: Cystine–glutamate exchange motivates psychiatric research; a small MRS crossover study changed regional glutamate but cannot establish cessation or psychiatric efficacy.
  sourceId: pubmed-22549117
  conceptId: glutamate
- title: CSF exposure does not establish brain benefit
  description: In 12 Parkinson patients, two days of oral NAC increased CSF NAC dose-dependently; 70 mg/kg per dose produced about 9.26 μM, with no acute motor or cognitive improvement.
  sourceId: pubmed-25765302
- title: "Exercise redox signaling: intravenous evidence"
  description: IV NAC attenuated selected early exercise-responsive signaling. This cannot be translated into established loss of training adaptation from ordinary oral doses.
  sourceId: pubmed-21827635
- title: Animal traumatic brain injury models
  description: Early NAC improved behavioral recovery in mouse and rat injury models; this does not establish efficacy in healthy adults.
  sourceId: pubmed-24740427
- title: Ethanol-fed rat model
  description: A 1.7 g/kg/day rat experiment altered oxidative stress and mitochondrial-biogenesis responses without preventing all mitochondrial damage. Its dose is excluded from adult oral exposures.
  sourceId: pubmed-25581647
- title: Combined antioxidants in a rat blast-injury model
  description: Rat blast-injury work combined NAC with another antioxidant; its biomarker findings cannot establish oral NAC-alone efficacy.
  sourceId: pubmed-24224042
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Oral gastrointestinal intolerance
  description: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dyspepsia, and abdominal discomfort are recognized oral adverse effects. The 2–4 g/day OCD trial chiefly reported mild gastrointestinal events.
  sourceId: pubmed-35304155
- title: Route-specific acute reactions
  description: Anaphylactoid reactions, bronchospasm, and marked hypotension are especially relevant to IV antidote regimens. Their incidence must not be transferred to oral supplement use.
  sourceId: nih-ods-nac
- title: Longer-term evidence has a defined population
  description: COPD trials extend one to three years at 600–1200 mg/day. They do not establish comparable multi-year safety of 2–4 g/day in healthy adults.
  sourceId: pubmed-15866309
- title: Two-year COPD safety observations
  description: Adverse events occurred in 25.2% versus 21.1%, and serious events in 8.6% versus 6.7%; differences were not statistically significant. This does not exclude rare harms.
  sourceId: pubmed-39349461
- title: Medical antidote treatment is a separate use
  description: Acetylcysteine treats acetaminophen poisoning under medical protocols. Supplement-style exposures and outcome data cannot substitute for time-sensitive poisoning management.
  sourceId: nih-ods-nac
- title: Cancer findings do not establish prevention or carcinogenicity
  description: EUROSCAN did not improve survival or second-primary-tumor outcomes. Its cancer-survivor population and duration cannot establish safety or prevention for every setting.
  sourceId: pubmed-10861309
- title: Exercise adaptation remains uncertain
  description: Acute performance and recovery findings are context-dependent; chronic oral NAC has not been shown to universally improve or impair training adaptation.
  sourceId: pubmed-42072098
- title: Mineral effects are preclinical
  description: Copper/zinc findings in cells and mice do not establish clinically important deficiency from ordinary oral NAC in humans.
  sourceId: pubmed-33198336
```
