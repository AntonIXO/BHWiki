## Summary

Astaxanthin is a xanthophyll carotenoid with formulation-sensitive human pharmacokinetics. Clinical findings differ by skin, visual, exercise and metabolic endpoint; antioxidant biomarkers do not establish a general clinical benefit.

## Description

Human studies use free or esterified astaxanthin, algal extracts, oils and proprietary mixtures. Formulation, meal context and isomer composition affect exposure. Clinical findings include positive and null results, with small samples and combination-product attribution limits.

## Evidence note

Selected primary studies and reviews, not an independently reviewed systematic assessment. Several trials involve manufacturers; declarations remain attached to each reference. Plasma exposure, clinical outcomes and felt-effect timing are distinct, and unknown molecular identity or disclosures remain unassessed.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Formulation-sensitive intestinal absorption and lipoprotein transport
  description: Human pharmacokinetic studies demonstrate that astaxanthin exposure depends strongly on carrier formulation and meal context. After absorption, circulating astaxanthin is distributed among chylomicron/VLDL, LDL and HDL fractions. These pharmacokinetic observations do not themselves demonstrate a clinical benefit.
  sourceId: ref-odeberg-2003
  conceptId: astaxanthin-oral-absorption
- title: Hydrolysis of fatty-acyl astaxanthin esters during absorption
  description: In a three-person human pharmacokinetic study of astaxanthin fatty-acyl diesters, unesterified astaxanthin appeared in plasma while intact esters were not detected, supporting ester hydrolysis before or during systemic uptake. Optical and geometric isomer-selective uptake was also observed.
  sourceId: ref-coral-2004
  conceptId: astaxanthin-oral-absorption
- title: Human oxidative-stress biomarker modulation is context-dependent
  description: Some randomized human studies report lower malondialdehyde or isoprostane and higher total antioxidant capacity, whereas trained-cyclist and exercise-recovery trials report null oxidative-stress outcomes. This supports context-dependent biomarker effects rather than a universal clinical antioxidant outcome.
  sourceId: ref-choi-2011
- title: Exploratory exercise immune-proteome response
  description: In trained runners, 8 mg/day did not modify soreness, CK, conventional cytokines or 42 exercise-responsive oxylipins but was associated with differential recovery of numerous immune-related plasma proteins. This is exploratory molecular evidence and not a validated clinical recovery mechanism.
  sourceId: ref-nieman-2023
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Regulatory food or supplement status is not therapeutic approval
  description: Novel-food or dietary-supplement market pathways address ingredient safety and conditions of use. They do not establish astaxanthin as an approved treatment for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline, eye disease, photoaging or exercise-performance enhancement.
  sourceId: ref-eu-2023
- title: High-dose pharmacokinetic studies do not establish chronic high-dose safety
  description: Human single-dose pharmacokinetic studies have used 40-100 mg and one very small exercise study used 28 mg/day for four days. These exposures cannot establish long-term safety at the same doses.
  sourceId: ref-odeberg-2003
- title: Pregnancy and lactation evidence is inadequate
  description: Adequate controlled human pregnancy or lactation safety data were not identified in the reviewed evidence. Lack of a demonstrated adverse signal should not be interpreted as established safety.
  sourceId: ref-efsa-2020
- title: Clinically characterized drug-interaction evidence is sparse
  description: No specific clinically validated astaxanthin drug-drug interaction was identified in the reviewed human evidence. Mechanism-only interaction claims were not promoted to structured interaction records.
  sourceId: ref-efsa-2020
- title: Formulations are not pharmacokinetically interchangeable
  description: Carrier lipid, emulsifier or dispersant, esterification state, geometric and optical isomer distribution, meal timing and dose can materially change exposure. Cross-trial milligram-dose comparisons should therefore preserve formulation context.
  sourceId: ref-odeberg-2003
- title: Biomarkers are not clinical outcomes
  description: Changes in creatine kinase, LDH, malondialdehyde, cytokines, adiponectin, fibrinogen or other biomarkers should not be represented as proof of symptom improvement, disease prevention or improved athletic performance unless corresponding clinical endpoints also improve.
  sourceId: ref-exercise-meta-2026
- title: Pediatric evidence is limited
  description: The identified digital-eye-strain evidence in children is based primarily on one 64-participant randomized trial. It does not establish broad pediatric efficacy or safety across ages and indications.
  sourceId: ref-hecht-2025
```
