# Noopept (omberacetam; GVS-111) — BHWiki Deep Research

Slug: noopept
Evidence cutoff: October 10, 2026
Scope: human primary studies, systematic-review/meta-analysis searches, official/current Russian regulatory or product-label sources, clinical-trial registries, and analytical/pharmacovigilance-style product surveillance. Animal and cellular findings are discussed only to prevent them from being misrepresented as human evidence.

## Evidence summary

Identity correction: the marketed Russian active ingredient should not be described as a racemate. Noopept/omberacetam/GVS-111 is conventionally identified as N-phenylacetyl-L-prolylglycine ethyl ester; the L stereochemistry is part of the drug identity. The current Russian product is omberacetam 10 mg oral tablets, registration ЛП-N(004920)-(РГ-RU) dated March 20, 2024. The current holder information lists omberacetam 10 mg/tablet and indications for adult cognitive/emotional-lability symptoms associated with traumatic brain injury/post-concussion states, cerebrovascular insufficiency/encephalopathies, asthenic disorders, and related reductions in intellectual productivity. [R1]

The human efficacy record is small and methodologically weak by modern standards. Short-term cognitive or symptom improvement has been reported in vascular/post-stroke cognitive impairment and in vascular/post-traumatic organic brain disorders, but much of the literature is Russian, small, open-label or active-comparator rather than blinded placebo-controlled, and incompletely reports allocation concealment, masking, attrition, prespecified outcomes, funding, and conflicts. The 2008 Russian and 2009 English Neznamov–Teleshova publications represent the same or substantially overlapping study rather than independent replication. [R2][R3]

A more recent 2022 randomized three-active-arm study in 150 patients with non-demented vascular cognitive impairment used omberacetam 10 mg twice daily for 45 days. MoCA increased during treatment, but at 4.5 months after the course the reported change from baseline was only +0.8 points and not statistically significant, p=0.061. The study was explicitly sponsored by OTISIFARM, the Noopept marketing authorization holder, and its disclosure states that the conflict of interest did not affect the results rather than declaring an absence of conflict. [R4]

Evidence for healthy-person cognitive enhancement/biohacking is insufficient. The directly relevant PubMed-indexed healthy-volunteer study exposed young adults to cold, heat, and hypobaric hypoxia and assessed “meteoadaptation”/functional state; it was not a contemporary placebo-controlled trial of memory, attention, executive function, or academic/work performance under ordinary conditions. A study of older teachers was confounded by concomitant head-and-neck self-massage. [R5][R6]

A PubMed search restricted to systematic reviews/meta-analyses of Noopept/omberacetam/GVS-111 returned zero compound-specific records. ClinicalTrials.gov searches on October 10, 2026 likewise returned zero records under “Noopept,” “omberacetam,” and a query covering “GVS-111” or the full chemical name. This establishes absence from ClinicalTrials.gov under those searched aliases, not that no study has ever been registered in any national or historical registry.

BHWiki evidence judgment: short-term symptomatic/cognitive benefit in selected vascular/post-traumatic populations is low-certainty; durable benefit is very-low-certainty/uncertain; ordinary healthy-person cognitive enhancement is unsupported by adequate clinical trials. [R2][R7][R8]

## Identity, formulations, pharmacokinetics, and metabolites

| Entity | What the human/regulatory evidence supports |
|---|---|
| Omberacetam / Noopept / GVS-111 | The registered Russian drug/API. Current product: 10 mg oral tablet. [R1] |
| Cyclo-L-prolylglycine / cycloprolylglycine / CPG | A distinct cyclic dipeptide proposed in product information and preclinical work as a metabolite/mediator. It is not the marketed drug. Importantly, the published comparative PK study reported that no Noopept metabolites were detected in human plasma, so CPG should not be presented as a demonstrated circulating human active metabolite on the available direct human evidence. [R9] |
| Phenylacetic acid / phenylacetylproline | Listed as metabolites in Russian product information, but direct quantitative human exposure was not established in the human PK publication retrieved. [R9] |
| Noopept “salts” | No regulator-approved salt formulation was identified in the current Russian record reviewed. A vendor-described salt should not be assumed pharmacologically or pharmaceutically equivalent to registered omberacetam tablets. [R1] |
| Intranasal sprays, research powders, custom capsules, bulk material | Not interchangeable evidence-wise with the registered 10 mg tablet. No included human efficacy or human PK study validated these formulations. Market-surveillance evidence documents Noopept in unauthorized/bulk-market channels. [R10] |

The direct human PK evidence is unusually thin. Boĭko and colleagues reported slower elimination in humans than in rats/rabbits, substantial human interindividual variability, and no detectable drug metabolites in human blood plasma, which they attributed to the low clinical dose and low concentrations. The PubMed abstract does not provide a sufficient modern concentration-time dataset to establish full human Cmax, AUC, metabolic fractions, renal clearance, food effect, or dose proportionality. [R9]

Current product information describes rapid absorption and gives values including approximately 15-minute time to peak concentration, half-life around 0.38 h, high relative bioavailability, and conversion to phenylacetic acid, phenylacetylproline, and cycloprolylglycine. Those label statements should not automatically be converted into fully validated human PK parameters, because the publicly retrieved label does not transparently expose the underlying species/study data for every parameter and the published human PK paper did not detect metabolites in plasma. [R11][R9]

Mechanistic claims require a strict evidence boundary. NGF/BDNF induction, HIF-1 activation, cholinergic/glutamatergic receptor effects, antioxidant pathways, and CPG-mediated receptor hypotheses are preclinical/mechanistic propositions, not demonstrated mechanisms of clinical benefit in humans. The human efficacy trials retrieved did not measure NGF or BDNF induction, HIF-1 target engagement, receptor occupancy, or a CPG exposure-response relationship. Current product information itself mixes clinical-use language with mechanisms derived substantially from experimental models, so these mechanisms should be stored in a separate BHWiki preclinical field rather than used to grade human efficacy. [R11]

## Human clinical evidence

| Population | Design and formulation/dose | Human result | Main limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vascular and post-traumatic mild/moderate cognitive impairment — Neznamov & Teleshova | Comparative study; Noopept 20 mg/day versus piracetam 1,200 mg/day, approximately eight weeks in the reported program. | Cognitive/clinical measures and neurosis-like symptoms were reported to improve. Noopept was presented as comparable or favorable to piracetam on several measures. [R2] | Active comparator rather than placebo; small sample; masking insufficiently clear; Russian 2008 and English 2009 reports should not be double-counted. [R3] |
| Post-stroke cognitive impairment — Amelin et al. | Open prospective study, 60 stroke patients; abstract gives 20 mg/day for two months. | MMSE and verbal-association measures reportedly improved versus controls after two months; global assessment described mild improvement. [R7] | Open-label/high risk of bias. The abstract is internally inconsistent, first saying patients were treated for 12 months but later specifying a two-month 20 mg/day course. [R7] |
| Non-demented vascular cognitive impairment — Dadasheva et al. | 150 participants randomized among three active treatments: omberacetam 10 mg twice daily, piracetam/cinnarizine 400/25 mg three times daily, or aminophenylbutyric acid 250 mg three times daily; 45 days. | MoCA reportedly rose from 19.8 to 23.3 with omberacetam during treatment. At 4.5 months after treatment, change from baseline was about +0.8 and p=0.061, failing conventional statistical significance. [R4] | No placebo; commercial sponsorship by OTISIFARM; long-term persistence not statistically established. [R12] |
| Vascular/post-traumatic cognitive disorder with EEG assessment — Bochkarev et al. | Clinical study; dose not exposed in the PubMed abstract. | Alpha/beta power increased and delta power decreased; authors interpreted the pattern as nonspecific activation/anxiolysis, with stronger EEG effects in vascular than post-traumatic disease. [R8] | EEG is a surrogate, not evidence of patient-important cognitive benefit; investigators/population overlap with the broader development program limits independence. |
| Dyscirculatory encephalopathy — Barantsevich et al. | Comparative/evaluation study; detailed dose/allocation not available in the PubMed record retrieved. | Indexed as an efficacy study involving vascular cognitive disease/quality-of-life outcomes. [R13] | Reporting available through PubMed is too sparse for reliable effect-size or risk-of-bias assessment. |
| Adjunct during tuberculosis chemotherapy — Mordyk et al. | 60 newly diagnosed pulmonary-TB patients; treatment group n=30 received 10 mg twice daily for one month. | Reported less anxiety and fewer neuro-/cardiotoxic reactions to antituberculous therapy. [R14] | Small study outside the core cognitive indication; methodological detail limited. |
| Neurasthenia — Chutko et al. | Comparative study; exact dose unavailable in the PubMed record. | Published as a therapeutic study of Noopept in neurasthenia. [R15] | Insufficient accessible reporting to establish a psychiatric indication. |
| Healthy young volunteers under environmental stress — Shabanov et al. | Controlled trial, age 20–24; cold, heat and hypobaric-hypoxia testing in a thermobarocomplex. | Authors reported improvement in a psychological functional-state component under cold/heat and increased physical work capacity in heat. [R5] | Not a conventional cognition-enhancement trial; multiple agents/conditions; dose and sample size are not given in the PubMed abstract. |
| Older teachers — Lysenko et al. | Noopept plus a two-week course of head/neck self-massage compared with Noopept alone. | Combination reportedly improved functional/professional-performance measures more than drug alone; effects diminished toward baseline by month three. [R6] | The intervention is intrinsically confounded and cannot establish a standalone Noopept effect. |

Accordingly, claims that Noopept reliably improves memory, focus, learning, IQ, productivity, or executive function in healthy users exceed the human evidence. The evidence retrieved also does not establish Noopept as a treatment or preventive therapy for Alzheimer disease, Parkinson disease, ADHD, major depression, or other neurodegenerative/psychiatric conditions merely because related animal/cell models or molecular mechanisms have been published. [R5][R6]

## Safety, dependence, interactions, and special populations

The current Russian product information lists allergic reactions and possible increases in blood pressure in patients with hypertension, particularly severe hypertension. The comparative vascular/post-traumatic development program also reported sleep disturbance, irritability, and increased blood pressure during treatment. These trials are far too small to estimate uncommon or delayed adverse events reliably. [R11][R2]

For psychiatric and neurologic safety, the direct human record is inadequate to quantify risks of mania/hypomania, psychosis, suicidality, seizure provocation, movement disorders, or persistent sleep disturbance. Irritability and sleep disturbance provide a limited human signal, but neither small positive trials nor absence of published case series can establish the absence of rarer hazards. [R2]

For dependence and withdrawal, current product information states that a withdrawal syndrome is not observed after stopping treatment. That is a regulatory/product-label statement, not the equivalent of a dedicated human abuse-liability, tolerance, physical-dependence, or controlled withdrawal study; none was identified in the allowed evidence set. The presence of Noopept in illicit “smart-drug” commerce and in bulk-material interceptions establishes a nonmedical-use market, not pharmacologic dependence. [R11][R10]

For renal and hepatic impairment, current Russian instructions contraindicate use in severe liver and/or kidney dysfunction. No dedicated human renal-impairment or hepatic-impairment PK study, validated dose-adjustment algorithm, dialysis study, or quantitative organ-impairment exposure analysis was identified. [R11]

For pregnancy and lactation, pregnancy and breastfeeding are contraindications in the current Russian instruction. No prospective human pregnancy cohort, controlled reproductive study, placental-transfer study, breast-milk PK study, or infant-exposure dataset was identified in the permitted evidence base. [R11]

For drug interactions, Russian product information has stated that interactions were not established with ethanol, hypnotics, antihypertensive agents, or psychostimulants. “Not established” must not be rendered as “proven safe to combine”: no modern controlled human drug-drug-interaction program was identified. Likewise, absence of a characterized overdose syndrome does not establish a toxic threshold or a wide overdose safety margin. [R11]

The labeled adult regimen begins at 20 mg/day, given as 10 mg in the morning and 10 mg during the day; with inadequate efficacy and good tolerability it may be increased on medical advice to 30 mg/day in three 10 mg doses. Current instructions advise against administration after 18:00. These doses apply to the registered 10 mg oral tablet, not automatically to bulk powder, nasal products, salts, or online capsules of uncertain composition. [R11]

## Regulation, registries, and product quality

Russia. The current GRLS/PharmPortal record identifies the marketed medicine as Noopept/omberacetam, registration ЛП-N(004920)-(РГ-RU), with current product information specifying a 10 mg tablet; OTISIFARM is the marketing authorization holder. [R1]

United States. In a U.S. analytical study of cognitive-enhancement supplements, Cohen and colleagues treated omberacetam as an unapproved drug and found it in all ten purchased products selected for analysis. Recommended servings delivered measured omberacetam quantities as high as about 40.6 mg; 75% of quantified declared drug amounts across the products were inaccurate, and some products contained undeclared pharmaceuticals or omitted declared ones. Thus, “Noopept” on an online supplement label does not establish identity, dose accuracy, single-ingredient composition, or equivalence to the Russian pharmaceutical product. [R16][R17]

Europe/Australia surveillance. A 2025 coordinated study involving official medicines-control laboratories documented 159 samples and 166 molecular identifications involving 34 compounds collected from January 2020 through September 2024. Most samples were presented as dietary supplements or medicines; 69% came from the illegal market, and Noopept, phenylpiracetam, and phenibut were among substances intercepted as large quantities of raw material. The authors caution that surveillance probably captured only a fraction of the market. [R18]

ClinicalTrials.gov. The following searches each returned zero records on October 10, 2026:
