## Identity, approval, and exact dosing

Armodafinil is R-modafinil, the isolated R-enantiomer of racemic modafinil. It is not milligram-for-milligram equivalent to the racemate: the S-enantiomer in modafinil is cleared much more rapidly, with an elimination half-life of about 4 hours versus about 15 hours for R-modafinil. The U.S. NUVIGIL label approves armodafinil to improve wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea, or shift-work disorder. In OSA it treats residual sleepiness rather than airway obstruction; adequate CPAP or other primary OSA treatment remains necessary. [R1]

| Indication | Exact U.S.-labeled dose | Evidence-qualified note |
|---|---|---|
| Narcolepsy | 150–250 mg orally once each morning | Direct placebo-controlled Phase 3 efficacy evidence exists at both doses. [R2] |
| Residual sleepiness in OSA | 150–250 mg orally once each morning | Although 250 mg/day is tolerated, the label states there is no consistent additional benefit over 150 mg/day. [R1] |
| Shift-work disorder | 150 mg orally once daily about 1 h before the work shift | Pivotal trial used 150 mg 30–60 min before the night shift. [R3] |

NUVIGIL is Schedule IV in the United States. Approved indications do not include general cognitive enhancement, ordinary sleep-deprivation treatment, or replacement of adequate sleep. Postmarketing misuse, diversion, tolerance, physical dependence, and withdrawal have been reported. [R1]

## Patient outcomes

### Narcolepsy

The pivotal 12-week randomized trial enrolled 196 adults and compared armodafinil 150 mg/day, 250 mg/day, and placebo. Morning/early-afternoon Maintenance of Wakefulness Test sleep latency changed +1.3 min with 150 mg, +2.6 min with 250 mg, and −1.9 min with placebo. At least minimal CGI-C improvement occurred in 69%, 73%, and 33%, respectively. Late-day wakefulness favored armodafinil, as did selected memory, attention, and fatigue measures; principal adverse events were headache, nausea, and dizziness. [R2]

Registry NCT00078377 identifies Cephalon as sponsor and confirms the 196-participant 150/250 mg/placebo design. [R3] This is direct armodafinil efficacy evidence, but the pivotal development program was manufacturer-sponsored.

### Residual excessive sleepiness in OSA

In a 12-week randomized multicenter trial of 395 CPAP-adherent adults with residual sleepiness, armodafinil 150 or 250 mg/day produced pooled mean MWT change of approximately +1.9 min versus −1.7 min placebo. CGI-C improvement occurred in 72% versus 37%; ESS changed −5.5 versus −3.3 points, and fatigue improved. Nighttime polysomnography and CPAP use were not worsened. [R4][R5]

Another 12-week randomized OSA study of armodafinil 150 mg found MWT improvement +2.3 versus −1.3 min, CGI-C response 71% versus 53%, and statistically significant improvement in episodic secondary memory. [R6]

Results were not uniformly positive across subgroups. In treated OSA with comorbid depression, armodafinil 200 mg improved CGI-C and ESS but objective MWT was not significant: mean increase 2.6 min versus 1.1 min placebo, P=.30. [R7]

A 2023 network meta-analysis of 14 randomized trials and 3,085 participants pooled armodafinil and racemic modafinil, so estimates are not armodafinil-specific. At about four weeks, pooled class effects improved ESS by 2.25 points and MWT; adverse-event discontinuation was probably increased, RR 2.01 (95% CI 1.14–3.51). The review reported no primary funding source. [R8]

### Shift-work disorder

In the pivotal 12-week randomized SWD trial, 254 night workers received armodafinil 150 mg or placebo before each night shift. Mean MSLT sleep latency changed from 2.3 to 5.3 min with armodafinil versus 2.4 to 2.8 min placebo. CGI-C improvement occurred in 79% versus 59%. Diary-rated sleepiness improved during laboratory night shifts, real shifts, and the commute home; selected memory and attention measures improved. Daytime sleep was not measurably worsened by polysomnography. [R9]

A Cochrane review found two armodafinil SWD trials totaling 572 participants. Armodafinil reduced Karolinska Sleepiness Scale ratings by about 0.99 points and improved simple reaction time by about 50 ms; evidence quality was moderate. Headache, nausea, and blood-pressure increases were reported. There were no trials in ordinary shift workers without diagnosed SWD. [R10]

## Healthy volunteers, cognition, and sleep deprivation

The strongest direct healthy-volunteer armodafinil experiment studied acute sleep loss, not rested cognitive enhancement. In 107 healthy men randomized to armodafinil 100, 150, 200, or 300 mg, racemic modafinil 200 mg, or placebo during enforced overnight wakefulness, all armodafinil doses improved objective wakefulness and psychomotor vigilance versus placebo. At the same nominal 200 mg, armodafinil produced more sustained late plasma exposure than racemic modafinil, consistent with removal of the rapidly cleared S-enantiomer. Reported adverse physiological findings included tachycardia/palpitations, increased mean blood pressure, and reduced subsequent sleep efficiency. [R11]

This supports acute vigilance/wakefulness under experimental sleep deprivation. It does not establish improvements in complex judgment, learning, creativity, decision quality, or safety during recurrent/chronic sleep restriction.

NCT02468856 enrolled 12 healthy adults for a single armodafinil 250 mg dose or placebo after sleep deprivation, with planned simulated-driving, EEG, psychomotor-vigilance, working-memory, risk-taking, and other cognitive outcomes. It is listed completed, but has no posted results or linked publication, so it supplies neither positive nor null efficacy evidence. [R12]

The best-known null/limited cognitive-enhancement findings in rested healthy people are racemic-modafinil studies, not armodafinil studies: no significant battery-wide effect in 30 non-sleep-deprived students (100/200 mg) despite mood/somatic-anxiety effects; mostly null outcomes in 45 adults aged 50–67 with isolated improvements and more set-shifting errors at 200 mg; and many null reaction-time, attention, memory, executive, visuospatial, and fluency outcomes in 60 healthy young adults. [R13][R14][R15]

**Evidence boundary:** direct R-modafinil evidence supports wakefulness and vigilance during acute sleep loss. A convincing armodafinil-specific literature demonstrating broad cognitive enhancement in rested healthy people was not identified. Racemic-modafinil null findings must not be silently relabeled as R-modafinil evidence.

## Pharmacokinetics and medication interactions

Armodafinil is the pharmacokinetically persistent R-enantiomer. The U.S. label gives an apparent terminal half-life of about 15 h versus approximately 4 h for S-modafinil. After a single 50 mg NUVIGIL dose, the R-enantiomer concentration-time profile is nearly superimposable on 100 mg racemic PROVIGIL. At steady state, equal 200 mg total doses do not produce equal exposure: pure armodafinil yields substantially greater late R exposure because racemic modafinil includes rapidly cleared S-enantiomer. [R1]

A pooled analysis of three randomized PK studies involving 119 healthy subjects found approximately linear PK from 50–400 mg, fasted Tmax around 2 h, terminal half-life around 15 h, and steady state by about 7 days. Food did not materially change exposure but delayed Tmax roughly 2–4 h. Steady-state exposure was about 1.8-fold single-dose exposure. [R16]

### CYP3A4/5 induction and CYP2C19 inhibition

Repeated armodafinil 250 mg/day decreased midazolam AUC by approximately 32% after oral dosing and 17% after IV dosing; oral midazolam Cmax fell about 19%. Armodafinil 400 mg increased omeprazole exposure about 38–40%; caffeine PK was not materially altered. Investigators characterized armodafinil as CYP3A4 inducer and CYP2C19 inhibitor. [R17] The label likewise describes weak/moderate CYP3A4/5 induction and reversible/moderate CYP2C19 inhibition. [R1]

Armodafinil can lower concentrations/effectiveness of steroidal contraceptives, cyclosporine, midazolam, triazolam, and other CYP3A4/5 substrates, while increasing exposure or prolonging elimination of CYP2C19 substrates including phenytoin, diazepam, propranolol, omeprazole, and clomipramine. The label advises closer PT/INR monitoring with warfarin and caution with MAO inhibitors. Quetiapine exposure fell approximately 29% in a clinical interaction study. [R1]

Steroidal contraceptive effectiveness may remain reduced during treatment and for 1 month after discontinuation; alternative or concomitant contraception is advised. [R1]

### Hepatic and renal impairment

The label directs dose reduction in severe hepatic impairment, but does not specify one universal numeric dose. Evidence is extrapolated from racemic modafinil, not a dedicated armodafinil hepatic study: in nine patients with cirrhosis, modafinil clearance fell about 60% and steady-state concentration approximately doubled. [R1]

Renal evidence is also indirect. A single 200 mg racemic-modafinil study in severe chronic renal failure (CrCl ≤20 mL/min) did not substantially alter parent PK, but inactive modafinil-acid exposure rose approximately ninefold. This is not a dedicated armodafinil safety study in advanced kidney disease. [R1]

## Safety, misuse, dependence, and pregnancy

### Serious rash and DRESS

The U.S. label warns of serious dermatologic reactions including Stevens–Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis. Serious rash occurred from approximately 1 day to 2 months after initiation, with isolated later cases; no reliable predictor identifies serious rashes. Discontinue at the first sign of rash, skin/mouth sores, blistering, or ulceration unless clearly unrelated. [R1]

DRESS/multiorgan hypersensitivity has occurred. The label describes a fatal DRESS case temporally associated with armodafinil beginning about three weeks after initiation, while racemic modafinil supplies additional class-related evidence. [R1]

### Psychiatric effects

Controlled trials showed discontinuation because of anxiety/agitation/nervousness/irritability more often with armodafinil than placebo (about 1.2% vs 0.3%) and depression-related discontinuation approximately 0.6% vs 0.2%; suicidal ideation occurred. Postmarketing reports include mania, delusions, hallucinations, suicidal ideation, and aggression, sometimes requiring hospitalization and not restricted to patients with prior psychiatric history. [R1]

### Cardiovascular effects

Across short-term trials, mean BP increases versus placebo were small (roughly 1.2–4.3 mmHg) and pulse rose about 0.9–3.5 beats/min; new/intensified antihypertensive therapy was required in 2.9% of armodafinil recipients versus 1.8% placebo. The label recommends HR/BP monitoring and caution in cardiovascular disease. [R1]

Large outcome studies are mainly racemic-modafinil evidence. A three-database U.S. pharmacoepidemiology study found no overall increase in myocardial-infarction risk and mostly null cardiovascular-hospitalization estimates, but among OSA patients with prior stroke the adjusted recurrent-stroke HR was 1.96 (95% CI 1.02–3.76). This cannot be converted into an armodafinil-specific risk estimate. [R18]

### Tolerance, misuse, dependence, and withdrawal

Postmarketing NUVIGIL reports include increasingly large/repeated doses for desired effects, diversion, and obtaining the drug contrary to medical advice. Physical dependence has been reported; abrupt cessation or major reduction after chronic treatment has been associated with shaking, sweating, chills, nausea/vomiting, confusion, aggression, and atrial fibrillation. Withdrawal reports also include convulsions, suicidality, fatigue, insomnia, aches, depression, and headache. Multiple tolerance reports are specifically noted. [R1]

Controlled abuse-liability evidence cited by the label is largely racemic-modafinil evidence, which produced psychoactive/euphoric/stimulant-like effects in drug-experienced participants. Monkey self-administration is animal evidence and not a human armodafinil-dependence experiment. [R1]

### Pregnancy

Direct human armodafinil data are insufficient to quantify pregnancy risk; the U.S. label notes reports of intrauterine growth restriction and spontaneous abortion. [R1]

The completed 14-year U.S. Provigil/Nuvigil Pregnancy Exposure Registry pooled modafinil and/or armodafinil. Among 137 prospective live births, 18 major congenital malformations occurred (13.1%, 95% CI 8.0–20.0); first-trimester prevalence was 13.7% versus a cited 3% population comparator. Because products were pooled, this cannot identify an armodafinil-specific absolute risk. [R19]

Independent population studies concern predominantly racemic modafinil. Sweden/Norway reported 3 major malformations among 133 first-trimester-exposed infants (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.35–3.25). [R20]

A 2026 French cohort comparing 786 modafinil-exposed pregnancies with 3,144 matched unexposed pregnancies found first-trimester major malformations 4.2% vs 3.3%, adjusted RR 1.32 (95% CI 0.81–2.15); most other outcomes were not significantly different, although elective termination was more frequent. [R21]

A separate French study of 865 prenatally modafinil-exposed children found first-trimester adjusted RR for major anomalies 1.23 (95% CI 0.76–1.99) versus matched unexposed children and 1.77 (95% CI 1.01–3.07) versus methylphenidate-exposed children; neurodevelopmental-disorder risk was not increased versus matched unexposed children. Authors said moderate malformation risk could not be excluded, reported no study-funding conflicts, and acknowledged COUPERIN CY26 open-access funding. [R22][R23]

**Synthesis:** the manufacturer registry has a substantial combined modafinil/armodafinil signal; larger independent racemic-modafinil cohorts are discordant and retain uncertainty. Evidence establishes neither safety nor a specific teratogenic-risk magnitude for isolated armodafinil. [R19][R23]

## Mechanisms, animal findings, and reference ledger

The precise wake-promoting mechanism remains unknown. The label reports dopamine-transporter binding and dopamine-reuptake inhibition in vitro and describes indirect dopamine-receptor agonism; these findings support dopaminergic involvement but do not establish a complete clinical mechanism. [R1]

Animal findings remain separate: R/S enantiomers have broadly similar actions in tested animal systems; modafinil loses wake-promoting activity in dopamine-transporter knockout mice; racemic modafinil is reinforcing in monkeys. These cannot quantify human abuse/dependence risk. Pregnant rats receiving armodafinil 60/200/600 mg/kg/day showed reduced fetal body weight and developmental-delay variations at the highest dose; the 200 mg/kg/day no-effect exposure was below human exposure at 250 mg/day. [R1]
