## Scope and evidence verdict

Modafinil is a prescription wakefulness-promoting drug. Under the current US label, oral modafinil is indicated in adults to improve wakefulness in excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), and shift-work disorder (SWD). In OSA it treats residual sleepiness, not airway obstruction; treatment such as CPAP remains necessary. [R1]

Evidence does not support treating modafinil as a general-purpose cognitive enhancer in healthy, rested adults. A 2020 systematic review/meta-analysis found a statistically significant but very small overall cognitive effect in healthy non-sleep-deprived adults (SMD 0.12), with a larger signal for memory updating (SMD 0.28); authors emphasized small, domain-specific effects and the poor match between laboratory paradigms and real-world nonmedical use. Individual healthy-volunteer trials show selective improvements mixed with numerous null outcomes and occasional slowing. [R2][R3][R4]

The distinction between rested cognition and a sleep-loss countermeasure is important. In healthy adults awake for 54.5 h, single doses of 200 or 400 mg improved performance and alertness versus placebo after 41.5 h awake, were broadly comparable with 600 mg caffeine, and did not differ significantly from each other. This supports acute wakefulness/performance during severe sleep loss, not enhancement above a healthy rested baseline or replacement for sleep. [R5]

Regulatory status is jurisdiction-specific. The current US label retains narcolepsy, OSA and SWD indications. The UK MHRA describes licensed use as excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy with or without cataplexy; European restrictions removed broader indications after risk-benefit review. The OSA meta-analysis records that the European indication for residual OSA sleepiness was removed after regulatory review. [R1][R6][R7]

## Regulatory status, formulation, and dose

The current US DailyMed SPL is **Modafinil tablets, for oral use, C-IV**, Apotex Corp., version 21, effective July 21, 2026. It is an oral immediate-release racemate in 100-mg and 200-mg tablets. [R1]

| Setting | Current US dose/formulation | Key qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Narcolepsy | 200 mg orally once each morning | 400 mg/day has been tolerated, but no consistent additional benefit beyond 200 mg/day. [R1] |
| OSA-associated residual sleepiness | 200 mg orally each morning | Treats sleepiness only, not obstruction; CPAP/underlying therapy remains essential. [R1] |
| Shift-work disorder | 200 mg orally about 1 h before the work shift | Applies to diagnosed SWD rather than ordinary shift-work fatigue. [R1] |
| Severe hepatic impairment | One-half the otherwise recommended dose | Cirrhosis produces about 60% lower clearance and approximately doubled steady-state concentration. [R1] |
| Older adults | Consider lower doses and close monitoring | Clearance can be reduced with advanced age. [R1] |

The drug is contraindicated under US labeling in people with known hypersensitivity to modafinil, armodafinil or formulation ingredients. [R1]

## Pharmacokinetics, interactions, and pregnancy

Modafinil is a 1:1 racemate of R- and S-modafinil. R-modafinil has an approximately threefold longer half-life than S-modafinil; steady-state R exposure is about three times S exposure and roughly 90% of trough circulating modafinil is R-enantiomer. Effective elimination half-life after multiple dosing is approximately 15 h; steady state occurs after 2–4 days; multiple-dose PK is approximately linear from 200–600 mg/day in healthy volunteers. [R1]

Oral Tmax is about 2–4 h. Food does not meaningfully change overall bioavailability but can delay Tmax by about 1 h. Apparent volume of distribution is about 0.9 L/kg and plasma-protein binding about 60%, primarily albumin. About 90% of elimination is metabolic, less than 10% of a dose is excreted unchanged, and a radiolabeled human study recovered 81% of administered radioactivity within 11 days, principally in urine. [R1]

Severe chronic renal failure did not substantially alter parent-modafinil PK after a single 200-mg dose, but inactive modafinil-acid metabolite exposure rose approximately ninefold. In advanced hepatic cirrhosis, oral clearance fell about 60% and steady-state concentration approximately doubled, supporting half-dose guidance in severe hepatic impairment. [R1]

Modafinil induces CYP3A4/5, lowering systemic exposure to steroidal contraceptives, cyclosporine, midazolam and triazolam; CYP2C19 inhibition can increase phenytoin, diazepam, propranolol, omeprazole and clomipramine exposure. Some SSRIs/TCAs may also be affected in CYP2D6-poor metabolizers using CYP2C19 as an ancillary pathway. The US label recommends additional PT/INR monitoring with warfarin and caution with MAO inhibitors. [R1]

In 41 women taking ethinyl estradiol 0.035 mg/norgestimate, modafinil was given at 200 mg/day for 7 days then 400 mg/day for 21 days, with triazolam 0.125 mg as a CYP3A probe. The label quantifies an 11% reduction in ethinyl-estradiol Cmax and 18% reduction in AUC0–24; triazolam Cmax fell 42%, AUC∞ 59%, and half-life shortened by about 1 h. [R1][R13]

Contraceptive guidance differs. The US label recommends alternative or concomitant contraception during treatment and for 1 month after discontinuation. UK MHRA advises effective contraception during treatment and for 2 months after stopping because CYP3A4/5 induction can reduce steroidal contraceptive effectiveness. [R1][R6]

Pregnancy evidence is conflicting and observational, while regulatory advice remains cautious. MHRA’s 2020 pharmacovigilance review concluded modafinil may increase congenital-malformation risk: among 78 prospectively reported pregnancies there were 61 live births and 9 major congenital anomalies; estimated major-malformation prevalence was 14.75% (95% CI 5.85–23.65) and cardiac-anomaly prevalence 4.92%. These were small, nonrandomized data combining modafinil and armodafinil exposure and cannot establish causality. [R6]

The official pregnancy registry NCT01792583 recorded 191 participants, was terminated because the study was “no longer feasible,” completed in November 2023, and had no posted results in its February 2024 update. Sponsor: Cephalon; record later maintained by Teva. [R13]

A French nationwide cohort published in October 2026 compared 786 exposed pregnancies with 3,144 unexposed pregnancies: first-trimester major congenital malformations were 4.2% vs 3.3%, adjusted RR 1.32 (95% CI 0.81–2.15), not statistically significant. Most other outcomes did not differ significantly, although elective termination was more frequent (PR 1.32, 95% CI 1.14–1.51). This is nonrandomized pharmacoepidemiology with possible residual confounding; it argues against a large easily detectable teratogenic effect, not proof of fetal safety. [R16]

The US label, effective July 2026, still states that adequate well-controlled studies in pregnant women are lacking and notes reports of intrauterine growth restriction and spontaneous abortion with uncertain causality. The newer cohort has not displaced regulatory caution as of this cutoff. [R1]

## Safety, tolerance, and dependence

Serious dermatologic/hypersensitivity risk is low-frequency but potentially severe. US labeling reports hospitalization-requiring rash, including postmarketing Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis and DRESS. Nearly all serious rashes arose within 1–5 weeks of initiation, but later cases occur; no reliable predictor distinguishes benign from dangerous rash. Discontinue at the first sign of rash unless clearly not drug-related. Pediatric trials recorded rash leading to discontinuation in about 0.8% (13/1,585), including a possible SJS case; modafinil is not US-approved for pediatric indications. Multi-organ hypersensitivity, including a postmarketing fatality, has also been reported. [R1]

Psychiatric adverse effects are established. Controlled adult-trial symptoms causing discontinuation and more frequent than placebo included anxiety 1%, nervousness 1%, and insomnia, confusion, agitation and depression each under 1%. Postmarketing reports include mania, delusions, hallucinations, suicidal ideation and aggression, some requiring hospitalization. One healthy volunteer developed ideas of reference, paranoid delusions and auditory hallucinations during repeated 600-mg dosing plus sleep deprivation; symptoms resolved after stopping. This supratherapeutic exposure does not estimate 200-mg risk but demonstrates possible clinically important psychiatric toxicity. [R1]

Cardiovascular effects are usually modest at the population level but not absent. Short controlled trials found no clinically important mean BP changes, yet 2.4% of modafinil-treated patients versus 0.7% placebo required new/increased antihypertensive medication; in OSA studies the figures were 3.4% vs 1.1%. The label records uncommon chest pain, palpitations, ECG abnormalities and a 9-second asystole episode in a predisposed narcolepsy patient receiving 300 mg/day. Increased heart-rate/BP monitoring is advised when cardiovascular risk is relevant. [R1]

Across pooled placebo-controlled narcolepsy/OSA/SWD studies: headache 34% vs 23%, nausea 11% vs 3%, nervousness 7% vs 3%, anxiety 5% vs 1%, insomnia 5% vs 1%, hypertension 3% vs 1%, palpitations 2% vs 1%, tachycardia 2% vs 1%; 8% of modafinil patients vs 3% placebo discontinued because of an adverse event. [R1]

US modafinil is Schedule IV. The label recognizes psychoactive/euphoric effects and instructs monitoring for dose escalation and drug-seeking. In a double-blind crossover abuse-liability experiment, 24 male volunteers with histories of polysubstance abuse received modafinil 200/400/800 mg, methylphenidate 45/90 mg and placebo. Participants distinguished and liked both active drugs; modafinil did not produce a significant Amphetamine Scale response and had a different physiological profile from methylphenidate. This supports less amphetamine-like subjective pharmacology, not zero abuse liability. [R1][R14]

Physical-withdrawal evidence is limited. The US label reports no specific withdrawal symptoms during 14 days after 9 weeks of exposure, although pathological sleepiness returned in narcolepsy. A 40-week open-label extension in 478 narcolepsy patients improved ESS from 16.5 to 12.4 by week 2 and maintained improvement through week 40; investigators reported no evidence of tolerance; 71% completed and 9% stopped because of adverse events. This is an open-label extension enriched for prior completers, not a long-term randomized withdrawal design, so it cannot establish that tolerance never develops. [R1][R15]

The official label states that the precise wakefulness mechanism remains unknown. In-vitro dopamine-transporter binding/reuptake inhibition and animal experiments are mechanistic findings, not evidence that a particular human cognitive or clinical outcome will occur. Animal reproductive-toxicity findings inform labeling but are not substituted for human pregnancy evidence. [R1]

## Unresolved evidence gaps

Principal gaps are long-term randomized safety/tolerance, real-world cognitive benefit in healthy rested users, and pregnancy risk. The best long-term narcolepsy dataset is a 40-week open-label extension rather than a long-term blinded randomized comparison; healthy cognition experiments are mostly small, acute and laboratory-based; pregnancy studies remain observational and discordant, while the industry pregnancy registry never posted final results. [R2][R13][R14][R16]
