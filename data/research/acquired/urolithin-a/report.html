Executive summary

Urolithin A (UA; PubChem CID 5488186) is a gut-microbiome-derived metabolite of ellagic acid/ellagitannins, but direct oral UA supplementation is pharmacologically distinct from eating pomegranate, walnuts, berries, or other ellagitannin sources. In the randomized NOURISH crossover study, only about 40% of participants became high UA producers 24 hours after pomegranate juice, whereas a direct 500 mg UA dose produced substantially more consistent systemic exposure and more than sixfold greater integrated exposure to UA-related analytes. 1

The human efficacy evidence is promising but not definitive. In older adults, 1,000 mg/day for four months improved some secondary muscle-endurance measurements, while the prespecified 6-minute-walk and ATP-production endpoints were not significantly better than placebo. 2 In the ATLAS trial, 500 or 1,000 mg/day for four months improved selected hamstring-strength endpoints, but the prespecified peak-power endpoint was negative and several whole-body performance outcomes did not achieve significant between-group differences. 3 A 2026 systematic review found only five randomized muscle-related trials totaling 236 participants; its pooled 6-minute-walk estimate was +17.03 m versus placebo (95% CI −5.33 to 39.40 m), statistically inconclusive and rated low-certainty evidence. 4

The mechanistic case is stronger than the clinical-outcome case. Preclinical work established UA as a mitophagy-promoting compound, while human skeletal-muscle studies have shown changes in mitochondrial gene expression, proteomic pathways, Parkin-associated markers, acylcarnitines, and related biomarkers. Those translational measurements support biological activity but do not prove that mitophagy flux is enhanced throughout the human body or that disease outcomes improve. The first-in-human trial explicitly characterized the clinical findings as a mitochondrial/cellular-health molecular signature. 5

Recent evidence broadens the biology without establishing new clinical indications. A 2025 randomized trial in 50 healthy middle-aged adults found that 1,000 mg/day for four weeks shifted CD8 T-cell phenotype and fatty-acid-oxidation capacity, including treatment differences of +0.50 percentage points (95% CI 0.16–0.83) for a less-terminally-exhausted/naive-like CD8 population and +14.72 percentage points (95% CI 6.46–22.99) for CD8 fatty-acid-oxidation capacity. These are immune-cell endpoints, not evidence that UA prevents infections, cancer, or other clinical events. 6

Short- and medium-term oral use has generally been well tolerated in the published studies examined here. The first-in-human trial reported a favorable safety profile; the older-adult RCT found no significant difference in adverse events between UA and placebo; and ATLAS found no significant treatment-related differences across its safety testing. Published randomized exposure has nevertheless generally been measured in weeks to about four months, leaving long-term safety and evidence in pregnancy, lactation, children, and many chronic-disease populations inadequately established. 5 2 7

Commercial involvement is an important evidence-quality consideration rather than evidence that the results are invalid. Amazentis sponsored the first-in-human study, and the paper disclosed employee, board, and scientific-advisory relationships; several subsequent pivotal trials were also Amazentis-funded or sponsored. 5 An independent 20-person resistance-trained-athlete RCT reported noncommercial academic funding and no declared conflict, but its sample was very small. 8 The 2026 meta-analysis accordingly treats the currently observed strength, endurance, and mitochondrial signals as exploratory rather than reproducible clinical efficacy. 4

In the United States, FDA GRAS Notice 791 concerns specified food-ingredient uses of UA. The notifier was Amazentis SA; FDA closed the notice on December 20, 2018 with a “no questions” response for the notified uses. That is not FDA approval of UA as a drug or approval of disease-treatment claims. 9

## Summary

Urolithin A (UA) is a dibenzopyranone metabolite generated by some human gut microbiomes after exposure to ellagic acid and ellagitannins from foods such as pomegranate and certain nuts and berries. The ability to generate UA from these precursors varies markedly among individuals. Direct synthetic UA supplementation therefore constitutes a different exposure from consuming ellagitannin-rich foods: a randomized crossover study found that direct 500 mg UA produced substantially greater and more consistent circulating exposure than pomegranate juice.

Controlled human studies have established oral bioavailability and short- to medium-term tolerability and have reported signals in skeletal-muscle endurance, selected strength measurements, exercise-recovery biomarkers, mitochondrial-associated molecular markers and immune-cell metabolism. The clinical evidence is nevertheless limited by small heterogeneous trials, frequent reliance on secondary or biomarker endpoints, several negative prespecified primary endpoints, intervention periods generally no longer than about four months in the published core trials, and substantial commercial sponsorship. A 2026 systematic review rated pooled 6-minute-walk evidence low certainty and statistically inconclusive.

## Description

Urolithin A is produced downstream of dietary ellagitannins and ellagic acid by specific gut microbial communities. Natural production is therefore conditional on precursor intake and microbiome metabolic capacity; it should not be assumed that a dose of pomegranate, walnut or ellagitannin extract is equivalent to a known dose of UA. In the NOURISH trial, only a minority of participants had detectable circulating UA at baseline and substantial interindividual variability persisted after pomegranate juice, while direct 500 mg UA produced relatively uniform circulating exposure.

The best-developed clinical formulation is proprietary synthetic UA marketed as Mitopure by Amazentis/Timeline. Research exposures have most commonly been 500 or 1,000 mg orally per day, but these are study doses rather than BHWiki dosing recommendations. Human studies have centered on mitochondrial and muscle biology, physical function and, more recently, immune-cell aging. Preclinical data also span neurological, cardiometabolic, inflammatory and cancer models, but those disease-model findings are not equivalent to demonstrated human therapeutic indications.

## Evidence note

As of 2026-10-04, the human evidence is best characterized as mechanistically credible but clinically preliminary. The first-in-human trial established exposure, short-term tolerability and mitochondrial-associated pharmacodynamic signals. Later placebo-controlled trials reported improvements in selected muscle-endurance or strength endpoints, yet important prespecified endpoints were null: maximal ATP production and between-group 6-minute-walk improvement were not significant in ENERGIZE, peak power output was not significantly improved in ATLAS, and 3,000 m running performance was not significantly improved in highly trained runners.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis identified five randomized muscle-related trials with 236 total participants. Only the 6-minute-walk test could be quantitatively pooled; the pooled mean difference was +17.03 m (95% CI -5.33 to 39.40 m), with low GRADE certainty and a confidence interval crossing no effect. Other strength, endurance, aerobic and biomarker findings were too heterogeneous for quantitative synthesis and were considered exploratory.

Most foundational direct-UA human studies were sponsored or funded by Amazentis and included employees, board members, scientific advisers, founders, shareholders or patent interests in their disclosures. An independent 2024 resistance-trained-athlete study reported noncommercial academic funding and no declared conflict, but included only 20 men. Commercial sponsorship is not itself evidence that results are invalid, but replication by independent groups and larger trials remains important.

Direct UA must be kept separate from studies of dietary ellagitannin precursors or multi-ingredient products. Biomarker changes, mitochondrial pathway signatures and immune-cell shifts are pharmacodynamic evidence; they do not by themselves establish prevention or treatment of sarcopenia, infection, cancer, dementia, cardiovascular disease or other clinical disorders.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: "Mitophagy induction in preclinical models"
  description: "UA induced mitophagy in C. elegans and mammalian experimental systems and was associated with improved muscle function in rodent models. This establishes a strong preclinical mitochondrial-quality-control mechanism but does not by itself establish human clinical efficacy."
  sourceId: ryu-2016-mitophagy
  conceptId: mitophagy

- title: "Human skeletal-muscle mitochondrial and Parkin-associated pathway engagement"
  description: "ATLAS skeletal-muscle transcriptomic, proteomic and targeted protein analyses showed UA-associated enrichment of mitochondrial pathways and Parkin-mediated quality-control markers, including increased phospho-Parkin in a small biopsy subset. These are supportive pathway-engagement markers; direct whole-body mitophagy flux was not measured."
  sourceId: singh-2022-atlas
  conceptId: mitophagy

- title: "Phase-II conjugation after absorption"
  description: "Human bioavailability studies detect parent UA together with UA-glucuronide and UA-sulfate in plasma, demonstrating extensive conjugative metabolism after direct oral exposure. Circulating conjugates should not be treated interchangeably with unconjugated parent UA in pharmacokinetic interpretation."
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: "Clinical efficacy remains uncertain"
  description: "A 2026 systematic review found five small heterogeneous randomized muscle-related trials and low-certainty, statistically inconclusive pooled evidence for the 6-minute-walk test. Strength, endurance and biomarker findings were considered exploratory rather than reproducible proof of efficacy."
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta

- title: "Several important primary or performance endpoints were null"
  description: "ENERGIZE did not establish significant UA superiority for its 6-minute-walk or maximal-ATP-production primary endpoints; ATLAS did not significantly improve its prespecified peak-power endpoint; and the trained-runner trial did not significantly improve 3,000 m performance."
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta

- title: "Published long-term safety evidence is limited"
  description: "The core randomized direct-UA studies evaluated exposures lasting weeks to approximately four months. They were generally well tolerated, but this evidence does not establish safety of indefinite use or safety across unstudied populations."
  sourceId: dao-2026-meta

- title: "Pregnancy, lactation and pediatric evidence is insufficient"
  description: "The principal direct-UA efficacy trials examined adults and frequently excluded pregnancy; the available trial set does not establish safety or benefit in pregnancy, lactation, infants or children."
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability

- title: "Direct UA is not equivalent to ellagitannin-rich food or extract"
  description: "Gut microbial conversion of ellagitannin and ellagic-acid precursors varies substantially among individuals. Direct 500 mg UA produced far more consistent exposure than pomegranate juice in NOURISH, so precursor-food studies should not be assigned the pharmacokinetics of direct UA."
  sourceId: singh-2022-bioavailability

- title: "Industry sponsorship and inventor interests are common in the foundational evidence"
  description: "ATLAS was funded by Amazentis SA and disclosed multiple authors as company employees plus board or scientific-advisory relationships. Other pivotal trials have similar commercial involvement. These disclosures warrant attention to independent replication but do not by themselves invalidate the findings."
  sourceId: singh-2022-atlas

- title: "Biomarkers are not disease-treatment evidence"
  description: "Changes in acylcarnitines, mitochondrial proteins, inflammatory markers or immune-cell states provide biological evidence but do not demonstrate that UA treats sarcopenia, infection, cancer, neurodegeneration or other diseases."
  sourceId: denk-2025-immune

- title: "United States GRAS status is not drug approval"
  description: "FDA's response to GRAS Notice 791 concerns specified food uses and states that FDA had no questions regarding the notifier's GRAS conclusion. It does not constitute FDA approval of UA as a drug or authorization of disease-treatment claims."
  sourceId: fda-grn-791
```

Study comparison and timeline

The core published direct-UA human evidence shows a recurrent pattern: biological activity and selected positive secondary outcomes coexist with null or inconclusive primary and integrated-performance outcomes. The 2026 systematic review's low-certainty assessment is therefore more representative of the total evidence than a simple count of statistically significant findings. 4

## Description

Coverage and limitations

The most important unresolved technical gap is analyte-specific human elimination half-life. The first-in-human paper clearly performed PK testing, and later peer-reviewed reviews report approximately 17–22 hours for parent UA/UA-glucuronide and longer values for UA-sulfate. However, the detailed supplementary PK PDF from the 2019 primary paper could not be independently retrieved during this run. To comply with the brief's rule against importing an unverified numeric estimate, the BHWiki source records only the approximately 24-hour generic statement that is directly visible in the later primary NOURISH paper and marks it modelEligible: false; the parent, glucuronide and sulfate records remain explicitly nonnumeric. The first-in-human publication itself confirms bioavailability and mitochondrial pharmacodynamic effects but its publicly rendered main article does not expose the detailed analyte-specific table. 5

The evidence base is still moving. ClinicalTrials.gov records include completed studies in frailty and obesity/vascular function with no posted results in the records inspected, while trials in glucose metabolism, obesity/weight loss, cancer immunotherapy, sleep/aging, and cerebrospinal-fluid kinetics are active, recruiting, invitation-only, or not yet recruiting. Registry presence does not establish efficacy, and unpublished studies were therefore not entered as positive outcome evidence. 11 14 15 16

The EU regulatory record is deliberately conservative. An official European Commission document establishes that Amazentis submitted a novel-food application for UA in 2018, but the official materials inspected here did not establish a final authorization outcome. The article therefore records the application rather than inferring authorization or prohibition. In contrast, U.S. GRN 791 has an explicit FDA database disposition: “FDA has no questions” for the specified notified food uses. 9

No adequate human drug–drug-interaction study was identified in the inspected evidence set. Consequently, Interactions remains empty rather than converting preliminary in-vitro CYP observations into clinical interaction warnings. Likewise, no subjective-effect records were populated: changes in exercise RPE or laboratory biomarkers are not interchangeable with BHWiki's canonical subjective-effect concepts.

The patent set is representative rather than exhaustive. It documents major Amazentis intellectual-property families around mitochondrial applications, autophagy/longevity claims, and scalable UA synthesis; patent ownership is treated as a commercial-interest disclosure rather than efficacy evidence. US10028932B2 identifies Amazentis as assignee for mitochondrial/neurodegenerative-use claims, while other families cover autophagy/longevity and process-scale UA manufacture. 17
