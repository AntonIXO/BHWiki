## Summary

Saccharomyces boulardii is a live probiotic yeast lineage generally classified within Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Evidence must be interpreted at strain and product level rather than assuming that every product labeled S. boulardii is interchangeable. CNCM I-745 is the most explicitly characterized medicinal-product strain in the sources reviewed here; other research products include CBS 5926 and ATCC MYA-796, while some clinical products disclose no deposit identifier.

Controlled human evidence is indication-specific and mixed. Adult antibiotic-associated-diarrhea studies range from benefit to clearly null results, with a small 2026 trial reporting an unusually large benefit under a sensitive diarrhea definition. Older trials and a strain-specific meta-analysis support a possible reduction in travelers' diarrhea. Adjunctive trials in Clostridioides difficile disease suggest a possible reduction in recurrence in selected populations, but they do not establish universal efficacy or replacement of standard antimicrobial therapy.

The major safety distinction from many nonliving supplements is that S. boulardii is a viable fungus. Invasive Saccharomyces infection has been associated with probiotic exposure, particularly in critically ill patients and people with central venous catheters or immunodeficiency. Current French CNCM I-745 labeling contraindicates use in those settings and instructs that oral or systemic antifungals not be combined with the product.

## Description

Saccharomyces boulardii was historically presented as a distinct probiotic yeast, but contemporary culture-collection, genomic and regulatory records place it within Saccharomyces cerevisiae. ATCC MYA-796 has strain designation Sb48, was isolated from a commercially available French Ultra-Levure probiotic, and is classified by ATCC as S. cerevisiae; ATCC also records that the deposit was originally made as "Saccharomyces boulardii, Seguela, nomen invalid." NCBI assigns the corresponding JRHY whole-genome project to S. cerevisiae. FDA GSRS separately maintains a validated organism record for S. cerevisiae var. boulardii CNCM I-745, UNII N9QG9Z2QEG.

The public MYA-796 WGS project JRHY00000000 contains 193 contigs totaling 11,405,855 bp in version JRHY01. A 2026 long-read comparative study of CNCM I-745, DBVPG 6763, ATCC MYA-796, CNCM I-3799 and CNCM I-1079 found greater than 99.98% average nucleotide identity among the S. boulardii isolates, approximately 11.8-Mb genomes in that assembly set, and a conserved approximately 68.3-kb inversion on chromosome XVI. The same study reported conserved differences in carbohydrate-active-enzyme copy number and sequence variants in SDH1, BNA2 and ARO8, together with reproducible acid tolerance, acetate/succinate production and host-cell signaling phenotypes. These lineage-level findings do not prove that commercial products have equal clinical efficacy.

CNCM I-745 is explicitly disclosed in current French Ultra-Levure 100-mg sachet and 200-mg capsule medicinal products. The 2016 German AAD trial used Perenterol forte labeled as Saccharomyces cerevisiae HANSEN CBS 5926 (boulardii). The 2026 Bularen AAD trial disclosed 250 mg per capsule and at least 5 × 10^9 viable CFU per capsule but did not disclose a recognized strain-deposit identifier in the inspected publication. These differences are retained rather than normalized away.

## Evidence note

The evidence base is heterogeneous in strain disclosure, formulation, viable count, dose, diarrhea definition, baseline event risk and patient selection. This is especially important for antibiotic-associated diarrhea: positive controlled studies coexist with two substantial null inpatient randomized trials, and the very large effect in a 2026 single-center trial occurred with a definition of at least two loose or liquid stools in 24 hours and a 71.4% placebo event rate. The result therefore should not be treated as a universal class effect.

For recurrent Clostridioides difficile disease, a 1994 randomized trial found an overall adjusted recurrence risk reduction, with the subgroup benefit concentrated in patients with prior recurrent disease and no demonstrated benefit in first-episode disease. A 2025 randomized trial of CNCM I-745 plus vancomycin in mild-to-moderate initial CDI found similar initial clinical cure but fewer recurrences and greater global cure; its small, single-center and selectively enrolled population limits generalization.

Traveler's-diarrhea evidence comes mainly from older trials. The large 1993 study reported dose- and adherence-related benefit, while later systematic review/meta-analysis found a pooled protective estimate for CNCM I-745; older-study attrition and limited strain-specific replication reduce certainty.

Microbiome sequencing endpoints are retained as biomarkers rather than translated into clinical benefit. In healthy volunteers, CNCM I-745 alone did not substantially shift measured bacterial genera, while coadministration with amoxicillin-clavulanate attenuated some antibiotic-associated compositional changes. This does not itself prove prevention of clinically important disease.

No molecular formula, molecular weight, SMILES representation or drug-like elimination half-life applies to a living yeast lineage. Mass in milligrams and viable-organism counts in CFU are also not assumed to be interchangeable across formulations. Current French labeling reports live gastrointestinal transit without colonization and stool disappearance 2 to 5 days after cessation; this persistence window is not encoded as an elimination half-life.
