## Summary

Choline chloride is a highly water-soluble choline salt used as a nutrient source. Human salt-specific evidence is strongest for short-term plasma choline delivery, prenatal supplementation within one small controlled-feeding cohort, and intravenous repletion in choline-deficient long-term parenteral-nutrition patients.

## Description

Choline chloride (CAS 67-48-1; PubChem CID 6209) is the chloride salt of choline. Pure anhydrous material is about 74.6% choline by mass; in one human crossover, 740 mg choline chloride supplied 550 mg choline. Commercial material can instead be aqueous or carried on solids, so product mass is not interchangeable with anhydrous salt mass. Human evidence is context-specific: oral pharmacokinetics have been studied acutely, prenatal controlled feeding used choline chloride to raise total maternal choline intake, and intravenous choline chloride has been studied in severe deficiency during long-term parenteral nutrition.

## Evidence note

This draft includes only outcomes for which choline chloride itself was the stated intervention or nutrient source. General choline-depletion/repletion findings and trials using phosphatidylcholine, citicoline, alpha-GPC, or choline bitartrate are not imported as choline-chloride outcomes. Prenatal cognition papers derive from the same small maternal feeding trial and are not independent replications. A single human elimination half-life was not established in the inspected pharmacokinetic report.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Choline delivery
  description: Choline chloride is an ionic source of choline; a human oral crossover demonstrated a rapid rise in plasma choline after a dose explicitly quantified as 740 mg choline chloride providing 550 mg choline.
  sourceId: bockmann-2022
  conceptId: choline-precursor
- title: Choline oxidation and methyl-donor pathway
  description: After oral choline chloride, plasma betaine increased, consistent with oxidation of absorbed choline into the one-carbon metabolic pool.
  sourceId: bockmann-2022
- title: Gut microbial trimethylamine pathway
  description: The oral crossover attributed post-dose TMAO formation from water-soluble choline sources to microbial trimethylamine generation followed by hepatic oxidation; the magnitude varied strongly among individuals.
  sourceId: bockmann-2022
- title: Hepatic lipid-export support during deficiency
  description: In severe long-term parenteral-nutrition deficiency, intravenous choline chloride restored plasma free choline and was accompanied by reversal of CT-estimated hepatic steatosis; the small clinical series does not by itself quantify the responsible biochemical step.
  sourceId: buchman-1995
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Distinguish choline mass from choline-chloride mass
  description: Pure anhydrous choline chloride is about 74.6% choline by molecular mass, and the acute human study used 740 mg salt to supply 550 mg choline. Commercial aqueous and carrier-based products can have substantially lower choline-chloride concentration, so label basis must be checked.
  sourceId: bockmann-2022
- title: TMAO response after oral dosing
  description: A single choline-chloride dose increased plasma TMAO in the six-man crossover, with large individual variation. The study did not establish the long-term clinical significance of repeated transient TMAO elevations.
  sourceId: bockmann-2022
- title: High total choline intake
  description: The adult 3.5 g/day tolerable upper intake level applies to total choline, not to choline-chloride mass; reported high-intake concerns include hypotension, sweating, diarrhea, and fishy body odor. It is not a choline-chloride dosing target.
  sourceId: fda-choline-2001
- title: Occupational skin and respiratory exposure
  description: EFSA's 2025 feed-additive review considers choline chloride a potential skin and respiratory sensitizer and treats skin or respiratory exposure as a user-safety risk; eye-irritation potential above 70% aqueous concentration could not be concluded.
  sourceId: efsa-2025
- title: Fire, oxidizers, dust, and moisture
  description: The ILO-WHO safety card describes choline chloride as combustible and hygroscopic, advises separation from strong oxidants, ventilation and basic eye/skin protection, and notes corrosive or toxic fumes including hydrogen chloride on combustion.
  sourceId: icsc-0853
```

Chemical, regulatory, toxicology, and use synthesis

The pure salt is a permanently ionic quaternary-ammonium chloride. ILO-WHO ICSC 0853 describes white hygroscopic crystals, water miscibility, a melting point around 305 °C, and log Pow −5.16; EFSA likewise characterizes it as freely water-soluble. 3 A key practical distinction is ingredient mass versus nutrient mass: from molecular composition, anhydrous choline chloride is approximately 74.6% choline by mass, closely matching the controlled human study's explicit 740 mg choline chloride → 550 mg choline conversion. 1

Manufacturing, supply chain, market, and environmental fate

Industrial production is based on inexpensive bulk chemical precursors. PubChem's manufacturing records describe reaction routes involving trimethylamine, ethylene oxide, water/acid, including a sequence in which trimethylamine hydrochloride is treated with ethylene oxide under pressure. EFSA's current feed-additive dossier describes the active substance as being manufactured as an aqueous solution containing at least 75% choline chloride, with downstream solid preparations on vegetable or silica carriers. 1

Environmental-fate evidence is comparatively sparse but directionally consistent. Choline chloride is water-miscible and has a strongly negative log Pow in ICSC data; Japan's National Institute for Environmental Studies database, incorporating METI existing-chemical assessment information, labels its biodegradability as good/readily biodegradable. EFSA's 2025 feed-additive reassessment concluded that authorized animal-nutrition use remains safe for the environment. 3 The diagram therefore confines itself to supported high-level pathways rather than inventing exact environmental half-lives.

The diagram should not be read as a quantitative multimedia-fate model: no reliable salt-specific environmental half-life, field degradation rate, bioaccumulation factor, or sediment partition coefficient was established from the inspected public primary/official sources. Water miscibility, low log Pow and reported ready/good biodegradability support the broad pathway, but not an exact residence time. 3

Safety quick reference

For workplace handling, the most current high-level risk assessment inspected was EFSA's 2025 feed-additive opinion, complemented by ILO-WHO ICSC 0853. EFSA is more cautious than some historical generic classifications: it treats the active substance as a potential skin and respiratory sensitizer, whereas ICSC supplies practical dust, spill, fire and oxidizer controls. 10

The human evidence supports a sharp separation between deficiency treatment and enhancement claims. In four long-term TPN patients, 1–4 g/day intravenous choline chloride for six weeks brought plasma free choline into the normal range within a week and accompanied CT-estimated resolution of hepatic steatosis; that is clinically meaningful but highly population-specific evidence. 14 Intravenous PK in four TPN patients showed saturable two-compartment elimination after 7, 14, 28 and 56 mmol infusions, but the inspected report did not establish a single usable elimination half-life. 16

The cognition evidence is much less generalizable. A controlled maternal feeding trial used choline chloride to create 480 versus 930 mg/day total-choline diets; infant follow-up found faster information-processing speed in the higher-intake group, and a 20-child age-seven follow-up found a mean Sustained Attention Task score of 0.71 versus 0.56 and less vigilance decrement. Both papers derive from the same underlying prenatal cohort, so they are longitudinal follow-ups rather than independent replications. 17 By contrast, small older direct-dose studies in elderly or young subjects did not show a consistent group-level memory benefit from very high oral choline-chloride exposures. 11

Coverage, limitations, and prioritized sources

The research covered salt identity, human oral pharmacokinetics, intravenous PK, deficiency/repletion, prenatal controlled feeding, direct cognition studies, current U.S. food regulation, current EU feed-additive assessment, occupational handling, manufacturing, feed-grade composition/impurities, U.S. production-volume reporting, and environmental fate. The BHWiki artifact was structurally checked for required heading order, canonical participant IDs and internal sourceId resolution before the downloadable files were created.

Several gaps are intentionally left unpopulated. Modifiers are empty because no verified factor was found that could be linked to an actual salt-specific human elimination-half-life observation; Effects are empty because subjective-effect reports were not promoted from clinical biomarkers or cognition tasks; Interactions are empty because no sufficiently explicit salt-specific interaction record met the supplied contract; and Experience links are empty rather than importing informal material. A product-specific modern SDS was not needed to support the core handling record because the higher-authority EFSA and ILO-WHO sources covered the relevant hazards, but actual commercial products can differ in concentration, carrier and impurities. 10

The most important unresolved evidence limitation is that much of the nutritional literature studies choline as a nutrient rather than choline chloride as a chemically isolated intervention. This package therefore does not transfer outcomes from phosphatidylcholine, alpha-GPC, citicoline, choline bitartrate or unspecified dietary choline into the salt-specific record. The prenatal data are especially easy to overstate: the experimental contrast was total choline intake, with choline chloride serving as the supplemental source, so those findings cannot establish that chloride salt chemistry itself is superior to other choline forms. 17
