## Summary

Phenylpiracetam (fonturacetam; racemic Phenotropil) is a chiral phenyl-substituted racetam used as a medicinal product in Russia. Patient studies report cognitive and fatigue-related signals, but healthy-person enhancement remains unestablished; a 2026 healthy-volunteer trial is registered without posted results.

## Description

Fonturacetam is the INN for phenylpiracetam; Phenotropil and Actitropil are trade names used for racemic drug products. The molecule has one stereocenter, and R- and S-enantiomers show different preclinical activity; R-phenylpiracetam is also studied as MRZ-9547. Russian studies include randomized placebo-controlled work in chronic cerebral ischemia and epilepsy plus controlled stroke rehabilitation research, but reporting is largely Russian-language and often lacks modern effect estimates or transparent disclosures. The current Russian label reports oral Tmax of 1 hour and a 3–5 hour half-life. R-enantiomer studies support dopamine-transporter inhibition in rodents and in vitro; human DAT occupancy for the racemate is not established.

## Evidence note

Human evidence is concentrated in Russian clinical populations, not healthy users. A 2010 double-blind placebo-controlled trial reported improvements in MMSE, Schulte and MFI-20 measures but null depression/sleep findings and six discontinuations; its tables contain minor MMSE inconsistencies. A 2026 healthy-volunteer study was first registered after completion and has no posted results. Label PK values lack an identified source study. Preclinical DAT findings are enantiomer-specific. Funding/COI declarations were unavailable for several older clinical reports, limiting bias assessment.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: R-enantiomer dopamine-transporter inhibition
  description: MRZ-9547, identified as the R-enantiomer of phenylpiracetam, inhibited DAT in vitro and moderately increased striatal dopamine in rats; the opposite enantiomer MRZ-9546 was substantially less potent. This does not establish human DAT occupancy for racemic phenylpiracetam.
  sourceId: sommer-2014
  conceptId: dopamine-transporter
- title: R-enantiomer brain penetration and target profiling
  description: In male mice, R-phenylpiracetam reached brain tissue 15 minutes after oral or intraperitoneal 50 mg/kg dosing; target profiling found DAT as the only significant molecular target in the tested panel. These are animal and in-vitro data.
  sourceId: zvejniece-2020-rph
  conceptId: dopamine-transporter
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Psychiatric and drowsiness warnings in Russian prescribing information
  description: The label contraindicates use in pregnancy, breastfeeding and under age 18, advises caution with severe hypertension and certain severe organ disease, and warns that anxiety, panic, hallucinations or delusions may worsen in susceptible patients; first-dose drowsiness can occur in marked exhaustion.
  sourceId: actitropil-label
- title: Discontinuations and tolerability in the 2010 placebo-controlled trial
  description: Six of 75 randomized participants discontinued. Active-arm reasons included dyspepsia and irritability/sleep disturbance; one placebo participant discontinued for daytime sleepiness. The trial was small and not powered for uncommon harms.
  sourceId: fedin-2010
- title: Dependence, tolerance and withdrawal are not independently established
  description: The Russian label states that dependence, tolerance and withdrawal syndrome do not develop during course use, but no dedicated human dependence or withdrawal study was identified in this review.
  sourceId: actitropil-label
- title: Illicit-market product quality is a separate risk
  description: A 2025 multi-country Official Medicines Control Laboratory surveillance study found phenylpiracetam among unauthorized Russian prescription drugs in European/Australian market samples and reported interceptions of bulk raw material near 100% purity; these findings do not establish the quality of any specific consumer product.
  sourceId: vanhee-2025-surveillance
```

Evidence coverage

The molecular identity is anchored to current PubChem CID 132441, which gives formula C₁₂H₁₄N₂O₂, molecular weight 218.25 g/mol, INN fonturacetam, phenylpiracetam/Phenotropil/Carphedon synonyms, the retained connectivity SMILES, and one undefined stereocenter in the racemic record. 2 The current Russian Actitropil information identifies fonturacetam as the active ingredient and reports 50/100-mg tablets, oral Tmax of 1 hour, absolute bioavailability of 100%, a 3–5-hour half-life, no metabolism, unchanged excretion, its general dosing framework, contraindications and interaction warnings; importantly, that label does not identify the underlying human PK study, so those values are retained explicitly as label-derived rather than independently replicated PK estimates. 3

The human efficacy section deliberately distinguishes treatment research in clinical populations from enhancement in healthy people. The retrieved literature includes a controlled 400-patient ischemic-stroke rehabilitation report and a 90-patient double-blind placebo-controlled epilepsy add-on trial, while the richer 2010 chronic-cerebral-ischemia trial supplies both positive and null outcomes rather than a one-sided efficacy narrative. 4 5 The newer 2025 Russian meta-analysis synthesized 11 reports and 549 patients, reporting a 16.3-point average MFI-20 reduction after a month of 200 mg/day, but its heterogeneous regional evidence base does not convert these patient findings into evidence of healthy-person cognitive enhancement. 6

For healthy adults, ClinicalTrials.gov presently has a completed 2026 study that included phenylpiracetam among several active compounds, enrolled 70 participants overall, used 100/200/300-mg phenylpiracetam conditions, and had no posted results as of the October 4, 2026 research date. The registry was first posted October 1, 2026, after the September 2026 completion dates, and the active conditions were small, so it is represented as an unresolved evidence gap rather than efficacy evidence. 7

The stereochemical and dopaminergic records remain explicitly preclinical. The 2011 enantiomer comparison found stronger memory-related activity for R-phenotropil than S-phenotropil in mice despite similar brain concentrations. 8 MRZ-9547/R-phenylpiracetam was subsequently characterized as a selective dopamine-transporter inhibitor that increased striatal dopamine in rats; that study was funded by Merz Pharmaceuticals and disclosed Merz employment/consulting relationships, which are preserved in the reference metadata rather than treated as proof for or against the result. 9 The 2020 R-enantiomer work further reported mouse-brain penetration and DAT target profiling, with institutional metadata identifying an internal Latvian Institute of Organic Synthesis grant and target-profiling data supplied by JSC Olainfarm; because the publisher disclosure itself was not independently inspected, its sponsorship status remains not-assessed. 10

Regulatory context is deliberately activity- and jurisdiction-specific. FDA's 2019 warning letter treated the particular phenylpiracetam products marketed with therapeutic/structure-function claims as unapproved new drugs and misbranded drugs; the article does not turn that enforcement action into a claim that simple U.S. possession is federally prohibited. 11 For sport, FINCIS/SUEK's current database states that its classification is based on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List and lists fonturacetam/4-phenylpiracetam/carphedon among S6.A non-specified stimulants prohibited in competition, with relevant optical isomers included. 12 A 2025 Official Medicines Control Laboratory surveillance project additionally documented phenylpiracetam in unauthorized-market surveillance and bulk raw-material interceptions, supporting a product-quality caution rather than an efficacy conclusion. 13

Open questions and limitations

Several older Russian clinical publications were available only through English abstracts or regional mirrors, so arm-level numerical results, randomization details, adverse-event denominators and publication disclosures could not always be reconstructed without over-inference; those fields remain explicitly unassessed instead of being imputed. 4 5 The current Russian label's human PK values likewise lack a traceable source study, preventing population-specific PK modeling beyond the stated 3–5-hour label range. 3

No posted result presently resolves the central healthy-enhancement question, and animal DAT findings cannot bridge that gap: the current healthy-volunteer registry has no results, whereas direct DAT evidence comes from in-vitro and rodent work on stereochemically defined R-phenylpiracetam/MRZ-9547. 7 9 The supplied schema, canonical IDs, topic brief and existing phenylpiracetam article were available and used; a BHWiki repository/runtime for the requested bun run content:validate and bun run test commands was not among the supplied materials, so repository-level validation is not claimed. 1
