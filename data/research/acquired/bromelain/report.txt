Bromelain: Deep Research and BHWiki Source Package
Executive synthesis

Bromelain is not one chemically discrete molecule. It is a family/mixture of pineapple-derived proteolytic enzymes whose composition depends materially on plant part and preparation. Stem bromelain contains multiple catalytically active papain-family cysteine proteases; fruit bromelain is biochemically distinct, with classic work on fruit bromelain FA2 reporting a roughly 31-kDa enzyme with properties different from stem bromelain. A single molecular formula, molecular weight, SMILES string, or PubChem CID is therefore inappropriate for the umbrella ingredient “bromelain.” 
1

This heterogeneity has practical consequences. Milligrams of “bromelain” are not equivalent to enzymatic potency, and GDU/FIP/CDU-type activity claims depend on the assay, substrate, conditions, and authenticity of the protease preparation. A 2026 analytical study of four commercial bromelain raw materials found that only one satisfied both identity and activity expectations; two had essentially no activity, while another met its nominal proteolytic-activity specification because it contained papain rather than authentic bromelain. The investigators therefore concluded that activity testing alone is insufficient and demonstrated a workflow combining functional activity, inhibitor profiling, total-protein measurement, SDS-PAGE, and bromelain-/papain-specific immunoblotting. 
2

For oral pharmacokinetics, the strongest classic human evidence is a 19-man study showing that a small amount of orally administered stem bromelain can reach plasma in immunoreactive and partly proteolytically active form. Its estimated plasma half-life was approximately 6–9 hours, with circulating material associated with α2-macroglobulin and α1-antichymotrypsin. This establishes limited systemic absorption, but it does not establish the frequently repeated claim of a precise high percentage of human oral bioavailability; absolute oral bioavailability remains unestablished. A separate human study using 500 mg twice daily for 30 days detected bromelain in serum and sinonasal tissue, supporting tissue penetration but not providing a robust absolute bioavailability estimate. 
3

The best modern oral clinical signal is in short-term postoperative inflammation after mandibular third-molar surgery, but results are not uniformly positive. A 2014 randomized trial found less pain with 1,000 mg/day but did not show significant swelling or trismus benefits; a 2017 trial using 40 mg every six hours found no significant swelling benefit from bromelain alone; and a small 2024 double-blind three-arm trial found materially lower pain and swelling with purified bromelain than placebo while again finding no significant trismus difference. Meta-analyses have generally favored bromelain for some pain/swelling endpoints but disagree on effect size and consistency, particularly for trismus and early postoperative pain. 
4
 
5

A notable newer finding is a 2025 triple-blind randomized trial in mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis. Seventy participants were randomized to bromelain 200 mg twice daily or placebo for eight weeks. The bromelain group had a larger improvement in Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index (SCCAI) score, −3.29 ± 2.17 versus −1.11 ± 1.71, with p<0.001; however, the between-group quality-of-life change was null, and the study did not perform confirmatory colonoscopic/endoscopic assessment. The authors appropriately characterize the result as preliminary. 
6
 
7

Evidence for sinusitis is substantially older and weaker. Several trials from the 1960s reported positive adjunctive findings, but the original reports provide limited modern methodological detail. A systematic review found encouraging signals in acute rhinosinusitis while judging the overall evidence limited.

The clearest regulatory efficacy evidence concerns topical anacaulase-bcdb/NexoBrid, not oral bromelain supplements. The current U.S. label describes an 8.8% topical product whose drug substance is a pineapple-stem-derived mixture containing stem bromelain, ananain and other proteins. In the DETECT trial, ≥95% eschar removal occurred in 93% of NexoBrid-treated subjects versus 4% with gel vehicle; surgical eschar removal occurred in 4% versus 72% with standard care. FDA originally approved NexoBrid on December 28, 2022; it is also authorized in the EU for deep partial- and full-thickness thermal-burn eschar removal. These findings cannot be extrapolated to oral bromelain supplements. 
8

Safety data are generally reassuring for short oral research exposures but are not sufficient to establish uncommon or long-term risks. Hypersensitivity is biologically and clinically credible, including occupational IgE-mediated reactions and current NexoBrid contraindications for bromelain/pineapple hypersensitivity and papaya/papain cross-sensitivity. Bromelain can inhibit platelet aggregation or alter coagulation measures in laboratory/ex-vivo experiments, but that evidence is much stronger than evidence for clinically important bleeding from routine oral supplementation; the regulatory label therefore uses a precautionary, rather than quantified clinical-risk, framing. Pregnancy and lactation data are inadequate. 
9

Biochemistry, standardization, and pharmacokinetics

Stem and fruit preparations should be treated as related but non-interchangeable protease systems. Fruit bromelain FA2 was purified as a distinct fruit-juice proteinase, while subsequent stem work resolved numerous catalytically active forms rather than one uniform enzyme. Commercial “bromelain” can additionally contain nonprotease proteins, inhibitors, glycans, carbohydrates and low-molecular-weight components; the current FDA label for anacaulase-bcdb, for example, describes stem bromelain alongside ananain, a jacalin-like lectin, bromelain inhibitors and phytocystatin inhibitor. 
1
 
9

Extraction is correspondingly source- and purpose-dependent. Classical purification uses precipitation and chromatography, while contemporary work includes aqueous two-phase extraction, membrane-based methods and lyophilization. For quality control, a proteolytic activity number should be interpreted as an assay result, not an identity test. The 2026 authentication study used a pGlu-Phe-Leu-p-nitroanilide chromogenic assay calibrated to an FIP standard, inhibitor-response profiling, BCA total-protein analysis, SDS-PAGE and specific Western blots; papain could otherwise mimic nominal bromelain activity. 
10

This also explains why a universal conversion between mg, GDU, FIP and CDU should not be hard-coded. The 2024 oral-surgery study used the same nominal 200 mg bromelain dose in its purified-bromelain and pineapple-extract arms while reporting very different product activity specifications, illustrating that enzyme mass and assay activity are separate attributes. 
5

The human oral PK evidence remains sparse. The Castell study supports a 6–9-hour estimated plasma half-life of the orally absorbed stem-bromelain analyte, but only small circulating amounts were detected and absolute bioavailability was not determined. Passali and colleagues later found serum and sinonasal-mucosal bromelain after 500 mg twice daily for 30 days. In contrast, the 12 ± 4.4-hour terminal half-life in the current NexoBrid label follows topical application to damaged burn surfaces and is formulation/wound specific; its median serum Tmax is four hours. It should not replace the oral PK estimate. 
3
 
9

Safety, interactions, and regulatory status

Short oral trials generally report few serious adverse events, but absence of rare events in small trials is not evidence of long-term safety. A systematic review found gastrointestinal complaints such as flatulence or nausea and headache among reported oral adverse events while not identifying a strong major-toxicity signal across the included studies; the evidence base remained heterogeneous. In the 2024 oral-surgery trial no treatment-related adverse effects were reported, but only 42 subjects were analyzed for seven days.

Mechanism map and research gaps

The mechanistic literature supports genuine proteolytic and immunologic activity but is much stronger at the biochemical, cellular, ex-vivo and animal levels than at the level of demonstrated human clinical mediation. Bromelain can proteolytically alter leukocyte surface proteins involved in adhesion and migration; ex-vivo inflamed human intestinal biopsies show reduced release of several inflammatory cytokines/chemokines after bromelain treatment; cellular systems show NF-κB/MAPK modulation. Platelet aggregation/coagulation effects are also measurable in laboratory systems. None of those findings, by itself, proves that the same mechanism causes a specific oral clinical benefit at supplement exposures.

Systemic mechanism uncertainty

The priority evidence gaps are unusually clear. Human absolute oral bioavailability has not been established; modern mass-balance, dose-proportionality, metabolism and excretion studies are lacking. Preparations need source-part identity, protease composition and validated activity assays, rather than mg-only labeling. Larger third-molar trials should prespecify pain, edema and trismus endpoints and report effect estimates with confidence intervals. The 2025 UC finding needs independent replication with endoscopic, histologic and biomarker outcomes. Legacy sinusitis evidence needs modern replication. Long-duration safety, pregnancy/lactation data and prospectively measured bleeding/DDI outcomes remain deficient. Finally, bromelain-only trials must remain analytically separate from multi-enzyme products such as “systemic proteolytic enzyme” combinations, because effects from mixtures cannot be attributed to bromelain alone. 
6

## Summary

Bromelain is an umbrella term for pineapple-derived proteolytic preparations rather than one chemically discrete molecule. Stem and fruit preparations contain different cysteine-protease profiles and other co-extracted constituents, so milligram amounts alone do not define biological potency. Proteolytic activity is reported with assay-dependent units such as GDU, FIP or CDU; these values should not be treated as universally interchangeable without the underlying assay method.

Human oral pharmacokinetic evidence shows that a small amount of orally administered stem bromelain can reach plasma in intact, partly proteolytically active form. The best directly measured human oral estimate places the plasma elimination half-life at approximately 6–9 hours, but absolute oral bioavailability, complete metabolism and excretion remain inadequately characterized.

Clinical evidence is indication- and formulation-specific. Short oral randomized trials after third-molar surgery show a recurring signal for reduced postoperative pain and sometimes swelling, but swelling and trismus findings are inconsistent. A 2025 randomized placebo-controlled trial in mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis reported improved symptom-based disease activity but no between-group quality-of-life benefit and did not include confirmatory endoscopy. Historical sinusitis studies are old and methodologically limited. The strongest regulatory efficacy evidence concerns the distinct topical prescription product anacaulase-bcdb (NexoBrid) for enzymatic burn-eschar removal and must not be generalized to oral supplements.

## Description

Commercial bromelain can originate from pineapple stem, fruit, core or other processing streams. Classical biochemical work distinguishes stem bromelain from fruit bromelain, and modern stem fractionation has resolved multiple catalytically active protease forms. The umbrella material may also contain nonprotease proteins, inhibitors, carbohydrates and small molecules. The current U.S. label for the pineapple-stem-derived botanical drug anacaulase-bcdb describes stem bromelain, ananain, a jacalin-like lectin, bromelain inhibitors and phytocystatin inhibitor among its major protein components.

Because bromelain is a heterogeneous enzyme preparation, identity, purity and potency require more than a mass specification. Proteolytic activity measurements depend on substrate and assay conditions, and unrelated proteases can mimic activity. A 2026 quality study found inactive commercial raw materials and a nominally active material whose activity was attributable to papain; an integrated approach using enzymatic activity, inhibitor profiling, protein analysis and protease-specific immunodetection was needed to authenticate bromelain.

Proposed biological actions include direct proteolysis, modulation of leukocyte surface proteins and inflammatory mediators, laboratory platelet/coagulation effects, and product-specific enzymatic digestion of burn eschar. Mechanistic translation from cell, ex-vivo or animal experiments to clinical oral outcomes remains incomplete.

## Evidence note

The evidence base is heterogeneous in product identity, enzyme activity, dose, comparator and endpoint definition. Many older publications do not report modern randomization, allocation, activity-standardization or disclosure detail. Modern third-molar studies include both positive and null endpoints: postoperative pain is the most reproducible signal, swelling is less consistent, and trismus often remains nonsignificant. The 2025 ulcerative-colitis trial is an important new human result but remains a single-center preliminary trial using a symptom index rather than objective endoscopic confirmation.

Statements about systemic oral action should be separated from exaggerated bioavailability claims. Direct human evidence supports low-level systemic detection of intact/active stem bromelain and an estimated 6–9 hour plasma half-life, not a precise established percentage of absolute bioavailability. The approximately 12-hour terminal half-life in the NexoBrid label follows topical application to burn wounds and is a different formulation/exposure context.

Short oral trials generally suggest acceptable tolerability, but rare allergy, long-term safety, pregnancy/lactation safety and clinically quantified bleeding interactions remain insufficiently characterized. Laboratory platelet/coagulation effects justify caution but should not be misrepresented as proof of a defined oral major-bleeding risk. Regulatory approval of topical NexoBrid and JECFA food-enzyme evaluation are separate from evidence for therapeutic oral supplement indications.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Heterogeneous cysteine-protease activity
  description: Pineapple-stem bromelain is not one enzyme. Biochemical fractionation resolved multiple catalytically active papain-family cysteine proteases with different physicochemical and substrate properties. Proteolysis is therefore a core biochemical mechanism, but the activity of a commercial preparation depends on its protease composition and assay.
  sourceId: ref-matagne-2017
  conceptId: cysteine-protease-activity

- title: Human inflammatory-tissue cytokine modulation ex vivo
  description: Endoscopic colon biopsies from patients with ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease were treated with bromelain ex vivo. Inflamed tissue showed decreased secretion of several inflammatory mediators, including G-CSF, GM-CSF, IFN-gamma, CCL4/MIP-1beta and TNF. This human-tissue experiment supports biological plausibility but is not equivalent to demonstrating the same effect after an oral clinical dose.
  sourceId: ref-onken-2008
  conceptId: immune-inflammatory-modulation

- title: NF-kappaB and MAPK modulation in human cells
  description: In LPS-stimulated human dental-pulp cells, bromelain reduced IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-8, ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 and reduced NF-kappaB p65, ERK and p38 signaling. This is an in-vitro human-cell mechanism and should not be treated as proof of a clinical anti-inflammatory pathway at oral supplement concentrations.
  sourceId: ref-hong-2021
  conceptId: immune-inflammatory-modulation

- title: Platelet and coagulation modulation in laboratory systems
  description: Human platelet and whole-blood experiments show that bromelain can reduce aggregation and alter coagulation measures ex vivo. A thromboelastography study also found complex and partly paradoxical findings in vivo in mice, emphasizing that laboratory anticoagulant effects do not establish a quantified clinical bleeding effect after oral use.
  sourceId: ref-kaur-2016
  conceptId: hemostasis-modulation

- title: Product-specific enzymatic burn-eschar digestion
  description: The current NexoBrid label states that its proteolytic-enzyme mixture dissolves burn-wound eschar, while the specific individual components responsible have not been identified. This mechanism applies to topical anacaulase-bcdb placed directly on burn eschar and is not evidence that oral bromelain debrides wounds.
  sourceId: ref-dailymed-nexobrid-2025
  conceptId: enzymatic-eschar-debridement
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Bromelain, pineapple and papain/papaya hypersensitivity
  description: IgE-mediated occupational reactions to bromelain have been reported. The current NexoBrid label contraindicates use in patients with known hypersensitivity to anacaulase-bcdb, bromelain, pineapple or other product components and also in patients with papaya/papain hypersensitivity because of cross-sensitivity risk. Postmarketing serious hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis, has been reported for NexoBrid.
  sourceId: ref-dailymed-nexobrid-2025

- title: Bleeding and coagulation uncertainty
  description: Platelet inhibition, fibrinolytic effects and changes in coagulation measures are supported mainly by in-vitro, ex-vivo and animal evidence. The current NexoBrid label advises avoiding use in uncontrolled coagulation disorders and using caution with anticoagulant therapy or other bleeding risks. These sources do not establish a numerical major-bleeding incidence for routine oral bromelain supplementation.
  sourceId: ref-dailymed-nexobrid-2025

- title: Pregnancy evidence is inadequate
  description: Direct human pregnancy safety data for oral bromelain are inadequate. The current NexoBrid label states that human pregnancy data are unavailable; its animal intravenous developmental studies are product- and route-specific and cannot establish oral-supplement safety.
  sourceId: ref-dailymed-nexobrid-2025

- title: Lactation evidence is inadequate
  description: Human milk transfer and effects on breastfed infants have not been established for bromelain. The current NexoBrid label likewise reports no data on anacaulase-bcdb in milk or its effects on the infant or milk production.
  sourceId: ref-dailymed-nexobrid-2025

- title: Product identity and potency can be unreliable
  description: A 2026 analytical study of four commercial bromelain raw materials found two without meaningful enzymatic activity and one whose apparent compliant proteolytic activity was attributable to papain rather than bromelain. Activity units alone therefore do not prove bromelain identity or purity.
  sourceId: ref-benetti-2026

- title: Long-term safety and rare adverse events remain insufficiently characterized
  description: Most controlled oral studies are small and short. Short-term tolerability cannot exclude rare allergic, bleeding, gastrointestinal or other adverse events, and long-duration high-dose safety has not been adequately established.
  sourceId: ref-leelakanok-2023

- title: Dependence, tolerance and withdrawal are not established clinical phenomena
  description: The reviewed bromelain clinical literature does not provide a developed evidence base for pharmacologic dependence, a reproducible withdrawal syndrome or clinically meaningful tolerance. This is an evidence gap rather than proof that every long-term exposure is risk-free.
  sourceId: ref-leelakanok-2023
```

## Description

Laboratory human-blood studies support bromelain-sensitive platelet and coagulation effects, while animal findings and the absence of definitive oral clinical interaction studies make clinical translation uncertain.


Coverage is strongest for biochemical identity, oral human absorption, third-molar surgery, the new ulcerative-colitis trial, topical NexoBrid regulation, and current quality-control concerns. Important remaining access/evidence limitations are the sparse disclosure information in several older primary papers, incomplete primary-text recovery for the 1960s sinusitis trials and 1978 tetracycline study, and the absence of robust modern human mass-balance, absolute-bioavailability, pregnancy/lactation, long-term-safety and prospectively quantified bleeding-interaction studies.
