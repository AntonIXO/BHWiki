Phenibut Deep Research
Executive summary

Phenibut is 4-amino-3-phenylbutanoic acid (β-phenyl-γ-aminobutyric acid), a chiral synthetic analogue of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA. The BHWiki identity supplied in the research brief—formula C10H13NO2, molecular weight 179.22 g/mol, PubChem CID 14113, and the specified connectivity SMILES—was retained, with the parent compound kept distinct from phenibut hydrochloride and commercial formulations such as Noofen and Anvifen. The attached brief was treated as the governing BHWiki schema and editorial contract. 
1

The pharmacology is better established than the clinical efficacy. In preclinical receptor and animal experiments, R-phenibut accounts for most GABA-B-receptor activity: racemic phenibut and R-phenibut had reported GABA-B binding affinities of 177 ± 2 and 92 ± 3 µM, respectively, while baclofen was substantially more potent at 6.0 ± 1 µM. Selected R-phenibut behavioral effects were blocked by the GABA-B antagonist CGP35348. 
2
 Separately, rat-brain membrane experiments found that both R- and S-phenibut bind the α2δ subunit of voltage-dependent calcium channels, with reported Ki values of 23 and 39 µM; rodent pain experiments supported an α2δ-associated component of its antinociceptive pharmacology. 
3
 These results support molecular mechanism edges, but they are not evidence that phenibut is an effective human anxiolytic, sleep medication, cognitive enhancer, or analgesic.

The human efficacy literature is considerably weaker than common online descriptions imply. A small randomized 53-patient add-on study in people with cerebrovascular disease and persistent fatigue reported improvements in fatigue and Schulte-test cognitive performance, but the accessible abstract does not supply a verified dose, placebo/blinding details, or quantitative between-group effect estimates. 
4
 An uncontrolled 2022 post-COVID study reported pre/post improvements in HADS anxiety, subjective sleep, MoCA, TMT-B and fatigue after Anvifen, but lacked a concurrent comparator. 
5
 A more recent 2025 Latvian adjustment-disorder study found substantial ADNM-20 symptom improvement after three weeks of Noofen, but it too lacked a concurrent control and was funded by Noofen manufacturer JSC Olpha; two authors were Olpha employees and another disclosed financial relationships including Olpha. 
6
 No exact phenibut interventional study was returned by the ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search conducted for this review. 
7

The most convincing human evidence concerns harm rather than benefit. A regional poison-centre series recorded 56 phenibut exposure calls, with more than half showing CNS effects, 35.7% having documented coingestants, and 11 patients requiring intubation; this is a selected poison-centre population and is not a 19.6% risk estimate for all users. 
8
 A systematic review identified 62 published toxicity/withdrawal cases and described altered mental status, somnolence, psychosis and movement disorders in toxicity, while withdrawal commonly involved anxiety, agitation, insomnia and psychosis. 
9
 Another systematic withdrawal review found that its entire evidence base consisted of case reports or conference abstracts and documented severe manifestations including seizures, intubation and ICU admission; consequently, published case-management strategies cannot be treated as comparative evidence or converted into self-treatment protocols. 
10

A major finding of this research is that the often-repeated numeric pharmacokinetic story is less secure than it appears. The primary human study located was an open randomized crossover bioequivalence experiment in 18 healthy volunteers, measuring unchanged aminophenylbutyric acid in plasma after single oral administration and concluding that Anvifen capsules and Phenibut tablets were bioequivalent. Its accessible publication record does not report the numerical elimination half-life, absolute bioavailability, renal unchanged fraction, Tmax, Cmax, or sufficient metabolic information needed to validate commonly repeated secondary estimates. 
11
 The BHWiki deliverable therefore records the elimination half-life as not established from an accessible primary human source rather than propagating a secondary-source number.

Evidence synthesis

The clinical literature separates naturally into three tiers: modern but uncontrolled observational work; a small amount of older controlled regional research; and a much larger body of case-based toxicology. That distribution matters because a drug can have a clear receptor mechanism and clear ability to produce dependence while still having uncertain therapeutic efficacy.

These published quantities are research exposures, not recommendations. The 2020 systematic safety review described 0.25–2 g/day as the therapeutic-dose context represented in the older literature, while dependence and intoxication case reports frequently involved much larger and highly variable exposures. 
15
 The article file preserves this distinction explicitly so that the structured “Doses” cards do not become an informal recreational-use guide.

Taken as a whole, the evidence does not support equating “used clinically in Eastern Europe” with “proven by modern placebo-controlled trials.” The most recent positive efficacy paper itself is observational rather than randomized, and manufacturer involvement is substantial. 
6
 Conversely, the small randomized cerebrovascular study means it would also be inaccurate to say that no controlled human evidence whatsoever exists. 
4
 The defensible conclusion is that controlled human efficacy evidence exists but is sparse, indication-specific, regionally concentrated, and insufficient for broad anxiety/sleep/nootropic claims.

Pharmacology and pharmacokinetics

Phenibut should not be collapsed into a generic “GABA supplement.” The parent molecule is a β-phenyl-substituted GABA analogue and is chiral. The classic 2008 stereochemistry study found that pharmacological activity in several tested systems depended predominantly on the R-enantiomer. S-phenibut was inactive in several behavioral tests up to 500 mg/kg, while R-phenibut was approximately twice as potent as the racemate in most tests; the same study linked this stereoselectivity to GABA-B receptor affinity. 
2

The later α2δ work makes the mechanism more nuanced. R-phenibut, S-phenibut, baclofen and gabapentin had reported α2δ binding Ki values of 23, 39, 156 and 0.05 µM, respectively, in rat-brain membrane preparations. The authors found that R-phenibut's antinociceptive effects in their formalin and chronic-constriction-injury models were not blocked by a GABA-B antagonist and interpreted this as evidence for α2δ-mediated effects in those models. 
3
 This is why the companion concept graph contains separate gaba-b-receptor and alpha2delta-vgcc targets rather than reducing phenibut to a single mechanism.

The human PK record is much thinner. The 2011 randomized crossover study is useful because it directly measured unchanged aminophenylbutyric acid in human plasma after oral pharmaceutical formulations, but the accessible report only establishes bioequivalence and does not expose the numerical values needed for a trustworthy kinetic model.

Safety, dependence, withdrawal, and toxicity

The safety record has two very different faces. The 2020 systematic review of older clinical trials found adverse events reported in 5.66% of 583 trial participants, with somnolence the most frequently reported event at 1.89%. 
15
 That finding should be interpreted cautiously because it aggregates heterogeneous older studies with uncertain adverse-event ascertainment. It should not be treated as a universal modern incidence estimate.

The poison-centre and case-report literature demonstrates that severe presentations can occur. The Minnesota Poison Control System identified 56 exposure calls over 19 years, 48 of them in the final five years of the observation period. More than half involved CNS effects, withdrawal concerns were documented in 10.7%, coingestants in 35.7%, and 11 patients were intubated; no phenibut-attributed deaths occurred in that particular regional series. 
8
 Because patients prompting poison-centre calls are not a representative sample of all exposed people, these fractions measure the composition of reported cases rather than individual-use probabilities.

The broader systematic case review found 62 published toxicity/withdrawal cases; 63.2% reported concomitant use of other addictive substances, with alcohol and benzodiazepines among the most commonly combined drugs. Toxicity presentations commonly included altered mental status, somnolence, psychosis and movement disorders. 
9
 This provides a basis for a co-exposure warning, not for claiming that every specific benzodiazepine–phenibut pair has been studied experimentally.

Withdrawal is a particularly important risk because abrupt reduction after repeated use can produce a syndrome qualitatively opposite to phenibut's acute depressant effects. The 2023 withdrawal review found published reports of anxiety, insomnia, agitation, hallucinations, delirium and seizures; withdrawal had been described as early as two hours after the last dose in the case literature, although dose histories and product composition were often uncertain. The authors explicitly noted that every included report was a case report or conference abstract and that publication bias and unrecognized polysubstance use were major limitations.

The legal picture is jurisdiction-specific. It is especially important not to translate “not internationally controlled” into “legal everywhere,” or FDA dietary-supplement status into federal controlled-substance status.

The U.S. position is particularly well documented. FDA first publicly acted against phenibut-labelled dietary supplements in 2019 because phenibut does not satisfy the statutory definition of a dietary ingredient.

29

Australia is the clearest prohibition among the requested jurisdictions. Government import guidance explicitly calls phenibut Schedule 9, while TGA explains that Schedule 9 is intended for substances with a high propensity for dependence/abuse that should generally be limited to approved analytical, medical or scientific research contexts.

Funding, conflicts of interest, and evidence quality

Commercial influence was assessed source by source rather than inferred from author affiliation alone. The clearest industry-funded efficacy publication is the 2025 Noofen adjustment-disorder paper: its funding statement says JSC Olpha supported the study, Guna Dansone and Yulia Troshina were JSC Olpha employees, and Elmārs Tērauds disclosed financial benefits from several pharmaceutical companies including Olpha. 
6
 This does not invalidate the observations, but the combination of commercial sponsorship, declared company employment, and an uncontrolled before/after design substantially limits causal confidence.

By contrast, the 2020 systematic safety review reports support through the EU-ERA-NET NEURON project TRAINS and its PubMed record states that the authors declared no conflicts of interest. 
30
 Its conclusions are nevertheless constrained by the quality of the underlying older trials and case reports.

For the foundational GABA-B and α2δ experiments, PubMed indexes non-U.S.-government research support, but the exact funder and full COI disclosures were not verified from accessible full-text declarations in this run. 
2
 
3
 They are therefore marked not assessed for exact sponsorship rather than being labeled independent.

The later online-product analysis offers a useful example of why funding and conflicts are encoded separately. The article says it received no external funding, but one author was an employee of Noofen manufacturer JSC Olpha and Olpha supplied phenibut hydrochloride reference material. 
18
 The generated article records the declared employment conflict rather than treating “no external funding” as evidence of complete commercial independence.

For several older Russian-language clinical papers, only abstracts were accessible through the sources inspected. Those records are coded not assessed for funding/COI, not “none declared.” This follows the brief's requirement that absence of accessible disclosure must not be mistaken for evidence of independence.

Coverage and access gaps

The largest unresolved scientific gap is primary human pharmacokinetics. A human randomized crossover study was located, but the accessible report did not expose enough numerical PK information to validate the familiar half-life, renal-excretion, onset or duration figures found in secondary summaries. 
11
 The generated BHWiki page therefore uses an explicit nonnumeric PK observation with modelEligible: false rather than fabricating precision.

The second major gap is high-quality efficacy evidence. Controlled human research is not literally absent, but modern placebo-controlled evidence for anxiety, insomnia, or healthy-person cognitive enhancement was not identified. The newest positive human study is uncontrolled and commercially sponsored. 
4
 
6

The final gap is regulatory. Australia and the U.S. dietary-supplement position are well supported by current official sources, while this run did not obtain sufficiently specific official records to populate definitive BHWiki legal entries for EU-wide status, current Russian registration, or phenibut-specific Canadian status. Rather than filling those cells from secondary legality websites, the article leaves those jurisdictional records out and documents the uncertainty in the companion research report.
