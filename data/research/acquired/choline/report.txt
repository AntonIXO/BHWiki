## Summary

Choline is an essential nutrient required for phospholipid, acetylcholine, and methyl-group metabolism. Controlled depletion can cause reversible liver or muscle injury, whereas evidence for benefits above nutritional adequacy—especially cognition and cardiometabolic outcomes—is formulation- and context-dependent.

## Description

Choline (PubChem CID 305; C5H14NO+, 104.17 g/mol) is a permanently charged quaternary ammonium nutrient. Foods supply free choline plus phosphocholine, glycerophosphocholine, phosphatidylcholine, and sphingomyelin; selected NIH values include beef liver 356 mg/3 oz, one large egg 147 mg, and roasted soybeans 107 mg/½ cup. Choline supports phospholipid synthesis, acetylcholine production, and betaine-dependent one-carbon metabolism. Dietary choline, choline salts, and phosphatidylcholine are exposure forms, whereas citicoline (CDP-choline) and alpha-GPC are separate choline-containing compounds with separate BHWiki slugs; efficacy should not be transferred between them. Gut microbes can convert susceptible choline substrates to trimethylamine and subsequently TMAO. Sources: pubchem-choline; nih-ods-choline; efsa-2016-choline-drvs.

## Evidence note

High confidence applies to nutrient essentiality, deficiency-related liver/muscle injury, and established dietary reference values. Prenatal cognition evidence is promising but inconsistent and derives partly from small trials; adult cognitive supplementation evidence is sparse. Choline-bitartrate can raise TMAO and platelet reactivity, but formulation matters and downstream TMAO associations do not establish cardiovascular harm from dietary choline. No general elimination half-life is established. Funding/COI classifications are assessed only where declarations were inspected. Sources: fischer-2007; caudill-2018; bahnfleth-2022; cheatham-2012; wilcox-2021; jia-2019; yoo-2026.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: CDP-choline phosphatidylcholine synthesis
  description: "Free choline can be phosphorylated by choline kinase and enter the CDP-choline/Kennedy pathway; PCYT1A catalyzes a regulatory cytidylyltransferase step leading toward phosphatidylcholine, a major membrane and lipoprotein phospholipid."
  sourceId: efsa-2016-choline-drvs
  conceptId: phosphatidylcholine-biosynthesis

- title: Endogenous PEMT phosphatidylcholine synthesis
  description: "PEMT provides a hepatic de novo phosphatidylcholine-synthesis route from phosphatidylethanolamine. Estrogen-responsive PEMT activity is one reason endogenous choline-related phospholipid synthesis and dietary requirement can differ by sex and reproductive state."
  sourceId: fischer-2007
  conceptId: phosphatidylcholine-biosynthesis

- title: Acetylcholine synthesis
  description: "Choline is a direct substrate for choline acetyltransferase, which forms acetylcholine from choline and acetyl-CoA; this biochemical precursor role does not by itself establish cognitive enhancement from supplemental choline."
  sourceId: efsa-2016-choline-drvs
  conceptId: choline-precursor

- title: Betaine and one-carbon metabolism
  description: "Choline oxidation supplies betaine-related one-carbon metabolism. Betaine can donate a methyl group through BHMT to remethylate homocysteine to methionine, linking choline status with methyl-group metabolism."
  sourceId: efsa-2016-choline-drvs
  conceptId: betaine-one-carbon-metabolism

- title: Gut microbial trimethylamine and host TMAO generation
  description: "Gut microbes can metabolize susceptible choline-containing substrates to trimethylamine; host oxidation, principally involving FMO3, generates TMAO. Human TMAO production varies with choline source, formulation, microbiota, and host context."
  sourceId: tang-2013
  conceptId: gut-microbial-tmao-generation

- title: Analytical quantification by isotope-dilution LC-MS/MS
  description: "Human choline/TMAO studies commonly use chromatographic mass-spectrometric methods, including stable-isotope dilution LC-MS/MS, to distinguish choline, TMAO, betaine, dimethylglycine, and labeled isotopologues in plasma or urine."
  sourceId: wilcox-2021
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Choline deficiency can injure liver or skeletal muscle
  description: "Controlled human depletion produced fatty liver and/or biochemical muscle injury in a substantial proportion of participants, with susceptibility differing by sex and menopausal status. Deliberately inducing low-choline intake is not a safe self-experiment."
  sourceId: fischer-2007

- title: High chronic intakes can produce adverse effects
  description: "NIH ODS identifies fishy body odor, vomiting, excessive sweating and salivation, hypotension, and liver toxicity among effects associated with excessive choline intake. The U.S. adult UL is 3,500 mg/day; lower age-specific ULs apply to minors."
  sourceId: nih-ods-choline

- title: Pregnancy trial doses are not established optimal intake targets
  description: "Small controlled-feeding studies found some offspring cognitive advantages with 930 versus 480 mg/day maternal total choline, but another phosphatidylcholine trial was null. These findings do not establish a universal pregnancy dose above the U.S. or EFSA AI."
  sourceId: yoo-2026

- title: TMAO effects are formulation-specific and not equivalent to proven cardiovascular harm
  description: "A 28-day trial found higher fasting TMAO and platelet reactivity with choline bitartrate but not whole eggs or phosphatidylcholine. TMAO predicts cardiovascular risk in observational cohorts, while causal inference remains contested and subject to reverse causality."
  sourceId: wilcox-2021

- title: Preserve choline-equivalent versus compound mass
  description: "A salt, phospholipid, citicoline, alpha-GPC, and free choline are not interchangeable mass units. Study interpretation should retain the exact ingredient, formulation, salt or phospholipid mass, and choline-equivalent amount when reported."
  sourceId: wilcox-2021
```

## Description

Schema-native directed relationship graph linking choline to phosphatidylcholine synthesis, acetylcholine synthesis, betaine-dependent one-carbon metabolism, and the microbiome-dependent TMA/TMAO route. The graph represents biochemical routing only and does not assert that any downstream metabolite or mechanism causes a clinical cognitive or cardiovascular benefit or harm.

Coverage and validation note

The molecular identity is anchored to the current PubChem record for CID 305, which reports C5H14NO+ and molecular weight 104.17 g/mol. 
1
 Nutritional-reference records preserve jurisdictional differences rather than collapsing them: EFSA specifies 400 mg/day for adults, 480 mg/day during pregnancy, and 520 mg/day during lactation, while U.S. pregnancy guidance uses 450 mg/day; FDA’s current nutrition-label Daily Value is 550 mg. 
2

The clinical evidence is deliberately separated by endpoint and formulation. Controlled dietary depletion caused fatty liver or muscle damage in 77% of men, 80% of postmenopausal women, and 44% of premenopausal women, supporting human nutritional essentiality. 
3
 Choline chloride also reversed hepatic abnormalities in a small randomized long-term-TPN trial. 
4
 In pregnancy research, the controlled-feeding 480-versus-930 mg/day study produced positive infant processing-speed and seven-year attention findings, but a larger phosphatidylcholine trial reported no significant infant cognitive advantage, so the article does not promote 930 mg/day as an established optimal pregnancy dose. 
5
 
6
 
7
 The corresponding registry confirms that the controlled-feeding pregnancy arms used 480 and 930 mg/day total choline with choline chloride supplementation. 
8

The TMAO treatment is likewise biomarker-specific rather than causal shorthand. In the 28-day randomized source-comparison study, choline-bitartrate-containing arms raised fasting TMAO and platelet reactivity, whereas whole eggs and phosphatidylcholine did not significantly do so. 
9
 The registry independently documents those five formulation arms and TMAO/platelet endpoints. 
10
 Higher TMAO was associated with later cardiovascular events in Tang et al., but that was not proof that dietary choline caused those events; bidirectional Mendelian-randomization evidence did not support genetically predicted TMAO as a cause of the tested cardiovascular endpoints after multiple-testing correction and identified evidence compatible with reverse causality from diabetes or kidney disease. 
11
 
12

The most recent broad evidence map located during this research, published in September 2026, included 117 primary human studies and judged evidence insufficient or equivocal for many health outcomes despite signals of possible maternal-choline benefit for offspring neurodevelopment.
