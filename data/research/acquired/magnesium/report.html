Magnesium — Deep Research and BHWiki Source

Executive summary

Magnesium is an essential element (Mg; PubChem CID 5462224; molecular weight 24.305 g/mol) whose physiologically important form is predominantly Mg²⁺. PubChem identifies elemental magnesium with formula Mg and canonical SMILES [Mg]. 1 In human physiology, magnesium is required for hundreds of enzyme systems, energy metabolism, protein and nucleic-acid synthesis, neuromuscular function, glucose regulation, blood-pressure control, ion transport, and bone structure. An adult body contains about 25 g magnesium, roughly 50%–60% in bone, while less than 1% resides in serum. NIH ODS therefore cautions that serum magnesium is convenient but correlates poorly with total-body or tissue magnesium; it explicitly states that no single status-assessment method is satisfactory. 2

The most important interpretive distinction is deficiency/repletion versus supplementation of magnesium-replete people. The kidneys strongly conserve magnesium when status is low, and hypomagnesemia can cause weakness, neuromuscular irritability, seizures, arrhythmia, and secondary electrolyte abnormalities. People with gastrointestinal losses or malabsorption, diabetes, chronic alcohol exposure, certain medication exposures, and older age have increased deficiency risk. 2 Human genetics reinforces the biological importance of magnesium handling: TRPM6 mutations cause hereditary hypomagnesemia with secondary hypocalcemia; TRPM7 has a central experimental role in cellular Mg²⁺ homeostasis; SLC41A1 mediates Na⁺-dependent Mg²⁺ extrusion in human-cell experiments; and CNNM2 mutations cause dominant renal hypomagnesemia. 3 4 5 6

For clinical outcomes, the evidence is not uniform. A 2025 meta-analysis of 38 randomized trials involving 2,709 participants found average reductions of about 2.81 mmHg systolic and 2.05 mmHg diastolic, with larger reductions in hypomagnesemic and treated-hypertensive subgroups but no statistically significant effect in normotensive groups; heterogeneity was high. 7 A 2026 cardiometabolic meta-analysis of 78 randomized trials likewise found modest pooled improvements in blood pressure and several glycemic measures, while emphasizing that clinical relevance and certainty varied across outcomes. 8 FDA's regulatory framing remains deliberately cautious: its magnesium/high-blood-pressure qualified health claim must communicate that the evidence is inconsistent and inconclusive. 9

Sleep evidence is especially formulation- and population-dependent. A small older-adult magnesium-oxide trial reported improvement in several insomnia measures; a 2025 magnesium-bisglycinate trial found a statistically significant but small Insomnia Severity Index effect (d = 0.2); and magnesium-L-threonate studies have yielded mixtures of positive subjective or secondary outcomes and null primary/objective sleep outcomes. 10 11 12 The L-threonate literature is also commercially entangled, making independent replication particularly important. The article source below therefore avoids presenting magnesium as an established routine insomnia treatment.

Supplement chemistry matters. Magnesium citrate has repeatedly produced higher absorption-related responses than magnesium oxide in comparative human studies, despite oxide's much higher theoretical elemental-magnesium percentage. 13 14 This is why salt mass, elemental-magnesium mass, and bioavailability must never be treated as interchangeable quantities.

Safety is similarly context-dependent. The U.S. adult supplemental/medication UL is 350 mg/day elemental magnesium, whereas the RDA is 400–420 mg/day for adult men and 310–320 mg/day for adult women because the RDA includes food and the UL does not. High supplemental doses commonly produce diarrhea, nausea, and cramping; severe hypermagnesemia can cause hypotension, neuromuscular depression, respiratory compromise, arrhythmia, and cardiac arrest, with impaired renal function being the major risk amplifier. 2 Magnesium also interacts with oral bisphosphonates and tetracycline/quinolone antibiotics through absorption interference; diuretics can change renal magnesium losses; and prolonged proton-pump-inhibitor exposure can cause clinically important hypomagnesemia. 2

Pregnancy requires a separate medical distinction: ordinary nutritional magnesium is not interchangeable with parenteral magnesium sulfate used for pre-eclampsia/eclampsia. WHO states that magnesium-sulfate injections in pre-eclampsia can reduce the risk of eclampsia by more than half. 15

Analytical synthesis

The resulting BHWiki entry deliberately models magnesium as an essential nutrient/homeostatic ion rather than a conventional drug molecule. No universal elimination half-life was assigned. That is important because magnesium resides in multiple body pools with different exchange kinetics; a half-life measured for a particular parenteral salt, serum compartment, or clinical protocol would not legitimately become the half-life of “magnesium” as a nutrient. NIH's description of tightly regulated serum concentrations, bone/soft-tissue distribution, renal conservation, and variable gastrointestinal absorption supports this conservative representation. 2

The supplement-form table distinguishes theoretical elemental fraction from absorption. Magnesium oxide is approximately 60.3% elemental magnesium by anhydrous formula, yet older and newer comparative studies generally find citrate more bioavailable. Lindberg and colleagues found citrate much more soluble and associated with a much larger post-dose urinary-magnesium response than oxide; Walker and colleagues likewise found citrate superior to oxide over 60 days. 13 14 Chloride is relatively soluble but its elemental fraction depends strongly on hydration state. Bisglycinate's clinical sleep evidence is still too limited to justify a blanket “superior absorption” claim, and the 2025 sleep trial also delivered about 1.523 g/day glycine, creating a direct attribution problem. 11

The analytical-methods treatment likewise separates assay technology from biological validity. Serum/plasma total magnesium is the routine clinical measurement, whereas RBC magnesium, ionized magnesium, urinary magnesium, and loading tests probe different compartments or physiological processes. NIH ODS specifically warns that serum concentrations have little correlation with total-body magnesium or specific tissue concentrations and lists erythrocyte, urinary, ionized, and loading-test approaches as alternatives without endorsing one universal gold standard. 2 AAS and ICP-MS can improve elemental quantitation but cannot, by themselves, make an inappropriate biological compartment a valid measure of whole-body sufficiency.

The mechanistic graph is deliberately restrained. TRPM6 has strong human-genetic causal evidence for epithelial magnesium homeostasis. 3 TRPM7 evidence is predominantly cellular/mechanistic. 4 SLC41A1's Na⁺/Mg²⁺ exchange behavior comes from cellular transport experiments. 5 CNNM2 is unequivocally important for renal magnesium handling because pathogenic variants cause human hypomagnesemia, but the article avoids oversimplifying CNNM2 as a universally settled direct transporter with a single accepted stoichiometry. 6

Safety and regulatory interpretation

The article distinguishes normal intake targets from upper limits and from trial exposures. NIH's current U.S. values are 310–420 mg/day for most adults depending on sex/age, 350–400 mg/day during pregnancy depending on age, and 310–360 mg/day during lactation; the 350 mg/day adult UL applies only to supplemental/medicinal magnesium, not magnesium naturally present in food. 2 EFSA's adult Adequate Intakes are 350 mg/day for men and 300 mg/day for women, illustrating that dietary-reference systems are jurisdiction-specific rather than interchangeable dosing instructions. 16

Renal function is the key toxicity modifier. Healthy kidneys can eliminate excess absorbed magnesium, whereas renal impairment reduces that safety margin. NIH ODS reports gastrointestinal adverse effects at high supplemental doses and potentially life-threatening cardiovascular, respiratory, and neuromuscular manifestations with severe hypermagnesemia. 2 This is particularly relevant in geriatrics because older adults can simultaneously have greater deficiency risk from lower intake/absorption and greater excess risk if renal function has declined. 2

Regulatory wording was kept conservative. FDA's 2022 action was a letter of enforcement discretion for qualified claims, not approval of magnesium as an antihypertensive drug; FDA expressly requires uncertainty language. 9 In the EU, magnesium is covered by the food-supplement framework under Directive 2002/46/EC, and a 2025 regulation specifically amended Annex II to include magnesium L-threonate as a permitted magnesium source. 17

Methods and limitations

The evidence search used no publication-date cutoff and prioritized original human trials and mechanistic papers, PubMed/PMC, NIH ODS, FDA, EFSA, WHO, EUR-Lex, and trial registries. Systematic reviews were used primarily to contextualize heterogeneity and identify null or inconsistent evidence rather than to replace original studies. ClinicalTrials.gov screening identified, among other records, an active-not-recruiting magnesium-L-threonate sleep/recovery trial in collegiate athletes and a completed magnesium-citrate sleep/cognition study in Saudi adults whose inspected registry record had no posted efficacy results; those registry records are treated as evidence of research activity, not evidence of efficacy. 18 19

Known evidence limitations are made explicit in the source: heterogeneous magnesium status at baseline, inconsistent elemental-dose reporting, chemically different salts, variable co-ingredients, substantial placebo responses in sleep research, short study durations, numerous secondary endpoints in some trials, wearable-derived rather than polysomnographic sleep staging, and commercial sponsorship in several magnesium-L-threonate studies. For older mechanistic and clinical papers where a complete funding or conflict statement was not inspected, the schema records not-assessed rather than inferring absence of conflicts.

One unresolved methodological area is the degree to which RBC and ionized-magnesium assays improve clinical decisions over well-interpreted serum/urinary testing; the article therefore describes their potential relevance without declaring either a universal gold standard. That is consistent with NIH ODS's statement that no single assessment method is satisfactory. 2

## Summary

Magnesium is element 12 (Mg; atomic/molecular weight 24.305 g/mol). In biology it is predominantly relevant as Mg2+, an essential intracellular and extracellular cation that participates in energy metabolism, nucleic-acid and protein synthesis, neuromuscular function, glucose handling, blood-pressure regulation, ion transport, and bone structure. The U.S. NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) states that an adult body contains about 25 g magnesium, roughly 50%–60% in bone, while less than 1% is in serum. Consequently, a normal serum value does not reliably exclude depleted tissue stores.

The strongest interpretation of supplementation evidence is context-dependent. Repletion is biologically and clinically important in true deficiency, and several randomized trials in hypomagnesemic or low-intake populations report improvements in insulin resistance or blood pressure. Across broader populations, pooled blood-pressure effects are small on average, while effects appear larger in hypomagnesemic or hypertensive subgroups. Sleep evidence remains inconsistent: individual trials of magnesium oxide, bisglycinate, and L-threonate report some favorable subjective endpoints, but a 2026 systematic review judged certainty low to very low and did not support routine oral magnesium as an insomnia treatment. Cognitive findings are formulation-specific and are concentrated in small, commercially funded magnesium-L-threonate studies; they should not be generalized to elemental magnesium or other salts.

Magnesium dose statements must distinguish **elemental magnesium** from **salt mass**. For example, 2.5 g magnesium chloride is not 2.5 g elemental magnesium, and hydration state further changes the elemental fraction. U.S. adult RDAs are 400–420 mg/day for men and 310–320 mg/day for women, with life-stage adjustments; the U.S. supplemental/medication UL is 350 mg/day for adults and does not include magnesium naturally present in food. Research trials can and do exceed this UL; those exposures are not recommendations. The major preventable toxicity risk is impaired renal excretion, because hypermagnesemia from supplements, antacids, or laxatives becomes much more likely with significant kidney dysfunction.

## Description

### Identity, physiology, and homeostasis

PubChem identifies elemental magnesium as CID 5462224, formula Mg, molecular weight 24.305 g/mol, and canonical SMILES `[Mg]`. Elemental-metal identity should not be conflated with the hydrated Mg2+ species that participates in physiology or with any particular magnesium salt.

NIH ODS describes magnesium as a cofactor in more than 300 enzyme systems and notes roles in protein synthesis, muscle and nerve function, glucose control, blood-pressure regulation, oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis, DNA/RNA synthesis, glutathione synthesis, and active transmembrane transport of calcium and potassium. Much ATP-dependent biochemistry uses magnesium-bound nucleotide species rather than uncomplexed ATP as the functional substrate.

Human genetics gives unusually strong causal evidence for selected transport proteins. Biallelic TRPM6 mutations cause hereditary hypomagnesemia with secondary hypocalcemia, establishing a critical role for TRPM6 in intestinal and renal magnesium homeostasis. TRPM7 has strong cell-biological evidence as a Mg2+-permeable channel-kinase involved in cellular magnesium balance. SLC41A1 can mediate Na+-dependent Mg2+ extrusion in overexpression experiments. CNNM2 mutations cause dominant renal hypomagnesemia, establishing that CNNM2 is necessary for normal renal magnesium handling, although it is safer to describe CNNM proteins as regulators/components of basolateral Mg handling than to assume a single universally accepted transport stoichiometry.

### Analytical methods and magnesium status

NIH ODS emphasizes the central diagnostic limitation: **"No single method is considered satisfactory."** Serum magnesium is convenient but represents less than 1% of total body magnesium and correlates poorly with total-body or tissue magnesium. Analytical platform and biological matrix answer different questions.

| Assay / matrix | What it measures | Clinical/research relevance | Main limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serum/plasma total Mg, usually automated colorimetric/photometric chemistry | Total circulating Mg in the sampled fluid | Routine first-line test; detects overt hypo- or hypermagnesemia; useful with kidney function and clinical context | Strong homeostatic control; may remain normal despite depleted intracellular/bone stores |
| Atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS) | Total elemental Mg after atomization | Historically used for serum, urine, RBC, and absorption/balance research; analytically specific for total element | Measures total Mg, not ionized fraction or tissue sufficiency; instrument/lab methodology matters |
| ICP-MS | Total elemental Mg after ionization in plasma | High-sensitivity multi-element research/reference quantitation; useful for carefully controlled elemental analysis | Expense, calibration/matrix effects, and the same biological-compartment problem as other total-Mg assays |
| RBC/erythrocyte Mg | Mg in erythrocytes, often measured after cell preparation/lysis | Intracellular-adjacent biomarker used in research and some clinical settings | RBCs are only one cell compartment; reference intervals, preanalytics, and interlaboratory harmonization are less mature than routine serum testing |

## Evidence note

**Methods.** Literature and disclosure searches were conducted without a publication-date restriction through 2026-10-04. Sources prioritized PubMed/PMC primary human studies, original mechanistic/genetic papers, ClinicalTrials.gov or recognized trial registrations when available, NIH ODS, FDA, EFSA, WHO, EUR-Lex, and authoritative nutrition references cited by those agencies. Systematic reviews/meta-analyses were used to contextualize heterogeneous trial evidence and identify negative/null results. Included evidence comprised primary randomized or controlled human studies, mechanistically informative human genetics/cell studies, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, and official guidance/regulatory documents. Excluded evidence included vendor pages, unsupported commercial claims, non-magnesium multi-ingredient interventions unless necessary to identify a formulation-specific confounder, and animal findings used as proof of human clinical efficacy.

The evidence base is heterogeneous by baseline magnesium status, salt, elemental dose, co-ingredients, population, outcome instrument, and duration. This matters clinically: a repletion effect in hypomagnesemia is not evidence of enhancement in magnesium-replete people, and an effect of a patented magnesium-L-threonate product cannot automatically be generalized to oxide, citrate, chloride, bisglycinate, or dietary magnesium.

Key source language illustrates the uncertainty. NIH ODS states, **"No single method is considered satisfactory"** for magnesium-status assessment. FDA's permitted qualified hypertension claim explicitly says the evidence is **"inconsistent and inconclusive."** The 2026 systematic review of adult sleep RCTs concluded, **"Current evidence does not support oral magnesium as a routine treatment for insomnia."** In contrast, the hypomagnesemic insulin-resistance trial concluded, **"Oral magnesium supplementation improves insulin sensitivity in hypomagnesemic non-diabetic subjects."** These statements address different populations and should not be collapsed into one universal efficacy claim.

The ClinicalTrials.gov search also identified magnesium sleep/cognition studies whose registry records had no posted efficacy results as of the research date, including NCT07015047 (active, magnesium L-threonate in collegiate athletes) and NCT07515417 (completed magnesium-citrate study in Saudi adults). Registry presence is not evidence of benefit.

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- title: Magnesium-nucleotide coupling in energy-dependent biochemistry
  description: Human physiology and biochemical literature identify Mg2+ as a required cofactor for many enzymes and for energy-production pathways. ATP-dependent reactions commonly involve magnesium-complexed nucleotide species. This biochemical requirement establishes essentiality but does not by itself prove that extra magnesium improves performance in magnesium-replete people.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
  conceptId: mg-atp-complex
- title: TRPM6-dependent epithelial magnesium homeostasis
  description: Human positional-genetic studies found biallelic TRPM6 mutations in hereditary hypomagnesemia with secondary hypocalcemia. TRPM6 is expressed in intestinal epithelia and kidney tubules, making the human genetic evidence directly relevant to transepithelial Mg2+ homeostasis.
  sourceId: schlingmann-2002
  conceptId: trpm6
- title: TRPM7 and cellular magnesium balance
  description: In vertebrate cell experiments, TRPM7 deletion caused cellular magnesium deficiency and growth arrest, and extracellular magnesium rescued viability. This supports TRPM7 as a cellular Mg2+-permeable homeostatic pathway, but it is cell-mechanistic evidence rather than a magnesium-supplement efficacy trial.
  sourceId: schmitz-2003
  conceptId: trpm7
- title: SLC41A1-mediated sodium-dependent magnesium extrusion
  description: In HEK293 overexpression experiments, SLC41A1 increased Mg2+ efflux in a strongly extracellular-Na+-dependent manner, supporting a Na+/Mg2+ exchange function. The result is a cellular transport experiment and should not be converted into a clinical supplementation claim.
  sourceId: kolisek-2012
  conceptId: slc41a1
- title: CNNM2 in renal basolateral magnesium handling
  description: Human CNNM2 mutations were identified in families with dominant hypomagnesemia, and CNNM2 localized to basolateral membranes of distal renal tubular segments. These data establish a critical role in epithelial Mg2+ handling while leaving aspects of the exact molecular transport mechanism open.
  sourceId: stuiver-2011
  conceptId: cnnm2
- title: Renal conservation and toxicity control
  description: Kidneys are the principal homeostatic regulator of circulating magnesium: urinary excretion falls when status is low, while impaired renal function reduces the capacity to eliminate an excess. This same physiology explains both resilience against short-term low intake and the disproportionate hypermagnesemia risk in renal failure.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
  conceptId: magnesium-homeostasis
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- title: Renal impairment and hypermagnesemia
  description: The risk of magnesium toxicity rises substantially when kidney function is impaired because renal elimination is reduced. Very high magnesium exposure can cause hypotension, vomiting, respiratory depression, arrhythmia, and cardiac arrest; magnesium-containing laxatives and antacids are important non-supplement sources.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Gastrointestinal dose limitation
  description: Supplemental magnesium commonly causes diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramping through unabsorbed osmotic magnesium salts. Tolerability varies by dose and formulation and is not equivalent to systemic toxicity.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Supplemental UL is not the RDA
  description: The U.S. adult UL is 350 mg/day from supplements and medications only; it excludes magnesium naturally present in food. Adult RDAs of 310–420 mg/day refer to total intake. Trial doses above 350 mg/day are research exposures and should not be presented as self-treatment recommendations.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Serum magnesium can miss depletion
  description: Less than 1% of total body magnesium is in serum, and serum magnesium correlates poorly with total-body or tissue stores. A normal serum result therefore does not prove adequate tissue status; interpretation should integrate clinical context and, when indicated, renal and urinary data.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Pregnancy and lactation
  description: U.S. magnesium intake targets change with age and pregnancy/lactation, while the supplemental UL remains 350 mg/day for ages 14 years and older. Injectable magnesium sulfate for prevention or treatment of eclamptic seizures is a separate medical therapy requiring clinical protocols and must not be conflated with oral dietary supplementation.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Pediatric considerations
  description: U.S. supplemental magnesium ULs are age-specific: none is established for infants, 65 mg/day for ages 1–3, 110 mg/day for ages 4–8, and 350 mg/day from age 9 onward. Pediatric hypermagnesemia has occurred with excessive magnesium-containing products, so adult supplement exposures should not be extrapolated to children.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Geriatric considerations
  description: Older adults tend to have lower dietary intake, reduced intestinal absorption, and greater urinary magnesium loss, increasing deficiency risk. At the same time, declining renal function can increase toxicity risk from supplements and magnesium-containing medications, so deficiency and excess risks can coexist.
  sourceId: nih-ods-2026
- title: Formulation and co-ingredient confounding
  description: Clinical outcomes cannot be assigned to elemental magnesium alone when the tested product has a distinctive ligand, co-ingredient, or proprietary formulation. Magnesium bisglycinate delivers glycine, and magnesium-L-threonate trials test a specific salt and often a branded product.
  sourceId: schuster-2025
- title: Commercial sponsorship in magnesium-L-threonate evidence
  description: Several prominent magnesium-L-threonate cognition/sleep trials were funded by companies with commercial interests in the tested ingredient, with author employment, consulting, or sponsor involvement disclosed in the publication or correction. These trials are not invalidated by sponsorship, but independent replication is limited.
  sourceId: lopresti-smith-2026
```
