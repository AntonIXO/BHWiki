### Abstract

> Δ<sup>9</sup>-Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the principal psychoactive cannabinoid in cannabis. Following consumption, it is metabolized in the liver to 11-hydroxy-Δ<sup>9</sup>-tetrahydrocannabinol, and subsequently to 11-nor-9-carboxy-Δ<sup>9</sup>-tetrahydrocannabinol, which are excreted in urine. The presence of THC and its metabolites in urine can serve as indicators of recent or past cannabis use. Here, a routine method for the simultaneous quantification of these three compounds is presented, using tandem enzymatic-alkaline hydrolysis, solid-phase extraction, and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). The procedure is optimized for operational efficiency and analytical sensitivity and is routinely applied to urine samples submitted for cannabinoid testing in clinical and forensic toxicology.
