### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p>Digital logistics can improve visibility, coordination, reliability, and customer interaction, yet technological sophistication alone does not guarantee that firms realize these benefits. This study identifies conditions that are necessary and alternative configurations that are sufficient for high perceived e-logistics effectiveness in Bangladesh’s ready-made garment (RMG) sector. A cross-sectional survey used random respondent selection, 70 professionals were invited and 60 usable questionnaires were returned (85.7%). Necessary Condition Analysis (NCA) and fuzzy set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) were combined. The study specific four-item benefit composite shows strong empirical coherence (Cronbach’s α = 0.936; McDonald’s ω = 0.937). CE-FDH NCA with 10,000 permutations identifies top management awareness as the only condition with both a practically meaningful and statistically supported necessity effect (d = 0.433, p < 0.001). Using frequency ≥ 2, consistency ≥ 0.80, and standard PRI ≥ 0.65, the observed-data complex fsQCA solution identifies two pathways: MGT*~TECH*~INTL*EXT and MGT*SIZE*INTL*EXT, with solution consistency 0.963 and coverage 0.595. A separate low outcome analysis yields different configurations, supporting causal asymmetry. Robustness checks show that management awareness is the most stable cross specification element, while exact pathway structure is sensitive to some coding and case-exclusion choices. The study contributes a transparent, bounded configurational explanation of e-logistics benefit realization in Bangladesh RMG.</p>
