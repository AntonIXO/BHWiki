### Abstract

> Radiation-induced crosslinking enables the fabrication of protein-based hydrogels and nanogels with tunable physicochemical properties without requiring chemical crosslinkers. Here, we provide protocols for the preparation of protein-based hydrogels that mimic the in vivo extracellular matrix and protein-based nanogels designed for nanomedicine applications, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) contrast agents and drug delivery systems (DDS). The method is based on ionizing radiation of aqueous protein solutions, where hydroxyl radicals generated by water radiolysis react primarily with aromatic amino acid residues (tyrosine, phenylalanine, and histidine), inducing intermolecular crosslinking. The resulting hydrogels and nanogels retain biocompatibility and enzymatic degradability while preserving key cell-adhesive sequences. Bulk hydrogels allow precise control over stiffness and surface topography, whereas nanogels offer size control and support straightforward functionalization. Representative applications include culturing various cell types, such as epithelial, fibroblast, muscle, and stem cell types, on hydrogels with tunable mechanical and topographic cues, and functionalizing nanogels for imaging and therapeutic use. This chapter focuses on gelatin-based hydrogels and nanogels. Supplementary notes direct readers to related protocols for collagen hydrogels and peptide-based nanogels. Collectively, these methods provide a versatile platform for biomaterials and nanomedicine research.
