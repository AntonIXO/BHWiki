### Abstract

> How organisms recover from starvation to nutrient-replete physiology remains poorly understood. In C. elegans, we previously observed that transcription factor hlh-30 or a combination of glucose and linoleic acid in its absence are essential for survival with refeeding following starvation, i.e. recovery competence. A forward genetic screen to evaluate mechanisms identified depd-5, which encodes a GATOR1 subunit, loss of which is expected to impair amino acid sensing with unrestrained raga-1 and mTOR activation. Surprisingly, glucose refeeding reduced recovery competence in depd-5(lf, loss-of-function) animals, which was rescued by amino acids, specifically tryptophan. Increasing tryptophan levels in depd-5(lf) worms through supplementation, reduced tryptophan catabolism with tdo-2 ablation and prolonged starvation restored recovery competence. raga-1(gf, gain-of-function) recapitulated whereas raga-1(lf) rescued the impaired recovery competence in starved depd-5(lf) worms. An analogous DEPDC5 mutation in mammalian cells impaired starvation-induced mTOR inactivation. We conclude that tryptophan counters unrestrained mTOR activity to restore recovery competence following starvation.
