### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p>Problematic social media use has emerged as a significant global public health concern, particularly among university students. This systematic review synthesizes contemporary evidence across diverse geographical contexts to evaluate the relationship between social media addiction and objective sleep quality parameters. Following strict inclusion criteria, ten high-quality empirical studies, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses published between 2023 and 2026—representing populations in Bangladesh, China, India, Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, the United Kingdom, Nigeria, and Brazil—were synthesized. The collective findings across a cumulative sample size of over 40,000 participants demonstrate a robust, negative correlation between excessive digital exposure and sleep duration. Late-night screen time, driven by Fear of Missing Out (FoMo) and digital fatigue, severely alters circadian rhythms and suppresses melatonin secretion, leading to fragmented sleep, daytime sleepiness, and heightened depressive symptoms. While protective behavioral strategies like physical exercise offer a viable mitigation buffer, their efficacy is heavily undermined by extreme digital dependencies. In conclusion, unregulated social media consumption poses an escalating threat to student well-being. Immediate multi-sectoral interventions, digital literacy programs, and campus-based health policies are required to safeguard the physical and psychological health of the higher education workforce.</p>
