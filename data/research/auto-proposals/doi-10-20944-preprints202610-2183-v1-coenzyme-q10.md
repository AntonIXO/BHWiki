### Abstract

> Many widely used nutraceuticals, including curcuminoids, polyphenols, carotenoids, coenzyme Q10 and labile vitamins, reach the systemic circulation poorly after oral intake because of low solubility, gastrointestinal degradation, efflux and presystemic conjugation. Lipid-based nanocarriers have become the leading commercial solution. This narrative review examines liposomes, niosomes, solid lipid nanoparticles, nanostructured lipid carriers, nanoemulsions, micelles and self-emulsifying systems for nutraceutical delivery. It explains the three mechanisms by which lipid excipients raise absorption (mixed-micelle solubilisation, lymphatic transport and modulation of enterocyte handling), compares carrier classes on composition, stability and manufacturability, and examines the conversion of liquid dispersions into stable powders, using spray-dried liposomal vitamin C as a worked example. Human pharmacokinetic studies are tabulated and critically appraised. Reported gains range from about 1.3-fold to more than 180-fold, but this spread reflects study design as much as technology: measurement of total rather than unconjugated analyte, unequal comparator doses, small industry-funded cohorts and few head-to-head comparisons. In the most rigorous equal-dose comparison of eight curcumin formulations, only micellar and cyclodextrin products outperformed native curcumin. The review proposes critical quality attributes for lipid-based ingredients and a minimum reporting standard for future bioavailability trials.
