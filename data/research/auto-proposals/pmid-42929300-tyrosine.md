### Abstract

> Modern dietary patterns are increasingly dominated by highly processed foods rich in advanced glycation end-products (AGEs). Although dietary AGE intake has been epidemiologically linked to pulmonary diseases, its systemic impact on the distal pulmonary microenvironment remains poorly understood. In this study, we investigated the effects of a chronic high-AGE diet on the pulmonary microbiota and metabolites in female C57BL/6 mice. Results showed that high-AGE intake induced significant pulmonary structural damage, evidenced by a reduced lung-to-body weight ratio. Concurrently, it provoked systemic inflammation, marked by an elevated blood eosinophil percentage and a markedly increased neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio. 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing revealed pulmonary taxon-specific alterations in the high-AGE group, characterized by enrichment of potentially pathogenic, strictly anaerobic, and proteolytic Gram-negative bacteria in lung tissue, alongside depletion of beneficial immunomodulatory and short-chain fatty acid-producing commensals in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid. Functional predictions further indicated significant downregulation of amino acid biosynthesis (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan), vitamin B6 metabolism, and ABC transport systems in the high-AGE group. Metabolomics confirmed that pulmonary essential amino acid biosynthesis was affected by high-AGE intake. Collectively, our study provides important evidence that dietary AGEs fundamentally reshape the pulmonary microbial and metabolic ecosystem, underscoring the diet-lung axis as a potential critical target for respiratory health in the context of industrialized modern diets.
