### Abstract

> Marked differences in solubility and stability between hydrophilic bioactive peptides and hydrophobic carotenoids constrain their synergistic oral delivery. Building on the complementary activities of <i>Meretrix meretrix</i>-derived immunomodulatory peptide (MMIP) and astaxanthin (AST), this study developed co-loaded nanoparticles, HLC<sub>10</sub>, featuring a BSA-dextran glycoprotein core and a composite outer layer of hyaluronic acid (HA) and yeast β-glucan. Hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions, and electrostatic forces collectively drive the multicomponent assembly. By tuning particle interfacial hydration and the dispersion state of the payloads, the formulation improved co-encapsulation capacity, processing and storage stability, and gastrointestinal bioaccessibility of MMIP and AST. The composite polysaccharide outer layer further increased particle accumulation in Peyer's patches-associated regions and increased uptake by RAW264.7 macrophages. Oral HLC<sub>10</sub> administration ameliorated cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression, restored immune organ and ileal architecture, and produced coordinated shifts in gut microbiota, fecal short-chain fatty acids, and spleen nucleotide-related metabolic profiles. These findings support the immunomodulatory potential of HLC<sub>10</sub>, but the evidence remains largely limited to endpoint measures in animal models, and direct validation of cellular functions and the underlying mechanisms is still required. Overall, this stratified co-assembly strategy offers a feasible route for the synergistic oral delivery of food-derived active ingredients with markedly different physicochemical properties.
