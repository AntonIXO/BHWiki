### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p>To explore the feasibility of using coal gasification slag (CGS) as a substitute for limestone filler (LF) in asphalt mastic, six representative CGS powders and one commonly used LF were selected. The physicochemical properties of the CGS powders and the rheological properties of the corresponding asphalt mastics were investigated, and an economic evaluation was conducted. Through comparative analysis of the high- and low-temperature rheological properties of the asphalt mastics at a fixed filler-to-binder (F/B) ratio of 1.0 by mass, low-carbon gasification slag generated by the pulverized-coal process (coded as LCPGS) was identified as the preferred raw material, achieving the optimal balance between high- and low-temperature properties. Compared with LF asphalt mastic, LCPGS asphalt mastic (LCPGS-AM) showed a 19.4% higher G*/sinδ at 64 °C, while m/S value at −12 °C was only 2.2% lower. Based on the rheological and fatigue-resistance properties of LCPGS-AMs with different F/B ratios, a suitable F/B range of 30%–40% by volume was determined, which can serve as a reference for asphalt mastic and mixture design. Complete replacement of conventional LF with LCPGS can reduce material cost by approximately 56.3%, yielding significant economic benefits. The preliminary performance and economic analyses indicate that the use of CGS powder as an alternative filler in asphalt mixtures is feasible.</p>
