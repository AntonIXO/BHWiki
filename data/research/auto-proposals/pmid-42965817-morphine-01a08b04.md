### Abstract

> The identification and quantification of drugs of abuse in biological specimens remain a key objective in forensic toxicology. Oral fluid has become a valuable alternative matrix due to its simple, noninvasive collection, minimal risk of adulteration, and good correlation with recent drug use. This chapter presents a validated gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) method for the detection and quantification of multiple drugs of abuse and metabolites in oral fluid samples using the dried saliva spot (DSS) technique. The target analytes include amphetamine (ANF), methamphetamine (MANF), 3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine (MDA), 3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA), cocaine (COC), benzoylecgonine (BE), Δ<sup>9</sup>-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), 11-nor-9-carboxy-Δ<sup>9</sup>-tetrahydr ocannabinol (THCCOOH), morphine (MOP), codeine (COD), 6-monoacetylmorphine (6-MAM), and phencyclidine (PCP). The described GC-MS protocol is a practical and robust approach for routine forensic analysis of oral fluid. DSS offers a convenient and cost-effective sampling and extraction strategy, requiring only small sample volumes and allowing for simplified storage and transport. The analytical protocol provides high sensitivity and specificity for multiple analytes using minimal sample preparation. Its applicability in forensic toxicology makes it a useful tool for routine drug testing.
