### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p>As polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) durability targets continue to increase, rapid and reliable evaluation of stack durability becomes increasingly important. Conventional catalyst accelerated stress test (AST) methods are primarily designed for single-cell evaluation using a potentiostat, limiting their direct application to stacks. In this study, a catalyst AST method applicable to stacks is investigated using load cycling. A three-cell stack is subjected to four durability protocols with different voltage ranges, operating temperatures, and oxidants, and the resulting performance and catalyst degradation are evaluated. Increasing the operating temperature by 10°C accelerates the performance degradation rate by 36%. The use of oxygen as the oxidant with an increased upper potential further accelerates the degradation rate by 85%. When the temperature increases by 10°C, oxygen is used as the oxidant, and both the upper and lower potential limits are extended, the degradation rate increases by more than twofold. These results indicate that Pt oxidation promoted by increased oxidant concentration and upper potential, together with Pt dissolution associated with the applied potential range, plays an important role in accelerating catalyst degradation. The proposed load-based AST provides a practical approach for rapid catalyst durability evaluation at the PEMFC stack level.</p>
