### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p>This study investigates the potential of a development framework for generating personalized light schedules aimed at achieving specific sleep-wake cycle targets. Unlike existing approaches that rely on predefined heuristics or static lighting recommendations, the framework formulates light scheduling as a sequential decision-making problem and employs a reinforcement learning–based deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithm to iteratively modify real-world light exposure patterns toward achieving a desired sleep-wake class. This process is assisted by a convolutional neural network-based sleep-wake classification model trained on ecologically collected data. The circadian impacts of the generated schedules are evaluated using dim light melatonin onset (DLMO) simulations from a validated circadian model. By incorporating melanopic alongside photopic illuminances and embedding industry-oriented constraints, this work explores possibilities of a novel method in generating and modifying light schedules for sleep-wake adjustments using modern approaches, standards and findings.</p>
