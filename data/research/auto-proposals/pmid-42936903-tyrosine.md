### Abstract

> The copper(II)-proline-L-tyrosine and copper(II)-proline-D-tyrosine complex ions, denoted as LL and LD, play an important role in chiral ligand-exchange chromatography (CLEC). These singly charged complexes, containing one neutral and one anionic amino acid ligand, have been isolated in a cryogenic ion trap and characterised by laser spectroscopy in the 3 µm and fingerprint regions. The two diastereomers can be clearly differentiated by their vibrational spectra. Two different isomers for LL and three for LD are deduced from IR-IR hole burning spectra. Quantum chemical calculations show that LL and LD both exist in two square planar structures involving deprotonation of Tyr for one of them or Pro for the other. Although similar in nature, the corresponding LL and LD structures differ by the relative position of the Tyr aromatic ring and the Pro ring, which are on the same side of the N/O⋯Cu⋯N/O plane for LL and on opposite sides for LD. They also differ by the puckering state, <i>endo</i> or <i>exo</i>, of the proline ring. In addition, the LD complex stands out by the presence of a kinetically trapped structure that contains Pro in its zwitterion form, with a very characteristic spectroscopic signature. This finding confirms the role of zwitterionic structures in the chemistry of copper clusters of amino acids.
