### Abstract

> <h4>Background</h4>Dopamine neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA), a midbrain structure, have been widely implicated in promoting emergence from general anesthesia. However, most studies have focused on anesthetics that primarily target γ-aminobutyric acid type A receptors. Whether these neurons also facilitate emergence from mechanistically diverse drug-induced unconscious states is not known. This study examined the specific contribution of VTA dopamine neurons in restoring arousal after sevoflurane-, dexmedetomidine-, fentanyl-, and ketamine-induced unconsciousness.<h4>Methods</h4>Adult Sprague Dawley rats received viral constructs driving selective expression in tyrosine hydroxylase-positive VTA neurons of either channelrhodopsin or the control protein, yellow fluorescent protein. Accurate targeting in VTA TH:ChR was confirmed by assessing righting in response to photostimulation during continuous sevoflurane inhalation (1.4 to 2.0%). In a randomized block design, the rats were exposed to dexmedetomidine (15 µg/kg IV bolus), fentanyl (50 µg/kg IV bolus), or ketamine (50 mg/kg IV infusion). After loss of the righting reflex, behavioral arousal and frontal electroencephalography activity were recorded with or without photostimulation. The proportion of animals righting during the light-on and light-off conditions was assessed in a mixed-effects Cox regression model, and spectral power differences were quantified with effect size estimates.<h4>Results</h4>Optogenetic stimulation of VTA dopamine neurons during continuous sevoflurane inhalation restored righting in VTA TH:ChR rats, with a median latency of 4.7 min (95% CI, 2.6 to 6.4) after the onset of photostimulation. VTA TH:ChR rats were significantly more likely to right during photostimulation after dexmedetomidine (hazard ratio, 27.7; 95% CI, 2.7 to 287.1; P = 0.0053), and responders showed attenuated spectral power in low frequencies (less than 12 Hz) in VTA TH:ChR . Optogenetic stimulation did not affect behavioral nor neurophysiologic measures of arousal after fentanyl. Finally, optogenetic stimulation in VTA TH:ChR rats modestly delayed emergence from ketamine sedation without altering electroencephalography spectral power.<h4>Conclusions</h4>These data suggest that the role of dopamine VTA neurons in anesthetic emergence is agent specific and likely shaped by distinct and sometimes opposing neural pathways.
