### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p> <bold>Background</bold> Fruits and vegetables are essential to human diets yet are among the most perishable agricultural commodities, with postharvest losses reaching 30%–55% and undermining the sustainability of supply chains. Nanotechnology-based treatments, including nano-coatings, nano-packaging and nanocarrier delivery systems, have been widely explored to extend storage life and maintain quality. However, reported effects vary considerably among studies, and how much of the benefit persists when conventional preservation treatments rather than untreated controls serve as the reference remains unquantified, leaving a conspicuous gap in the evidence for this technology. To address this knowledge gap, 118 publications (23 on vegetables and 95 on fruits) comprising 15,119 effect sizes were synthesized to quantify the effects of nanotechnology-based treatments on postharvest quality and to determine how these effects depend on nanomaterial category and storage condition. <bold>Results</bold> Relative to untreated controls, nano-treatment significantly reduced decay rate (−69.5%), weight loss (−42.3%) and microbial counts (−22.8%), increased firmness (+37.3%), vitamin C (+17.6%) and titratable acidity (+15.6%), and lowered respiration, ethylene, hydrogen peroxide and malondialdehyde, whereas electrolyte leakage, water content, soluble solids, sugars and pH did not differ significantly. Relative to conventional treatments the advantage was smaller but remained significant for decay rate (−51.1%), weight loss (−24.6%), firmness (+8.2%), vitamin C (+9.5%) and browning index (−17.6%). The benefit is thus concentrated in delaying decay and water loss rather than in altering basic quality composition. Nanomaterial category exerted a broader influence than storage regime: categories differed significantly for decay rate, weight loss and vitamin C, with metal/metal-oxide materials most consistent, whereas storage condition differed only for weight loss, reduced more under cold storage. We thus recommend positioning nano-treatment as a complement to conventional preservation, prioritizing metal/metal-oxide nanomaterials and cold storage. <bold>Conclusions</bold> These results collectively indicate that nanotechnology-based treatments retain a measurable advantage over conventional preservation treatments in delaying decay and water loss and in maintaining firmness and vitamin C, and remain effective under both cold and room-temperature storage without altering basic quality composition, thus providing a quantitative basis for the targeted application of nanotechnology in postharvest preservation. </p>
