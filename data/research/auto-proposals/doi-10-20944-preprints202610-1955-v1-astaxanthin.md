### Abstract

> The solar energy reaching the Earth's surface is limited, influenced by parameters that create variability and promote inefficient operation of photovoltaic (PV) systems in solar centers. Efforts and strategies are needed to characterize the real available resource with a focus on estimation. To better assess the variability of solar energy at the Earth's surface, an emerging metric for predicting the clear sky index (\( K_{t}^{\ast } \)) and its increments (\( ∆K_{t}^{\ast } \)) is used. For this purpose, the Intermediate Sky Intermittency Index (ISII) model is used, which estimates solar energy based on several parameters that increase the efficiency of the estimate. A solar energy sample from multiple sites, comprising thirteen collection locations, was used. The results show a variation in the \( K_{t}^{\ast } \) for intermediate days on the order of 0.4 to 0.7, and its increments varying in the range of -1 to 1; The proposed model is always maximized and corrects the estimate by selecting values always within the margin of eliminating fluctuations on intermediate day. It concludes that there is a mass reduction of increments, mainly those &gt; 0, which are corrected for stabilization of PV production, even on intermediate days with emerging characteristics of clear and cloudy skies. With the rectification of fluctuations to reduce attenuation, it proves viable for modeling rectified output power in k-central units, maximizing their lifetime.
