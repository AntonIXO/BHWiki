### Abstract

> Persimmon fruit accumulates high levels of proanthocyanidins (PAs), causing extreme astringency. In pollination-constant and non-astringent (PCNA) cultivars, PA accumulation ceases early in fruit development. Although the PCNA trait is recessively controlled by the ASTRINGENCY ( AST ) locus, its causal gene has remained unknown. Using haplotype-resolved forward genetics in a hexaploid genetic background, we identified the previously uncharacterized TANNIN ( TAN ) as the causal gene. Long-read sequencing revealed that PCNA originated through the accumulation of independently derived ast alleles that cause transcriptional deficiency of TAN . These findings resolve the complex genetic basis of natural astringency loss in persimmon and provide a framework for molecular breeding. Further investigation of TAN function would uncover mechanisms underlying massive accumulation of PAs in persimmon fruit and insight into specialized metabolite sequestration.
