### Abstract

> Fusarium sambucinum is a soil-borne pathogen that causes dry rot in potato, leading to significant losses in yield and post-harvest losses across the world. In the present study, a potent bacterium, Bacillus subtilis MGM123, was isolated from the rhizospheric soil of Aloe vera and characterised by morphological, biochemical and molecular Sanger sequencing analysis The isolate showed antagonistic activity with 86.67% inhibition of the mycelial growth. The fungal isolate proved to be pathogenic on potato tubers, and molecular study done with ITS sequencing confirmed the fungal pathogen as Fusarium sambucinum csnmgmu7. B. subtilis MGM123 was cocultured with the fungal pathogen to increase the production of antifungal metabolites. The antifungal compounds were extracted by Diaion HP-20 and then purified by Sephadex LH-20 column chromatography. The spot with an Rf value of 0.86 was found using TLC and was identified as surfactin. The different peaks that were observed in the HPLC analysis revealed the presence of purified bioactive compounds. The structures of the homologues of surfactin were further confirmed by LC–MS/MS analysis by the characteristic molecular ions [M + H]⁺. Furthermore, the PCR confirmed the presence of the srfA biosynthetic gene. B. subtilis MGM123 and lipopeptide treatments increased the activities of defence-related enzymes (PAL, POD, PPO), chlorophyll content and tuber yield in potato. The results indicate that B. subtilis MGM123 and its surfactin-rich lipopeptides have the potential to be used as an effective biological control measure against F. sambucinum in potato production.
