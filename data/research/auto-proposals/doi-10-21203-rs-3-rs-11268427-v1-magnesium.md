### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p> <bold>Background:</bold> Potassium (K) is essential for stevia production, but increasing K beyond sufficiency may not simultaneously improve root growth, harvestable biomass, magnesium (Mg) nutrition, and steviol glycoside production. This study examined whether these traits share a common response across a broad K gradient in <italic>Stevia rebaudiana</italic> . <bold>Results:</bold> Plants of ‘Tiannong 5’ were grown at five nutrient-solution K concentrations of 1, 4, 8, 16, and 24 mmol L <sup>-1</sup> . Root architecture, biomass, leaf and root K–Mg status, and 13 steviol glycosides were quantified, and treatment comparisons were integrated with restricted dose–response models and bootstrap-derived response ranges. Intermediate K supported root expansion and whole-plant biomass. Root length and whole-plant dry weight had model-estimated peaks at 4.69 and 5.38 mmol L <sup>-1</sup> K, with 90% high-response ranges of 2.88–7.65 and 3.17–9.16 mmol L <sup>-1</sup> , respectively. Leaf dry weight differed among treatments but showed no supported continuous optimum. Leaf Mg concentration declined from 1.769 to 0.896 mg g <sup>-1</sup> as K increased, accompanied by lower leaf Mg content and a higher leaf K/Mg molar ratio. Neither total steviol glycoside concentration nor content per plant showed a supported K breakpoint. Their observed means at K24 were 9.05% and 20.54% lower than those at K16, respectively. In contrast, the rebaudioside A/stevioside ratio increased with K supply, indicating divergent responses of glycoside composition and total productivity. The candidate K16–K24 interval corresponded to fitted leaf Mg concentrations of approximately 0.9–1.1 mg g <sup>-1</sup> , but this treatment-level association does not represent a diagnostic Mg threshold. <bold>Conclusions:</bold> Root expansion, biomass accumulation, Mg status, glycoside productivity, and glycoside composition showed distinct K-response patterns. Once K supply exceeded the intermediate range supporting root and whole-plant growth, further K provided diminishing production returns, intensified leaf K–Mg imbalance, and failed to sustain total steviol glycoside productivity. These findings provide a rationale for independently testing coordinated K–Mg nutrition as a strategy to sustain stevia leaf and glycoside production. </p>
