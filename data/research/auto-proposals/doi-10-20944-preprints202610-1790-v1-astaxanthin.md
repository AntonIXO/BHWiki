### Abstract

> Isolate-based antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) is highly reproducible across workflows, laboratories, and institutions. While the basis of the test is remarkably consistent, multiple constraints may obscure clinically relevant diversity and resistant subpopulations. Exponentially increasing rates of antimicrobial resistance have created a desire for faster AST methods to decrease empiric antimicrobial treatment in the hope of reducing both treatment failure and further development of resistance. This review evaluates the limitations of conventional isolate-based AST including colony selection bias, population diversity loss, and heteroresistance and assesses several new rapid phenotypic AST methods through the lens of those limitations and biases.
