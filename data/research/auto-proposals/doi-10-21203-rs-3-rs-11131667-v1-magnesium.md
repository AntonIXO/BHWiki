### Abstract

> <title>Abstract</title> <p> Water contamination is a crucial ecological concern impacting aquatic ecosystems and human health, particularly because of heavy metals entering water bodies from the industrial, agricultural, and mining activities. To address this, magnesium-doped cobalt ferrite nanoparticles (MgCoFe <sub>2</sub> O <sub>4</sub> @SiO <sub>2</sub> -CPTMS-Cur) were expanded for effective heavy metal removal from wastewater. These nanoparticles were made initially through coprecipitation technique and then were coated with silica and modified with 3-chloropropyltrimethoxysilane (CPTMS) and curcumin (Cur) to improve the adsorption capabilities. The as-synthesized product was fully characterised by FTIR, EDX, XRD, TEM SEM, BET, TGA and VSM, showing a crystalline structure and a porous surface with a BET surface area of 24.176 m <sup>2</sup> /g. The adsorption experiments showed an effective removal of Lead Pb(II) and Cadmium Cd(II), with a maximum abilities of 259.50 mg/g for Pb(II) and 208.35 mg/g for Cd(II). Kinetic testing revealed that the adsorption followed a second-order kinetic model, despite the Langmuir isotherm model accurately representing the equilibrium data. The adsorption process was endothermic, spontaneous, and beneficial, according to thermodynamic study. Research on regeneration demonstrated the durability and reusability of the composite over four cycles, highlighting its promise as a frugal and operative adsorbent for heavy metal extraction in environmental cleanup. The effective removal of Pb(II) and Cd(II) metal ions from tap, groundwater, and lake water was achieved utilising this innovative adsorbent. </p>
