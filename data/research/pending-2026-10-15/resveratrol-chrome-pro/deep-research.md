## Summary

Resveratrol has formulation- and population-specific human signals, not a demonstrated general anti-aging effect. A small cognitive benefit in postmenopausal women contrasts with null verbal-memory results in another older-adult trial; altered Alzheimer biomarkers do not establish dementia treatment or prevention. (sourceId: reshaw-2021; huhn-2018; turner-2015)

Metabolic results range from improved glycemic measurements in a short diabetes trial to no improvement in a larger six-month trial. Improved vessel-dilation measurements are not evidence of fewer cardiovascular events. Resveratrol also attenuated some exercise-training adaptations in older men. (sourceId: movahed-2013; bo-2016; wong-2013; gliemann-2013)

Pterostilbene is a different compound: its own small trial found increased LDL cholesterol alongside lower blood pressure. Neither its findings nor those from resveratrol mixtures or enhanced-delivery products establish effects of ordinary trans-resveratrol. (sourceId: riche-2014; wightman-2014; howells-2011)

## Description

Trans-resveratrol is 3,5,4′-trihydroxy-trans-stilbene, a polyphenolic plant constituent. Pterostilbene is its 3,5-dimethoxy analogue, not another name, salt, or interchangeable dose of resveratrol. Widely repeated comparative bioavailability figures for these compounds originate from rat experiments. (sourceId: kapetanovic-2011)

This record distinguishes purified trans-resveratrol, incompletely characterized study products, resveratrol–quercetin and resveratrol–piperine combinations, micronized SRT501, isolated pterostilbene, and pterostilbene-containing combinations. Milligrams of an extract or mixture are not automatically milligrams of trans-resveratrol. (sourceId: witte-2014; wightman-2014; howells-2011; riche-2014; jensen-2022; efsa-2016)

## Evidence note

This is a targeted primary-evidence synthesis, not a pooled estimate. Neuropsychological tests, walking performance, glucose-control markers, vascular physiology, imaging, and cellular measurements remain separate endpoints. Small samples, multiple outcomes, short exposure, baseline imbalances, and incomplete formulation reporting limit generalization. A within-group improvement is not necessarily a treatment effect versus placebo. (sourceId: witte-2014; movahed-2013; restore-2017; turner-2015)

Commercial support is recorded separately from authors’ conflict declarations. RESHAW received Evolva support; the pterostilbene metabolic trial received ChromaDex funding; the muscle-recovery combination study used Elysium-supplied pills and disclosed an employee with stock options. These relationships affect interpretation, not automatic acceptance or rejection. “Not verified” below means disclosure information was not established from the retrieved material, not that conflicts were absent. (sourceId: reshaw-2021; riche-2014; jensen-2022)

## Pharmacokinetics

```yaml
- parameter: "Absorption versus circulating parent"
  finding: "In six volunteers, at least 70% of a 25-mg radiolabeled oral dose was absorbed, but unchanged plasma resveratrol remained below 5 ng/mL. Extensive sulfate and glucuronide conjugation explains why absorption is not equivalent to parent bioavailability. Much of the administered material was recovered in urine."
  sourceId: walle-2004
- parameter: "Concentration and peak timing"
  finding: "After a single 5-g dose of nonmicronized resveratrol, mean parent Cmax was 539 ng/mL, approximately 2.4 micromolar, at 1.5 hours. Major conjugates had substantially greater exposure; metabolite AUC reached up to 23 times parent AUC."
  limitation: "Ten participants at this dose; substantial variability; not applicable to every formulation or lower dose."
  sourceId: boocock-2007
- parameter: "Study-specific parent half-life"
  finding: "A 40-person randomized study reported unchanged trans-resveratrol half-lives of 1–3 hours after single doses and 2–5 hours after repeated doses. Active groups received 25, 50, 100, or 150 mg six times daily for 13 doses."
  limitation: "Short pharmacokinetic experiment with substantial variability; not a universal half-life or established dosing schedule."
  sourceId: almeida-2009
- parameter: "Radiolabel half-life boundary"
  finding: "The frequently quoted 9.2-hour value from the radiolabel study describes circulating resveratrol-derived material, not a clean unchanged-parent elimination estimate or duration of clinical benefit."
  sourceId: walle-2004
- parameter: "Micronized SRT501"
  finding: "At 5 g/day for up to 14 days before surgery for hepatic metastases, mean peak plasma resveratrol was about 1,942 ng/mL. The reported 3.6-fold advantage used historical nonmicronized data, not a randomized head-to-head pharmacokinetic comparison."
  limitation: "Formulation-specific exposure; neither therapeutic superiority nor equivalence to other micronized, liposomal, or nanoparticle products follows."
  sourceId: howells-2011
- parameter: "Pterostilbene comparison"
  finding: "Approximately 80% oral bioavailability for pterostilbene versus 20% for resveratrol was measured in rats. These are not human absolute-bioavailability estimates or evidence of superior human outcomes."
  sourceId: kapetanovic-2011
```

## Modifiers

```yaml
- modifier: "Piperine coadministration"
  finding: "In a 23-person acute crossover, 250 mg trans-resveratrol plus 20 mg piperine increased cerebral blood-flow measures more than resveratrol alone or placebo, without improving cognition, mood, or blood pressure. A separate six-person pharmacokinetic cohort showed no significant exposure increase."
  interpretation: "Human absorption enhancement was not demonstrated by this small experiment."
  sourceId: wightman-2014
- modifier: "Quercetin and attribution"
  finding: "The positive 26-week Witte memory experiment administered both resveratrol and quercetin. It cannot isolate the contribution of either ingredient or establish enhanced absorption."
  sourceId: witte-2014
- modifier: "Dose and baseline metabolic state"
  finding: "Positive findings at 150 mg/day in obese men and null findings at 1.5 g/day came from different trials. They do not establish an optimal dose, an inverted-U response, or that increasing exposure improves efficacy."
  sourceId: [timmers-2011, poulsen-2013]
```

## Effects

```yaml
- endpoint: "Brachial flow-mediated dilation"
  evidenceType: "Human randomized physiological endpoint"
  finding: "Twenty-eight obese adults received resVida trans-resveratrol 75 mg/day or placebo for six weeks each. Flow-mediated dilation increased by 23% relatively, not 23 percentage points; P=0.021. Blood pressure, arterial compliance, and Stroop performance were unchanged."
  sourceId: wong-2013
- endpoint: "Forearm mitochondrial capacity"
  evidenceType: "Human randomized surrogate endpoint; combination product"
  finding: "Sixteen young adults received 500 mg resveratrol plus 10 mg piperine daily or placebo during four weeks of forearm training. Near-infrared spectroscopy estimated approximately 40% versus 10% improvement in the trained arm; P=0.02. Untrained arms did not improve."
  limitation: "Not isolated-resveratrol evidence or a demonstration of whole-body endurance improvement."
  sourceId: polley-2016
- endpoint: "IGF-axis measurements"
  evidenceType: "Uncontrolled human dose study"
  finding: "Daily resveratrol at 0.5–5 g for 29 days altered circulating IGF-I and IGFBP-3 compared with pretreatment values. Biomarker changes without placebo control do not establish cancer prevention or slower aging."
  sourceId: brown-2010
```

## Outcomes

```yaml
- outcome: "Postmenopausal cognition"
  formulation: "Trans-resveratrol, 75 mg twice daily"
  result: "The 24-month RESHAW crossover analysis included 125 women, with 12 months on each treatment. Overall cognitive performance favored resveratrol, Cohen's d=0.170, P=0.005. Cerebrovascular and insulin-related secondary measures also changed."
  interpretation: "Small test-score effect; not evidence of reduced dementia incidence. Earlier RESHAW reports are not independent replications."
  sourceId: reshaw-2021
- outcome: "Older-adult verbal memory: positive pilot"
  formulation: "200 mg resveratrol plus 320 mg quercetin daily"
  result: "Among 46 analyzed participants after 26 weeks, word retention over 30 minutes favored the combination; P=0.038. Hippocampal connectivity and some metabolic measures also changed."
  limitation: "Small matched-completer analysis with multiple outcomes; cannot attribute the result to resveratrol alone."
  sourceId: witte-2014
- outcome: "Older-adult verbal memory: null trial"
  formulation: "Study product delivering 200 mg/day resveratrol; complete coformulation not verified from retrieved primary material"
  result: "Sixty adults aged 60–79 were randomized for 26 weeks. The primary California Verbal Learning Task outcome did not improve versus placebo. Pattern-recognition results were nonsignificant, P=0.07."
  sourceId: huhn-2018
- outcome: "Alzheimer disease"
  formulation: "Escalating resveratrol to 2 g/day"
  result: "In 119 participants over 52 weeks, no consistent benefit emerged across cognitive/global measures. A secondary activities-of-daily-living measure favored treatment, but the study primarily assessed safety and biomarkers and was not powered to establish clinical efficacy."
  caveat: "Amyloid-marker trajectories changed and brain-volume loss was greater with resveratrol; the latter finding has uncertain significance. Neither establishes neuroprotection."
  sourceId: turner-2015
- outcome: "Metabolic profile in obesity"
  formulation: "resVida trans-resveratrol, 150 mg/day"
  result: "An 11-man, 30-day crossover reported improvements in insulin-resistance estimates, liver fat, and several metabolic measurements."
  limitation: "Small mechanistic study, not prevention of diabetes or cardiovascular events."
  sourceId: timmers-2011
- outcome: "Insulin sensitivity in obesity"
  formulation: "Fluxome trans-resveratrol, 1.5 g/day"
  result: "A 24-man four-week randomized trial found no improvement in the primary clamp-measured insulin-sensitivity endpoint, blood pressure, ectopic fat, energy expenditure, or the broader metabolic profile."
  sourceId: poulsen-2013
- outcome: "Type 2 diabetes: positive short trial"
  formulation: "Biotivia resveratrol, 1 g/day alongside existing medication"
  result: "In the 45-day trial, changes in HbA1c, fasting glucose, insulin resistance, and systolic pressure favored resveratrol versus placebo. Mean HbA1c change was -1.20 percentage points versus +0.01. Weight did not improve."
  limitation: "Small study, baseline imbalances in fasting glucose and insulin resistance, short follow-up, and multiple outcomes. No diabetic-complication endpoint."
  sourceId: movahed-2013
- outcome: "Type 2 diabetes: larger null trial"
  formulation: "Resveratrol, 40 or 500 mg/day"
  result: "Among 192 randomized participants over six months, neither dose significantly improved the primary CRP endpoint, HbA1c, fasting glucose, insulin, weight, or blood pressure versus placebo. Total cholesterol and triglycerides increased slightly in the 500-mg arm."
  sourceId: bo-2016
- outcome: "Exercise-training adaptation"
  formulation: "Trans-resveratrol, 250 mg/day"
  result: "Twenty-seven inactive older men completed eight weeks of training with resveratrol or placebo. The increase in maximal oxygen uptake was 45% greater with placebo; some blood-pressure and lipid adaptations were also attenuated with resveratrol."
  limitation: "This describes a difference in training gains, not a 45% fall in oxygen uptake; generalization beyond this regimen is uncertain."
  sourceId: gliemann-2013
- outcome: "Walking with peripheral artery disease"
  formulation: "Resveratrol, 125 or 500 mg/day"
  result: "RESTORE randomized 66 older adults for six months. Six-minute walk changes were +4.6 m, -12.8 m, and -12.3 m for 125 mg, 500 mg, and placebo respectively. The lower-dose comparison had P=0.07 under a prespecified one-sided pilot threshold of 0.10; the higher dose and treadmill outcomes were unconvincing."
  interpretation: "Exploratory low-dose signal, not consistent confirmation of functional benefit."
  sourceId: restore-2017
- outcome: "Resveratrol added to nicotinamide riboside in peripheral artery disease"
  formulation: "ReserveAge 98%-pure trans-resveratrol, 125 mg/day, plus nicotinamide riboside, 1,000 mg/day"
  result: "NICE randomized 90 participants to the combination, nicotinamide riboside alone, or placebo for six months. The combination did not significantly improve the primary six-minute walk outcome versus placebo: difference +3.65 m, P=0.38."
  limitation: "Exploratory trial; lower adherence in the combination arm; no demonstrated added resveratrol benefit."
  sourceId: nice-2024
- outcome: "Pterostilbene lipid and blood-pressure effects"
  formulation: "Pterostilbene alone; distinct from the grape-extract combination arm"
  result: "In the 80-person, 6–8-week trial, pooled pterostilbene monotherapy increased LDL by 17.1 mg/dL versus placebo. The 250-mg/day arm lowered systolic/diastolic pressure by approximately 7.8/7.3 mmHg."
  interpretation: "Mixed cardiovascular risk-marker changes; no demonstrated reduction in cardiovascular events."
  sourceId: riche-2014
- outcome: "Muscle recovery with nicotinamide riboside plus pterostilbene"
  formulation: "1,000 mg nicotinamide riboside plus 200 mg pterostilbene daily"
  result: "Thirty-two adults aged 55–80 were randomized around experimentally induced muscle injury. Approximately six weeks of supplementation did not improve muscle-stem-cell recruitment or the assessed recovery/regeneration outcomes."
  limitation: "Combination evidence; neither ingredient's independent effect was isolated."
  sourceId: jensen-2022
```

## Mechanisms

```yaml
- mechanism: "Energy-sensing pathways"
  evidence: "Human muscle measurements in the small Timmers trial supported changes involving AMPK, SIRT1, PGC-1alpha, and mitochondrial function."
  boundary: "Pathway-associated changes are not proof of direct SIRT1 activation in every tissue, calorie-restriction equivalence, or lifespan extension."
  sourceId: timmers-2011
- mechanism: "Parent versus metabolites"
  evidence: "Human circulation contains substantially more conjugated material than unchanged resveratrol after oral administration."
  boundary: "An experiment exposing cells directly to parent resveratrol cannot automatically represent human tissue exposure or establish equivalent activity of conjugates."
  sourceId: [walle-2004, boocock-2007]
- mechanism: "Tumor apoptosis marker"
  evidence: "Micronized SRT501 increased cleaved caspase-3 in sampled malignant hepatic tissue in a small preoperative study."
  boundary: "A tissue biomarker is not evidence of tumor regression, longer survival, or prevention of cancer."
  sourceId: howells-2011
```

## Cautions

```yaml
- concern: "Gastrointestinal intolerance"
  finding: "Repeated doses of 2.5–5 g/day caused mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal symptoms in healthy volunteers. A small short study cannot exclude uncommon or long-latency harms."
  sourceId: brown-2010
- concern: "Weight loss and neurologic-disease context"
  finding: "Nausea, diarrhea, and weight loss occurred in the Alzheimer trial. Biomarker and MRI changes require interpretation alongside, not instead of, clinical tolerability."
  sourceId: turner-2015
- concern: "Serious renal events with SRT501"
  finding: "In a 24-patient relapsed/refractory myeloma study using micronized SRT501 5 g/day on 20 days of each 21-day cycle, five serious renal-failure events during monotherapy contributed to early termination. Several patients had pre-existing renal abnormalities, progressive disease, and preceding nausea/vomiting."
  boundary: "A serious formulation-, dose-, and disease-context signal; not an estimated renal-failure rate for low-dose supplements in healthy adults."
  sourceId: [popat-2013, srt501-registry]
- concern: "Pregnancy and lactation"
  finding: "In nonhuman primates given a diet containing 0.37% resveratrol throughout pregnancy, supplementation improved some maternal/placental measures but enlarged fetal pancreatic mass by 42% and increased proliferation."
  boundary: "Animal developmental hazard, not demonstrated human teratogenicity. These data and nonpregnant adult trials do not establish pregnancy or breastfeeding safety."
  sourceId: roberts-2014
- concern: "Pterostilbene safety boundaries"
  finding: "The 6–8-week study found no major hepatic, renal, or glucose safety-marker abnormalities at up to 250 mg/day, but its companion report identified increased LDL. These are the same trial population, not independent safety replications."
  boundary: "Long-term, pediatric, pregnancy, and lactation safety cannot be inferred."
  sourceId: [riche-2013, riche-2014]
- concern: "Renal or hepatic impairment"
  finding: "The healthy-volunteer interaction study required normal renal and liver function. Its results do not establish dosing adjustments or safety in organ impairment."
  sourceId: chow-2010
```

## Claims

```yaml
- claim: "Resveratrol extends human lifespan or reliably mimics exercise."
  assessment: "Not established by the cited human trials; metabolic markers are not longevity outcomes, and one training trial showed attenuation rather than enhancement."
  sourceId: [timmers-2011, gliemann-2013]
- claim: "Better cerebral perfusion proves better cognition or prevents dementia."
  assessment: "Unsupported equivalence: acute perfusion changed without cognitive improvement; a small chronic cognitive effect did not measure dementia prevention."
  sourceId: [wightman-2014, reshaw-2021]
- claim: "Pterostilbene is a clinically superior, better-absorbed resveratrol."
  assessment: "Compound substitution is invalid. Rat pharmacokinetics do not establish human superiority, and pterostilbene monotherapy increased LDL in its own trial."
  sourceId: [kapetanovic-2011, riche-2014]
- claim: "Enhanced delivery guarantees greater benefit or safety."
  assessment: "Higher parent exposure with SRT501 did not establish clinical benefit; serious toxicity occurred in a separate myeloma study of that formulation."
  sourceId: [howells-2011, popat-2013]
```

## Interactions

```yaml
- interaction: "Resveratrol and drug-metabolizing enzymes"
  evidenceLevel: "Human probe-drug study; uncontrolled before–after design"
  finding: "After 1 g/day for four weeks, CYP3A4, CYP2D6, and CYP2C9 probe indices suggested inhibition, while CYP1A2 indices suggested induction. Buspirone exposure increased approximately 33%."
  boundary: "Not a quantitative prediction for every substrate, lower dose, acute coadministration, or enhanced formulation."
  sourceId: chow-2010
- interaction: "Anticoagulants, some NSAIDs, oral glucose-lowering drugs, statins, and tamoxifen"
  evidenceLevel: "Mechanistic clinical extrapolation, not demonstrated interaction outcomes for each drug"
  finding: "The enzyme findings create potential for altered exposure to susceptible CYP substrates. Inhibition can also reduce activation of a prodrug, such as conversion of tamoxifen to endoxifen."
  boundary: "The study did not establish bleeding rates, hypoglycemia rates, or tamoxifen treatment failure from resveratrol coadministration."
  sourceId: chow-2010
- interaction: "Pterostilbene and metabolism"
  evidenceLevel: "Human microsomes in vitro"
  finding: "Pterostilbene inhibited CYP2C8 and UGT1A6 in laboratory assays. Clinical relevance remains unestablished; resveratrol's human enzyme profile cannot be substituted."
  sourceId: albassam-2019
- interaction: "Piperine or quercetin combinations"
  evidenceLevel: "Product-specific trials"
  finding: "Coingredients change the intervention being tested. Neither the small piperine pharmacokinetic experiment nor a resveratrol–quercetin cognitive trial establishes universal exposure enhancement or combination safety."
  sourceId: [wightman-2014, witte-2014]
```
