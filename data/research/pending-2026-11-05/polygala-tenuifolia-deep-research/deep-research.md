# Returned Deep Research report (acquisition extract)

The Deep Research run identified three human randomized studies involving BT-11, plus a systematic review/meta-analysis of Polygala tenuifolia with Acorus tatarinowii. It distinguished the named BT-11 dried-root preparation from generic Polygala products and isolated constituents.

Additional findings:

- Shin et al. 2009 (PMID 19699261) randomized 28 BT-11 and 25 placebo-treated older adults. Total CERAD scores and selected word-list recognition, constructional recall/praxis, and modified Boston Naming Test scores improved more with BT-11; the abstract does not establish a significant MMSE between-group benefit. Dose and duration were not established from the primary abstract.
- Park et al. 2019 (PMID 30576772) randomized 61 participants to BT-11 300 mg/day and 60 to placebo for 12 weeks, ages 9–19. Eleven adverse reactions occurred in 10 BT-11 participants and seven in six placebo participants; no statistically significant differences were reported in adverse reactions, laboratory tests, vital signs, weight, or ECG. This is short-term, age-specific safety evidence.
- Zhang et al. 2023/2024 (PMID 38283842; PMCID PMC10815298) synthesized 16 RCTs (1,103 Alzheimer’s disease participants) of Polygala tenuifolia plus Acorus tatarinowii, sometimes adjunctive to conventional drugs. Adjunctive pooled effects favored the combination for MMSE, ADL, and ADAS-cog, but evidence certainty was low/very low, heterogeneity for MMSE was high, and the contribution of Polygala alone could not be separated. PT+AT alone was not significantly superior to conventional drugs on those endpoints.
- Zhao et al. 2020 (PMID 33069838) reviewed Polygalae Radix (P. tenuifolia and P. sibirica), phytochemistry, pharmacology, toxicology, and pharmacokinetics. The review describes gastrointestinal/throat irritation with raw or excessive root material and emphasizes processing; it does not establish a clinically characterized PK profile for the whole root or BT-11.

Existing evidence retained: Lee et al. 2009, PMID 19429065, randomized healthy-adult BT-11 study with oral capsules three times daily for four weeks; K-CVLT immediate recall, recognition, and SOPT errors showed source-specific signals with placebo improvements on several subtests.

Major gaps: independent replication, transparent dose characterization for the cognitive RCTs, adult long-term safety, validated human PK, and documented drug interactions. Animal and in-vitro mechanisms (including acetylcholinesterase inhibition and glucose-utilization findings) were kept separate from human outcomes.
